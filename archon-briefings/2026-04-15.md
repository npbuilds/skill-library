The US naval blockade of Iranian ports is the cycle's most significant regime escalation. The United States is no longer a combatant paused by ceasefire — it is an active naval power running a military blockade against Iranian commercial shipping. This changes the oil supply picture structurally: whereas Iranian mines and a fragile ceasefire could be resolved diplomatically, a US naval blockade requires either Iranian capitulation or US military escalation to resolve. The oil floor is now guaranteed by US military doctrine, not negotiating dynamics.

**Yet the market rallied.** The S&P turned green for the year (+1.78% YTD) on the same day the blockade was announced, because Trump simultaneously stated Iran "wants to talk." The second-order interpretation — maximum pressure creates negotiating leverage, which creates a deal probability — drove risk assets higher. The market is not ignoring the blockade. It is pricing the reflexive logic that coercion accelerates concession.

The 10Y-2Y spread flattening to +0.45 bps (from +0.53) is the more honest signal. A flattening curve under oil inflation pressure typically means the bond market believes demand destruction will dominate supply shock. **Is the bond market correct that a US naval blockade contracts growth faster than it inflates energy prices? If so, the regime quadrant is quietly shifting from pure stagflation toward something more deflationary.** That's not the base case, but the curve flattening bears watching.

**What would prove this view wrong?** Iran responds militarily (curve steepens dramatically, oil spikes, everything reprices) OR Iran immediately accepts blockade terms and a deal is struck (oil falls, curve bull-steepens on anticipated cuts). Both tail scenarios are more likely now than they were last week.
