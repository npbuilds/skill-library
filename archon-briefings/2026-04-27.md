**Lesson 20 (April 27): The Counter-Offer Asymmetry**
When a kinetic actor (IRGC controlling Hormuz) initiates a public diplomatic offer that lowers their previous public ask, the offer represents the *minimum* the actor can concede without internal veto from the kinetic faction. This is informative on Iran's side — but does not move the US-side acceptance probability if the US has a different anchor (Trump's nuclear-first framework). Practical rule: when one side lowers a public ask, model the bid-ask gap separately rather than assuming the offer changes the joint probability of agreement. The dovish optionality is real on the offering side; the rejection optionality is real on the receiving side. The outcome depends on which side's anchor is more durable.
