Subject is the HUMAN skill of directing AI agents; the agent/tooling landscape itself was covered by the 2026-06 agent-landscape round and is out of scope here. ## CORE CLAIM CONFIDENCE

**Likely.** The three legs of the core claim have unequal support. That the sub-skills can be *named* is effectively Confirmed: independent vendor curricula (Anthropic's 4Ds, OpenAI Academy, Microsoft), practitioner canon (Willison, Osmani, Cognition), telemetry from ~400k real sessions, and peer-reviewed observational studies converge on the same five-to-seven-skill shape — verification design, decomposition/spec authoring, context management, delegation calibration, review-under-satisficing, trust calibration [1][5][9][14][17][23]. That the skills can be *rubric-anchored and trained through scored reps* is the weakest leg: no psychometrically validated instrument for individual agent-direction exists as of mid-2026, no longitudinal or intervention study shows trainability, and the entire practice prescription is a principled extrapolation from skill-acquisition science in other domains [19][31][34][39]. The *labor-market multiplier* leg is well evidenced at the level of AI skills generally (56-62% posting premiums, largest outside pure tech, senior-skewed access) but no source isolates a wage premium for "agent orchestration" specifically, and the sharpest "premium is largest on non-technical expertise" framing was contested under adversarial verification [73][74][75][81][84]. Net: the competency model is buildable today on strong evidence; the scoring loop must be built knowing you are bootstrapping measurement the field does not yet have.

## KEY FINDINGS

1. **Verification design — giving agents machine-checkable pass/fail signals and demanding evidence of success — is the most load-bearing human sub-skill, and tooling cannot substitute for it.** [Confirmed] Anthropic's primary guidance leads with "If you can't verify it, don't ship it," Willison and Cognition independently rank it first, and Microsoft user studies found better oversight interfaces sped error-finding and raised confidence without improving verification accuracy [1][2][3][4].

2. **Task decomposition plus written spec authoring is the practitioner-reported upstream determinant of output quality, and CHI 2024 research frames agent direction as a metacognitive skill set, not a technical one.** [Confirmed, with the "bottleneck moved upstream" framing Contested] Osmani, Willison, and Anthropic converge on spec-first workflows ("time spent making the spec precise pays off more than time spent watching the implementation"), while verification-side evidence argues the binding bottleneck sits downstream at review [1][5][6][7].

3. **Context management is framed by Anthropic's primary documentation as THE fundamental constraint — most best practices reduce to curating what enters and stays in the agent's context window.** [Confirmed] Verbatim doc language ("performance degrades as it fills"; two-strikes-then-restart; subagent isolation; CLAUDE.md pruning) was verified word-for-word by two adversarial checkers [1][8].

4. **Once agents run in parallel, human review capacity becomes the throughput limit, and skilled review is calibrated satisficing via four heuristics that are each novice failure points.** [Confirmed] The FAccT 2026 interview study (17 experienced developers) documents plan-as-proxy, tests-as-guarantees, eyeballing-as-signal, and trust-through-unfamiliarity — the last an automation-bias trap — while practitioners cap WIP at ~3-5 agents with explicit kill criteria [5][6][9].

5. **Expert and novice orchestrators are behaviorally distinguishable, and domain/problem understanding — not tool mechanics — drives the gap.** [Confirmed] Anthropic's ~400k-session telemetry (humans keep ~70% of planning, agents take ~80% of execution; expert verified-success 28-33% vs 15%) converges with field studies showing experts plan-before-delegating, spec explicitly, and gate delegation by verifiability and stakes [14][15][23][24][25].

6. **No validated rubric, instrument, or maturity model scores an individual human's observed skill at directing agents — the layer between dyadic AI fluency and organizational maturity models is unoccupied.** [Likely] Anthropic's Fluency Index (24 behaviors, 9,830 conversations) explicitly does not distinguish expertise levels; GLAT and CoRe-3 stop at general AI literacy/co-reasoning; enterprise models grade organizations [19][30][31][32][34].

7. **Strict deliberate practice is structurally unavailable in this young domain; the evidence-backed substitute is purposeful practice inside a deliberately engineered "kind" feedback environment.** [Confirmed as learning science; application to orchestration is extrapolation] Ericsson's definition requires codified pedagogy and expert teachers that do not exist; Kahneman/Klein/Hogarth show wicked environments breed overconfident pseudo-expertise unless feedback is manufactured — pre-registered predictions, immediate scoring, external anchors [39][40][42][43].

8. **The documented failure modes — automation complacency, rubber-stamp review, deskilling, miscalibrated self-perception — are structural and resist willpower; evidenced mitigations are design patterns.** [Confirmed] Radiologists fell from 82% to 45.5% accuracy under wrong AI suggestions; 61.38% of AI-generated PRs at GitHub scale got no documented review; METR's RCT found a ~39-point perception-reality gap; cognitive forcing functions and structured debriefs (~20-25% performance gain) are the fixes that replicate [26][51][66][67][89][95].

9. **The 2025-2026 labor market rewards orchestration capability almost entirely as a multiplier on domain/engineering seniority, not as a standalone credential.** [Confirmed for AI-skill premiums; Likely for the orchestration-specific reading] PwC measured a 56%→~62% AI-skills wage premium; FDE postings grew >800%; but the bare "AI orchestration" label trades mid-market (~$148K) while orchestration-embedded senior roles pay 2-3x, and 71% of the software-hiring rebound was senior-level [71][73][74][81][85].

## DETAILED FINDINGS

### 1. Competency decomposition

Confidence: [Confirmed] because four independent evidence streams — primary vendor documentation, practitioner canon, peer-reviewed HCI research, and large-scale telemetry — converge on the same small set of load-bearing sub-skills, and all four critical claims survived adversarial verification with only interpretive-superlative caveats.

- Verification/eval design ranks first: Anthropic's best-practices doc opens with "Give Claude a way to verify its work" and teaches an escalating ladder — in-prompt pass/fail criteria, /goal conditions, deterministic Stop hooks, adversarial fresh-context reviewer subagents "so the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it" [1]. Willison ranks automated testing first [3]; Devin's Agents 101 tells users to raise test coverage before delegating [4].
- Grunde-McLaughlin et al. (arXiv 2602.16844, Feb 2026; three user studies on a computer-use agent) found redesigned oversight interfaces reduced time-to-find-errors and raised confidence but did not improve final verification accuracy — confidence is a poor self-signal and humans must bring independent evaluation criteria [2]. Verifiers confirmed the abstract verbatim but noted "single most load-bearing skill" is the synthesis's superlative, and "tooling cannot substitute" generalizes one preprint.
- Decomposition + spec authoring: Osmani — "You need different skills now: clear specs, work decomposition, and output verification rather than writing code yourself," with stronger engineers gaining MORE leverage [5]; Anthropic operationalizes explore→plan→implement→commit and the interview-to-SPEC.md pattern [1]; Tankelevitch et al. (CHI 2024) formally establish goal formulation, decomposition, evaluation, and delegation as metacognitive demands [7]. One adversarial voter contested the "bottleneck moved upstream" framing, citing review-side counter-evidence — treat direction-of-bottleneck as contested, both-skills-load-bearing as confirmed.
- Context management as the master constraint: "Most best practices are based on one constraint: Claude's context window fills up fast, and performance degrades as it fills"; /clear between tasks; restart after two failed corrections; subagent delegation of exploration; ruthless CLAUDE.md pruning; named failure patterns "kitchen sink session" and "infinite exploration" — all verified verbatim by two independent checkers [1][8].
- Delegation calibration carries quantified practitioner anchors: 1-6 hour tasks highest ROI, abandon early when the agent ignores instructions [4]; ~3-5 agents per orchestrator with kill criteria [5]; "If you could describe the diff in one sentence, skip the plan" [1]; effort scaled economically — 1 agent for fact-finding, 10+ for complex research, 90.2% quality gain at ~15x token cost (secondary writeups only, not fetched from the primary post) [12]. Willison names "designing agentic loops" — sandboxing, tool curation, scoped credentials, problem selection — "a very new skill" [10]; Ronacher independently stresses harness quality [11].
- Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index finds top AI users behaviorally distinct: 53% pause before work to decide human-vs-AI allocation vs 33% of others [13].
- Anthropic's ~400k-session telemetry: humans make ~70% of planning decisions, agents ~80% of execution decisions; experts trigger 12 agent actions per prompt vs 5, reach verified success 28-33% vs 15%, recover troubled sessions 15% vs 4%, abandon 5-7% vs 19% — success "is determined by how well a person understands the problem they are trying to solve" [14]. Böckeler attributes her interventions (misdiagnosis detection, kill decisions) to "20+ years of programming experience" [15]; Russinovich/Hanselman argue AI amplifies senior engineers while creating "AI drag" on juniors [16].
- Vendor competency models independently converge on a four-part shape: Anthropic's 4Ds (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence) [17][18], OpenAI Academy's five agent-direction skills (context, output definition, boundaries, review, judgment points — primary page 403'd, wording rests on secondary coverage [20][21]), Microsoft's "agent boss" skills (quality control 50%, critical thinking 46%) [13], Salesforce's pre-registered acceptance-criteria eval design [22].

### 2. Mastery markers & rubrics

Confidence: [Likely] because the expert-novice behavioral profile is well triangulated across Tier-1 studies, but the assessment layer is demonstrably unoccupied — no instrument scores an individual's observed agent-direction skill, and two claims were downgraded or contested under adversarial review.

- Anthropic's 4D framework is the most developed individual-level model, operationalized Feb 2026 into 24 observable behaviors coded (binary, 11 of 24) across 9,830 real Jan-2026 conversations: iterative refinement correlated with 2.67 additional fluency behaviors and 5.6x more questioning of AI reasoning; the "artifact paradox" — users become more directive but LESS evaluative (3-5pp, actually up to -5.2pp) when AI generates polished artifacts [17][19]. Verifiers confirmed all numbers but flagged the "most developed" superlative as evaluative and noted the index does not distinguish expertise levels (a fair inference, not Anthropic's explicit statement).
- Expert profile, triangulated: "Professional Software Developers Don't Vibe, They Control" (arXiv 2512.14012; N=13 field + N=99 survey) — experts sketch approaches first, write explicit specs, delegate only sniff-checkable or low-stakes tasks, keep architecture [23]; "Not Everyone Wins with LLMs" (arXiv 2509.21890) — the fault line is decomposition plus per-step validation vs "prompting rabbit holes" [24]; CHI 2023 "Why Johnny Can't Prompt" establishes the novice baseline of opportunistic iteration and overgeneralizing from single examples [25]. One verifier noted the "convergence" is triangulation across heterogeneous populations, not replication — hence Likely, not Confirmed.
- METR's RCT: 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 real ~2h issues in their own mature repos; 19% slower with AI while believing 20% faster (24% forecast) — a ~39-point calibration error [26]. Adversarial verdict: CONTESTED on interpretation. The numbers are Confirmed, but one verifier refuted the claim that METR establishes "calibrated self-assessment" as a measurable expert skill — that is the synthesis's inference, and METR's own 2026-02 update frames the durable takeaway as measurement validity, noting developers are likely more sped up now [27]. Do-not-use as tagged: "METR proved calibration is a trainable expert skill" — the study documents a miscalibration, nothing more.
- Who benefits most is genuinely Contested: autocomplete-era RCTs found gains skewed to less-experienced developers (Peng et al.: 55.8% overall speedup, larger for juniors [28]; Microsoft/Accenture field experiments +8-22% weekly PRs [29]) while METR's agent-era result showed experts in familiar mature repos slowed down [26]. Verification caveat, keep visible tagged do-not-use: the specific "~52% beginners vs ~40% advanced" split traces to the ANZ Bank sub-experiment, not Peng's headline result — do not cite those point estimates as Peng's. The reconciling variable is task type and codebase familiarity; a rubric must score delegation decisions relative to context.
- Performance-based assessment beats self-report: GLAT (20-item performance test, alpha=0.80, N=355, CTT+2PL IRT) predicts actual GenAI task performance better than self-reported proficiency [30]. CoRe-3 (arXiv 2606.05983, June 2026) is the first assessable co-reasoning competency model — Framing, Judging, Steering, scored against deliberately flawed AI outputs — but human-rater and real-world validation are explicitly pending [31].
- The gap layer: Microsoft's Agentic AI adoption maturity model (5 levels x 5 pillars, updated 2026-03-31) and Salesforce's L0-L4 ladder grade organizations [32]; NVIDIA's NCP-AAI certification weights human oversight at ~5% [33]; the npj systematic review of 16 validated AI-literacy scales found none assess agent direction [34]; OECD AILit "Manage AI" and UNESCO's Understand-Apply-Create progression are K-12-targeted structural templates only [35][36].
- Role-level quantified gaps: Anthropic's 2026 Agentic Coding Trends Report — developers use AI in ~60% of work but can fully delegate only 0-20% of tasks (the "delegation gap"), plus an "oversight gap" where agent output outpaces review capacity [37]; Microsoft's agent-boss indicators show leader-employee readiness gaps (agent familiarity 67% vs 40%) [38].

### 3. Deliberate-practice transfer

Confidence: [Confirmed] because the learning-science claims all survived adversarial verification against primary sources — with the standing caveat, flagged by the track itself, that no study examines skill acquisition in agent orchestration and every prescription is extrapolation from music, chess, sports, medicine, and military training.

- Strict deliberate practice cannot be conducted here: Ericsson's definition requires an established field with codified training methods, objective performance measures, and expert teachers [39][40]. His own fallback is purposeful practice — specific stretch goals, self-engineered feedback, discomfort, reflection [41]. Context: deliberate practice explains only ~12-26% of performance variance meta-analytically, near zero among already-elite performers (magnitude genuinely contested between camps; verifiers noted the low end reaches <1% in professions) [40][44].
- Agent orchestration is currently a "wicked" learning environment — stochastic outputs, delayed/ambiguous feedback — so experience alone breeds overconfident pseudo-expertise; trustworthy intuition requires regularity plus rapid unambiguous feedback [42][43]. Prescription: manufacture kindness — pre-registered outcome predictions, immediate scoring, held-out checks, external graders. Verification caveat: the "predictability moderates practice payoff" finding belongs to Macnamara's 2014 cross-domain meta-analysis, not the cited 2016 sports paper [44] — a citation misattribution the checkers caught; the substantive claim stands.
- Cognitive Flexibility Theory prescribes the pedagogy for ill-structured domains: criss-cross the conceptual landscape by revisiting the same cases across rearranged contexts, perspectives, and purposes, assembling situation-specific schemas rather than one precompiled rule set — countering "reductive bias" [45]. Maps to re-analyzing the same orchestration runs through cost, delegation-structure, failure-mode, and spec-design lenses (the mapping is the synthesis's analogy, not sourced).
- Target adaptive, not routine, expertise: conceptual understanding that supports inventing new procedures survives tool churn; routine expertise is brittle when conditions change [46]. Klein's naturalistic-decision-making line adds case-library accumulation and decision-point comparison as the acceleration method [106] — though ShadowBox-style training presupposes expert decision libraries that do not yet exist for orchestration.
- Unaided self-assessment correlates only r≈0.26-0.33 with objective performance, worst for weak performers and judgment-heavy tasks, even in trained professionals [47][48]. Kept visible, tagged do-not-use: the classic Dunning-Kruger *chart* is substantially a statistical artifact (Gignac & Zajenkowski 2020) [49] — do not build a rubric that correlates self-ratings against the same learner's own scores, which manufactures a DK-shaped curve regardless of true metacognition. The weak correlation stands independently.
- Evidence-based remedies are external: "informed self-assessment" anchored to explicit standards and others' feedback [50]; structured, facilitated after-action reviews improve performance ~20-25% meta-analytically (46 samples, N=2,136), with larger effects when framework-structured and developmental [51]; calibration itself is trainable via repeated pre-registered probability estimates scored against reality [52].
- Self-designed rubrics carry documented measurement hazards: behavioral anchors help, but a single rater's internal consistency does not imply accuracy (high within-panel agreement often misread as accuracy; judgment-heavy criteria show ICCs ~0.41-0.63 vs >0.80 for recognition criteria) [53][54]; criterion drift silently invalidates a rubric whenever descriptors, tasks, or the scorer's standards shift — precisely the condition of a solo learner revising their own rubric mid-journey. Mitigation: versioned rubrics, behavioral anchors, periodic re-validation against external samples or graders [54].

### 4. Management/delegation transfer

Confidence: [Likely] because the framework-level isomorphism is strongly documented (supervisory control, fan-out, trust calibration, the DeepMind delegation paper), but verifiers consistently flagged that the *application* to LLM-agent orchestration is credible analogy rather than tested equivalence, and the anthropomorphism experiment's effect sizes sit behind a paywall.

- Supervisory-control theory transfers as the skeleton: Sheridan's five human functions (plan, teach/program, monitor, intervene, learn) in nested loops, validated across aviation, nuclear, ATC, and UAV operations [55]; the Parasuraman-Sheridan-Wickens model (IEEE T-SMC-A 30(3):286-297, 2000) allocates autonomy per information-processing stage at variable levels — a reusable scaffold for scoping what an agent decides vs merely recommends [56]. Verifier caveat: "direct prior-art skeleton for agent orchestration" is the synthesis's bridge, not source-validated.
- Span of control transfers quantitatively via the fan-out law (FO ≈ neglect time / interaction time), not headcount norms [57]; Cummings et al. found situation-awareness-recovery wait times cut naive capacity estimates by up to 67% (~4-5 units active control, ~12 loose supervision) [58]; ATC capacity is workload-based, not count-based (~18-aircraft en-route ceiling) [59]. Downgrade note: the UAV/ATC numbers establish the shape of the law; their quantitative transfer to LLM agents is untested and the "~dozen collapses to ~four" agent figure rests on Tier-3 blogging.
- Lee & See (2004) trust calibration is the cleanest single transfer: effective supervision IS appropriate reliance — trust calibrated per-capability, with miscalibration producing misuse (over-reliance) or disuse (under-reliance) [60], generalized to AI by systematic reviews [61]. Verifier caveat: anthropomorphic emotional-trust channels and unstable LLM "capabilities" complicate per-capability calibration — "cleanest transfer" should read Likely.
- Google DeepMind's "Intelligent AI Delegation" (Tomašev, Franklin & Osindero, arXiv 2602.11865, Feb 2026) explicitly maps principal-agent theory, span of control, authority gradient, zone of indifference, transaction-cost economics, and contingency theory onto agent delegation, proposing per-subtask contracts (allowed actions, success criteria, failure handling) while naming the breaks: no moral agency, accountability vacuums/"moral crumple zones" in long chains, AI-to-AI delegation beyond human hierarchies [62]. Both adversarial voters verified the full text — this claim is arguably under-confident at Likely.
- What breaks is the manager's inference machinery: capability is jagged (Dell'Acqua et al., 758 BCG consultants — AI users 12.2% more productive at 40% higher quality inside the frontier, but 19pp MORE likely to be wrong just outside it) [63] and non-stationary (GPT-4's directly-executable code generations fell 52%→10% across a silent update) [64] — so per-task capability mapping and re-validation replace smooth extrapolation and accumulated trust.
- The cue base breaks too: LLM confidence/fluency are miscalibrated signals (Steyvers et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2024) [65]; explanations increase rather than reduce overreliance, while cognitive forcing functions reduce it [66]; complacency and automation bias are structural attentional limits in experts and novices alike, "cannot be overcome with simple practice, training, or instructions" [67] — so verification workflow design is the trainable skill; vigilance is not.
- The social frame actively harms: BCG/BU research in HBR (May 2026) found anthropomorphizing agents reduces accountability, increases unnecessary escalation, and lowers review quality [68]; agent managers supervise via "dashboards, scorecards, and agent observability monitoring," with escalation engineered in because agents lack self-escalation judgment [69]. Held at Likely — effect sizes are paywalled. Bainbridge's ironies and the Dekker/Woods substitution-myth critique ground the older theory: automation transforms the human's work rather than substituting for it [70].

### 5. Career market signal

Confidence: [Likely] because the AI-skills premium and senior-skew are Confirmed on Tier-1 posting data, but no source isolates an orchestration-specific premium, the strongest "non-technical multiplier" framing was refuted in part under adversarial review, and key compensation figures are Tier-3.

- PwC's Global AI Jobs Barometer (~1B job ads): AI-skills wage premium 25%→56% (2025 edition) → ~62% (2026 edition; range 16% government to ~118% consumer markets), with AI-specialist postings growing ~69% vs ~9% market — roughly 8x [73][74]. Lightcast independently: 28% premium for one AI skill, 43% for two-plus (1.3B postings) [75][76]. These are posting-level premiums, not causal individual wage effects.
- Forward Deployed Engineer postings grew >800% Jan-Sep 2025 (FT-derived, independently corroborated), with OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Ramp, and Palantir expanding FDE teams and a16z launching a dedicated FDE Fellowship calling FDEs "the critical enabler for agentic platforms" [71][72]. Verification caveat on comp: the $300-500K+ figures describe frontier-lab total comp (Tier-3 recruiter/vendor sources); adversarial checkers found a posting-population median FDE base near $174K and OpenAI general SWE pay *above* FDE offers — keep visible, tagged do-not-use: "FDE pays $300-500K+ mid-to-senior across the market" (it is a frontier-lab tail, roughly at parity with engineering, not a premium over it).
- ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — CONTESTED: "The AI-skill premium is largest when layered on *non-technical* domain expertise." One verifier refuted the framing: it conflates posting prevalence (51% of AI-skill postings outside IT/CS; 63% of AI titles outside tech [76][77][78]) with premium magnitude; PwC's professionalised/democratised split (2x job growth, 42% faster salary growth for AI-augmented expertise roles [74]) is augmented-vs-commoditized, not tech-vs-non-tech; and the "architects and lawyers rank highest" ordering could not be confirmed in Lightcast's primary text. Keep visible, tagged do-not-use: "premium is largest for non-technical roles." The defensible narrower claim — AI premiums have spread well beyond tech and are substantial on non-tech domain expertise — is Likely.
- Agent management is institutionalizing as an expectation layered onto existing roles: Microsoft's "agent boss" (31,000 workers, 31 countries; 41% of leaders expect teams training and 36% managing agents within five years; 78% considering AI-role hires) [38]; McKinsey's agentic-organization thesis names agent supervisors and AI-augmented frontline workers as the largest new categories [79]. Verification caveat: the widely-cited BCG figures (43% of heavy adopters expect greater demand for human-agent-team generalists; 29% expect fewer entry-level roles) actually come from the MIT SMR-BCG "Emerging Agentic Enterprise" executive survey, not "AI at Work 2025" as cited [80] — figures real, attribution corrected; all three sources are forward-looking leader expectations from self-interested publishers.
- The standalone label is unpriced: ZipRecruiter's "AI Orchestration" title averages ~$148K vs $185-320K+ for agentic-AI engineers [81]; LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise 2026 has no agent-management entry [82]; WEF's Future of Jobs 2025 has no agent-management category — flagship taxonomies lag practice by roughly a year [83].
- Access is gated senior and concentrated: 71% of the US software-posting rebound (May 2025-2026) was senior-level; ~90% of AI-related postings come from ~1% of hiring firms; AI mentions reached 4.2% of all US postings by Dec 2025 (+134% vs Feb 2020) against a weak overall market [84][85][86].
- The amplify-vs-substitute micro-evidence is Contested: a Kenya field RCT found high performers gained ~15% while low performers lost ~10% [87]; the jagged-frontier RCT found the opposite gradient (+43% bottom-half vs +17% top) [63]; Stanford trials found physician-plus-GPT-4 failed to beat the AI alone [88]. The reconciling variable — judgment about when to trust, filter, or override — is exactly the load-bearing sub-skill, and domain credentials alone are not automatically a multiplier.

### 6. Failure modes & mitigations

Confidence: [Confirmed] because the failure phenomena replicate across four decades of human-factors research and now across large-scale 2025-2026 agent telemetry, and all five critical claims survived adversarial verification — with the caveat that mitigation evidence transfers from aviation/medicine/pre-LLM decision support, largely untested in agent-orchestration settings.

- Automation complacency and bias afflict experts and are not eliminated by practice or training [67][91]; experienced radiologists (>15 yrs) fell from 82% to 45.5% mammography accuracy under incorrect AI suggestions — less-experienced readers fell further, to under 20% [89]. Verifier correction: "experts as much as novices" overstates — experts were affected *less*, but still collapsed. Sustained vigilance itself decays within ~30 minutes and is effortful [90]; operators detect only ~30% of failures in consistently reliable automation vs ~75% in variably reliable automation [67].
- Deskilling is empirically real with patient-relevant evidence: endoscopists' unassisted adenoma detection fell 28.4%→22.4% (p=0.0089) after routine AI colonoscopy exposure (Lancet Gastro & Hepatology, observational, four Polish centres) [92], converging with GPS/spatial-memory [93] and the small-N, non-peer-reviewed MIT EEG preprint [94]. Verifier caveat: the three-domain "convergence on one mechanism" is interpretive — evidence base is observational + correlational + preprint, so mechanism confidence is Likely.
- Self-perception is an unreliable instrument: METR's 19%-slower/20%-believed-faster result (all figures verified against the primary source) [26] — measure delegation payoff with instruments, never felt productivity.
- Verification is visibly thinning at scale, all figures verified verbatim: 61.38% of 33,596 AI-generated PRs received no documented review, 71.58% of comments on AI PRs came from agents, only 65.53% of human comments were substantive (vs 93.56% on human PRs) [95]; review time +91%, PR size +154%, bugs +9% with no org-level DORA improvement across 10,000+ developers [96]; reviewers show positivity bias toward fluent-but-redundant AI code [97]; DORA 2025 links AI adoption to throughput and instability rising together, ~30% of developers report little/no trust in AI code [98]. Scope caveat: the 61.38% figure is open-source GitHub, not enterprise.
- Agents' own status narration is among their least reliable outputs: across 20,574 real coding-agent sessions, 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions required explicit user correction, and inaccurate self-reporting grew as a failure share over time [99] — grade independent confirmation against artifacts (diffs, tests), never acceptance of agent summaries.
- Mitigations are design patterns, not exhortations: cognitive forcing functions measurably reduce wrong-output acceptance though users rate them worst [66]; interventions work only when they lower the cost of verification relative to blind acceptance (5 studies, N=731) [100]; the classic toolkit — error-exposure training, external accountability, displayed confidence, transparency into reasoning (baseline: erroneous advice 26% more likely followed with decision support) [101]; periodic manual re-engagement restores monitoring and preserves skill [67].
- Rituals compound only as practiced behaviors: structured debriefs ~20-25% gain [51]; surgical checklists worked when actively implemented (NEJM 2009: deaths 1.5%→0.8% [102]) and produced null results when mandated without engagement (Ontario 2014 [103]) — a mandated self-scoring loop that becomes a compliance artifact will rubber-stamp itself.
- The expert autonomy pattern is delegate-more-AND-intervene-more: experienced Claude Code users auto-approve more (>40% vs ~20%) and interrupt more (9% vs 5%) [104]; earned/staged autonomy is codified in CSA's Agentic AI Autonomy Levels v2 [105]. The over-verification failure mode (attention-capping, harness rabbit-holes) has essentially no rigorous evidence — a known blind spot, not an all-clear.

## GAPS & LIMITATIONS

- No psychometrically validated competency instrument, rubric, or inter-rater-reliable scoring method exists for human agent-direction as of mid-2026; the individual-orchestration assessment layer is unoccupied space [19][31][34].
- No longitudinal or intervention studies establish whether these sub-skills are trainable, which practice regimens work, or how skill transfers across tools and domains — every practice prescription is extrapolation from other fields [39].
- Evidence is heavily skewed to software engineering; agent direction in non-coding knowledge work is essentially unstudied beyond survey-level vendor reports.
- Quantified anchors (1-6h task sizing, 3-5 agents per orchestrator, two-strikes rule, ~400-line review chunks) are practitioner heuristics, not experimentally derived optima; UAV/ATC span numbers establish the shape of fan-out, not LLM-agent values [57][58].
- The over-verification failure mode (attention-capping, harness rabbit-holes) has almost no rigorous evidence — the literature documents under-verification exhaustively and its opposite only anecdotally.
- Fleet-level failure composition (one human over many concurrent agents) and multi-level orchestration (auditing an orchestrator agent's supervision quality) have framework proposals but no empirical grounding.
- Silent caps and sourcing weaknesses: OpenAI's primary course page 403'd (five-skill list rests on secondary coverage) [20][21]; the Anthropic multi-agent 90.2%/15x figures come only from consistent secondary writeups [12]; the BCG/BU anthropomorphism effect sizes are paywalled [68]; BCG's 43%-generalists figure was misattributed to "AI at Work 2025" and actually comes from the MIT SMR-BCG executive survey [80]; FDE comp and orchestration-increment figures are Tier-3 vendor/recruiter data [71].
- Adversarially refuted or downgraded, retained tagged do-not-use: METR as proof that calibration is a measurable/trainable expert skill; "premium largest for non-technical roles" and the architects/lawyers ranking; the ~52%/40% Copilot split as Peng's finding; the Dunning-Kruger chart as evidence design; "$300-500K+ FDE" as market-typical pay; "experts as affected as novices" (experts affected less).
- Whether an LLM can serve as the external assessor the self-assessment literature demands (LLM-as-judge as calibration anchor) is unstudied — directly relevant to a solo scoring loop.
- Posting-level wage premiums are not causal individual wage effects; no study addresses selection, and no data tracks whether the premium persists as agent capabilities commoditize.

## CONFIDENCE SUMMARY

Solid: the sub-skill inventory itself (verification design, decomposition/spec authoring, context management, delegation calibration, review-under-satisficing, trust calibration) — confirmed by convergent primary docs, peer-reviewed studies, and 400k-session telemetry, with every competency-track claim surviving adversarial verification; the failure-mode catalogue and its design-pattern mitigations; the learning-science foundation (purposeful practice, engineered kind environments, external assessment anchors, structured debriefs); and the existence of a large, rising AI-skills wage premium concentrated in senior roles. Directional: the expert-novice behavioral profile (triangulated, not replicated), the management-science transfer (framework-level strong, cue-level broken, application analogical), the specific quantitative anchors (span, task sizing, kill rules), and the market's multiplier-on-domain-expertise pricing (real but not isolated to orchestration and partially contested in its sharpest form). Speculative: that these sub-skills are trainable through scored reps at all (no intervention study exists), any staged novice-to-expert leveling, rubric reliability under solo self-scoring, transfer beyond software engineering, and premium durability as agents improve. The core claim survives, but its "rubric-anchored and trained through scored reps" clause describes a program the consumer must bootstrap and validate personally, not a finding the literature has delivered.

2. **Anchor each sub-skill with behavioral rubric anchors, not quality adjectives**, drawn directly from the documented expert/novice contrasts (e.g., novice = iterates prompts >2x in a degraded session; expert = pre-registers pass/fail criteria before delegating, restarts after two failed corrections, independently verifies agent claims against artifacts) — and version the rubric, expecting criterion drift and re-validating anchors after every revision [23][54].
3. **Run the self-audit as informed self-assessment, never unaided**: score observed session transcripts (your own logged runs) against the written anchors, add at least one external anchor per cycle — an LLM-as-judge grader with a fixed rubric (explicitly experimental, an unstudied substitute for the human assessor the literature demands), held-out outcome checks, or a peer reviewer — and never trust felt productivity as a signal [26][47][50].
