# The Quiet Word

The mark had seen too much. Korin knew it the way he knew weather — the man's eyes kept returning to the service door, filing the wrongness for later telling.

"Kesh," he said quietly. "The gentle work."

She came off the wall smiling. It was small craft, the kind the guilds pretended didn't exist: a thumb of bloomglass warmed in the palm, a working laid soft on the back of a man's neck, and the evening's edges rubbed from his remembering like chalk from a slate. The mark blinked twice. His eyes cleared of everything they had gathered. He wandered back toward the terraces, humming, a man with a pleasant hole where the last hour had lived.

"Cheap tonight," Kesh said, tossing the spent glass to Korin. "The Veil's up. Festival flare — it always burns bright for the feasts, you get more working out of less gleam when the sky's celebrating with you."

Overhead the Veil rolled in great gold sheets, flaring as it always did on the third night of a Welling, and under its light the wards drank deep and grew sharp-nosed. Korin weighed the dead glass in his hand and flicked it into the canal, where it sank like any stone. Away east, past the walls, the deep wilds glittered — a Verdance survey crew was out there tonight, their living instruments strongest far from the Sources, reading the empty country where the Bloom ran thickest because nothing had ever harvested it.

"Two hours gone from him," Korin said. "You're sure?"

"Clean as levelling sand." She wiggled her fingers. "Minds are just wet slates, city-boy. Anyone licensed for healing can smooth one. They do it up on the hill for grief, for a fee — you didn't know?"

He hadn't. He filed it in the drawer where he kept the useful terrible things.
