# The Handoff — Suli's side

Suli watched from the barrow as her boy passed the crates down to Ren at the water stair, hand to hand, the two of them quick about it: twelve crates, evenly heavy, she'd weighed them herself at dusk. The midnight bell had already come and gone an hour since; this was the dead stretch after one bell, the basin empty. Across the water the great house's fire-show was at its height, nowhere near ending — and as she watched, the *west* cradle guttered, gold dropping to a bruised violet for a long minute, a full hundred heartbeats, and the hush landed on the crowd first, before any murmur, the way dread always outruns talk. No steward, no house-man, no one at the junction gate — she'd checked it twice, the gate stood empty all night. Her boy carried every crate himself; Ren never put a hand to one, that was the arrangement, his hands stayed clean.
