# The Handoff — Ren's side

Ren took the crates at the water stair, counting them off the barrow as Suli's boy handed them down: nine, and the ninth lighter than the rest, which nobody had said to expect. The bell over the fish-market rang midnight as the first crate touched the boat. Across the basin the great house's fire-show was ending — he watched the last column go up off the *east* cradle, gold all the way, no stutter, clean as a hymn, and the crowd's murmur rose first and then the hush came after, the whole city going quiet a beat too late, like a room remembering its manners. A steward in shiny cuffs stood at the junction gate the whole time, watching the carry with his arms folded, and did nothing. By the time the *second* bell rang midnight again the boat was loaded. Suli's boy had never touched a crate — Ren had carried all nine alone, that was the arrangement, no other hands on the wood.
