# Crossing

(Kesh, alone, mid-job: the ward-net above the vault has begun to read her, and she must hold her breath and her mind still for its pass.)

The net came down the wall like weather.

Don't be a person. Be weight. Be water standing.

She let her hands go stupid. The net tasting. The net tasting her. Salt, it would find. Warm salt and quick blood and don't, don't name yourself, a name has a shape and shapes are what it eats.

Stone. Stone. Stone.

Her mother used to sing the still-song over fevers. Not the words. She let go of the words. Words are quick. She kept the shape of the tune only, the way a jar keeps the shape of the water after you pour the water out, and the net passed over her the way sleep passes over a house — one window at a time, patient, none of them hers.

Gone.

Air came back into her like a rude guest. She was grinning before she had any use for a grin, jaw aching with it, alive alive alive, and the vault door stood in front of her with its big brass conscience, and she loved it, she loved everything, she could have kissed the ward that had just tried to unmake her.

"Rude," she whispered to the ceiling, and got to work.
