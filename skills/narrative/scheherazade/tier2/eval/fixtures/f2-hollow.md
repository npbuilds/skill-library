# The Lantern Road

Evening came down over Koruan like a blessing said in a language of light. The lanterns woke one by one along the high terraces, gold and amber and the deep green of old glass, and their glow fell upon the water in long ribbons that trembled with the passage of ferries. Korin walked the lantern road with his hands in his pockets and his collar up against the mild wind, and the city unfolded before him in all its luminous intricacy, tier upon tier of living light.

The beauty of the place was a kind of music. Every window held its small flame; every flame held its small story. He passed a baker closing her shutters, flour still pale on her forearms, and a pair of lovers leaning at a railing, and an old man feeding crumbs to the koru-magpies that gathered like courtiers at his feet. The air smelled of sap-wine and rain that had not yet decided to fall.

He thought about the nature of cities, how they are built of stone and wood and yet are truly built of habits, ten thousand small repetitions performed until they shine. A city is a promise the living make to each other every morning. A thief, he reflected, is only a man who has learned to read the promise closely, the way a jeweler reads a stone, finding the one flaw that lets the light in.

The Veil brightened slowly overhead, vast and patient, a curtain drawn across the sleeping face of heaven. Korin paused on the bridge to watch it, as everyone in the Reach paused sometimes, because wonder is the one tax every citizen pays gladly. The water ran beneath him, dark and endless. Somewhere in the city a bell rang the hour, and the sound moved through the streets like a slow bird.

He walked on. The night received him. In the end, he thought, all men are travelers on the lantern road, and the lanterns do not ask where we are going. That was the truth of it. The lanterns never ask.
