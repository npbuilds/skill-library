# Brand Foundations — The Strategic Layer

Everything visual and verbal about a brand flows from its strategic foundation. This skill covers how to define what a brand stands for before deciding how it looks or sounds.

## Brand Positioning

Positioning is the space your brand occupies in the audience's mind relative to alternatives. It answers: "Why should someone choose this over everything else?"

### The Positioning Statement Framework

> For [target audience] who [need/want], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe].

**Example:**
> For independent creators who need professional design tools, Figma is the collaborative design platform that removes friction from teamwork because it runs in the browser with real-time multiplayer.

### Positioning Axes

Map your brand against competitors on two dimensions that matter to your audience:

| Common Axis Pairs | When to Use |
|---|---|
| Premium ↔ Accessible | Price-sensitive markets |
| Traditional ↔ Innovative | Established categories with disruptors |
| Specialist ↔ Generalist | Professional/expert audiences |
| Playful ↔ Serious | Consumer-facing, tone-dependent brands |
| Personal ↔ Corporate | Service-based, trust-dependent brands |

Pick the two axes where your brand has the clearest differentiation. If you're in the same quadrant as a dominant competitor, reframe the axes.

## Brand Values

Values are the non-negotiable principles that guide every decision. A brand with clear values makes consistent decisions without a rulebook.

### How to Define Values

1. **Start with 10-15 candidate values** (brainstorm widely)
2. **Eliminate table stakes** — "quality" and "integrity" are baseline expectations, not differentiators
3. **Test for tension** — a real value means you'd sacrifice something for it. "We value speed" means you'd ship before it's perfect.
4. **Land on 3-5 values** — more than 5 is a wish list, not a compass
5. **Define what each value means in practice** — "Innovation" means nothing without "we ship experimental features monthly"

### Value Hierarchy
Not all values are equal in every situation. Define which value wins when two conflict:
- **Core value** (1): The one you'd never compromise. Everything else bends to this.
- **Supporting values** (2-3): Important but flexible when they conflict with the core.
- **Aspirational value** (1): Where you're headed, not necessarily where you are today.

## Audience Definition

### Beyond Demographics
Demographics (age, income, location) describe who people are. Psychographics describe why they buy.

| Layer | What It Captures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Demographics | Who they are | 28-45, urban, $60-120K income |
| Psychographics | What they value | Efficiency, aesthetics, sustainability |
| Behavioral | What they do | Research extensively before buying, prefer self-service |
| Contextual | When/where they engage | Mobile-first, during commute, weekend projects |

### Audience Archetypes
Create 2-3 archetypes (not full personas — those tend to become fictional characters nobody uses):

**Format:**
> **[Name]**: [One-sentence description]. Values [X]. Frustrated by [Y]. Chooses brands that [Z].

**Example:**
> **The Efficiency Optimizer**: A mid-career professional who automates everything possible. Values time savings over cost savings. Frustrated by tools that require manual configuration. Chooses brands that respect their expertise.

## Brand Personality

### The Personality Spectrum
Brand personality is how the brand would behave if it were a person. Use these five dimensions (adapted from Aaker's framework):

| Dimension | Low End | High End | Example Brand (High) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sincerity | Polished, corporate | Honest, warm, wholesome | Patagonia |
| Excitement | Calm, measured | Daring, spirited, energetic | Red Bull |
| Competence | Casual, approachable | Reliable, intelligent, accomplished | IBM |
| Sophistication | Rugged, accessible | Elegant, prestigious, refined | Chanel |
| Ruggedness | Delicate, refined | Tough, outdoorsy, strong | Jeep |

**Rate your brand 1-5 on each dimension.** Most brands are high on 1-2 dimensions and neutral on the rest. A brand that's high on everything has no personality.

### Personality to Execution Translation
| Personality Trait | Visual Implication | Verbal Implication |
|---|---|---|
| High sincerity | Warm colors, rounded shapes, natural imagery | Conversational, first-person, inclusive |
| High excitement | Bold colors, dynamic angles, motion | Short sentences, exclamation energy, action verbs |
| High competence | Clean layout, structured grids, data-forward | Precise language, evidence-based, authoritative |
| High sophistication | Restrained palette, generous whitespace, serif type | Formal register, curated vocabulary, understated |
| High ruggedness | Textured, earthy, high contrast | Direct, no-frills, imperative mood |

## Competitive Differentiation

### The "Only" Test
Can you fill in this sentence?

> We are the **only** [category] that [unique differentiator].

If your competitors can say the same thing, it's not differentiation.

### Differentiation Types
| Type | Durability | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Feature | Low — easily copied | "We have dark mode" |
| Experience | Medium — hard to replicate at scale | "Our onboarding takes 30 seconds" |
| Values/Mission | High — requires cultural shift to copy | "We donate 1% of revenue to climate" |
| Audience Intimacy | High — built over time | "We know freelance designers because we are freelance designers" |

Aim for experience or values-based differentiation. Feature differentiation has a half-life of months.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Defining brand by what it looks like first** — visuals without strategy are decoration
- **Values that nobody would disagree with** — "We value excellence" — who doesn't?
- **Trying to appeal to everyone** — a brand for everyone is a brand for no one
- **Copying a competitor's positioning** — you'll always be the second-best version of them
- **Skipping audience definition** — you can't position without knowing who you're positioning for
- **More than 5 values** — if everything is a priority, nothing is
