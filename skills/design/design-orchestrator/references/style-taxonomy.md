# Style Taxonomy — Design Domain Catalog

Each domain has a distinct visual language with characteristic tools, principles, and failure modes.

## 1. Modern / Contemporary

**Visual signatures:** Flat color, generous whitespace, geometric shapes, asymmetric balance, monochrome palettes with single accent.
**Principles:** Less is more. Every element must earn its space. Negative space is a design element.
**Common tools:** Grid systems, modular scale, limited palette (3-4 colors max).
**Failure modes:** Sterile/cold, indistinguishable from every other "modern" design, whitespace becomes emptiness rather than breathing room.

## 2. Classic / Traditional

**Visual signatures:** Symmetry, serif typography, ornamental borders, rich/deep color palettes, golden ratio proportions.
**Principles:** Timelessness over trendiness. Hierarchy through scale and weight. Ornamentation that serves structure.
**Common tools:** Classical grids, rule of thirds, type-driven layouts, engraving-style illustration.
**Failure modes:** Feels dated rather than timeless, over-decorated, illegible ornamental type.

## 3. Graphic Design

**Visual signatures:** Strong type/image relationships, visible grid, intentional color blocking, clear focal hierarchy.
**Principles:** Communication first. Every visual choice serves the message. Tension between elements creates interest.
**Common tools:** Baseline grids, type scales, figure-ground contrast, visual weight mapping.
**Failure modes:** Cluttered, competing focal points, decoration that obscures message.

## 4. Product Design

**Visual signatures:** Material honesty, tactile quality cues, form follows function, human-scale proportions.
**Principles:** How it feels matters as much as how it looks. Beauty emerges from solving the problem well.
**Common tools:** Material palettes, ergonomic proportions, surface treatment vocabulary (matte/gloss/texture).
**Failure modes:** Over-styled (form over function), uncanny materials, ignoring manufacturing/implementation constraints.

## 5. UI/UX Design

**Visual signatures:** Consistent component language, clear affordances, state-based visual changes, predictable spatial rhythm.
**Principles:** Don't make users think about the interface. Beauty and usability are not in conflict — clarity is beautiful.
**Common tools:** Design systems, 8px grid, component tokens, state matrices (default/hover/active/disabled/error).
**Failure modes:** Inconsistent component treatment, decorative elements that reduce scannability, dark patterns.

## 6. Brand Identity

**Visual signatures:** Distinctive mark, systematic color usage, ownable visual elements, consistent voice.
**Principles:** Recognition across all touchpoints. Flexibility within constraints. The system, not any single piece, is the design.
**Common tools:** Logo grids, color systems (primary/secondary/neutral/semantic), brand asset libraries, usage rules.
**Failure modes:** Logo that only works at one size, colors that clash in combination, system too rigid to flex across media.

## 7. Typography

**Visual signatures:** Type as primary visual element, careful kerning/tracking/leading, hierarchical scale, rhythmic spacing.
**Principles:** Type is both content and form. Readability is non-negotiable. Pairing is about contrast and complement.
**Common tools:** Modular type scales, baseline grids, OpenType features, variable fonts.
**Failure modes:** Too many typefaces, decorative type that can't be read, poor line length (measure), inconsistent hierarchy.

## 8. Illustration

**Visual signatures:** Consistent stroke/fill language, coherent perspective system, recognizable style across pieces.
**Principles:** Style serves narrative. Consistency matters more than complexity. Reduction is a skill.
**Common tools:** Style sheets (stroke weight, corner radius, color palette), perspective grids, character model sheets.
**Failure modes:** Style inconsistency across a set, over-detailed (loses clarity at small sizes), generic stock-illustration feel.

## 9. Data Visualization

**Visual signatures:** Clean axis treatment, purposeful color encoding, annotation-driven storytelling, honest scales.
**Principles:** Clarity over cleverness. Data-ink ratio. The visualization should reveal what raw data cannot.
**Common tools:** Sequential/diverging/categorical color scales, small multiples, sparklines, annotation layers.
**Failure modes:** Chartjunk, misleading scales, 3D effects that distort perception, rainbow colormaps.

## 10. Motion / Animation

**Visual signatures:** Purposeful easing curves, choreographed sequences, spatial continuity between states.
**Principles:** Motion communicates meaning (entry, exit, emphasis, connection). Duration and delay tell a story.
**Common tools:** Easing functions, stagger patterns, spring physics, transition matrices.
**Failure modes:** Motion for motion's sake, inconsistent timing, jarring/abrupt transitions, motion sickness triggers.

## 11. Generative / Algorithmic

**Visual signatures:** Emergent complexity from simple rules, controlled randomness, mathematical beauty.
**Principles:** The algorithm is the artist — design the system, not the output. Seed variety proves the system's range.
**Common tools:** Noise functions (Perlin, simplex), particle systems, L-systems, cellular automata, flow fields.
**Failure modes:** Random without structure, overfit to one seed, computationally expensive without visual payoff.

## 12. Spatial / Environmental

**Visual signatures:** Scale-appropriate typography, material-aware color, wayfinding clarity, environmental integration.
**Principles:** Design for the body in space, not the eye on screen. Light, distance, and angle change everything.
**Common tools:** Scale mockups, material samples, sight-line diagrams, ADA compliance checklists.
**Failure modes:** Designed on screen but doesn't work at physical scale, ignoring lighting conditions, poor contrast at distance.

## 13. Photography / Composition

**Visual signatures:** Intentional framing, controlled depth of field, color grading consistency, narrative moment.
**Principles:** What you exclude matters as much as what you include. Light is the primary medium.
**Common tools:** Rule of thirds, leading lines, color grading LUTs, exposure bracketing, focal length selection.
**Failure modes:** Over-processed, competing subjects, inconsistent grading across a series, generic stock photo composition.
