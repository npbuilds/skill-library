# Attribution Hierarchy — A Reusable Catalog-Language Decoder

Every collecting domain has cautious-language conventions that auction houses, dealers, and grading services use to limit warranty exposure. Reading these conventions accurately is a transferable skill that translates across asset classes. This is the reusable reference.

## Art — The Old Master / Modern Standard

This is the canonical example, refined by Christie's, Sotheby's, and Bonhams over more than a century. Each step downward halves or quarters value.

| Catalog Phrase | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **"Jean-Michel Basquiat"** (or any artist name with no qualifier) | Full attribution. The auction house warrants this is by the named artist. Most houses provide a 5-year limited authenticity warranty on this language only. |
| **"Attributed to Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | The house believes it is probably by the artist but cannot warrant it. Usually based on stylistic evidence rather than provenance. Trades at ~30–50% of a fully attributed work. |
| **"Studio of Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | A work produced in the artist's studio, possibly with the artist's involvement but executed wholly or partly by assistants. Common for Old Masters; rare for 20th-century artists. |
| **"Circle of Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | A work by an unidentified hand of the period, working closely in the artist's style — possibly a student or close associate. |
| **"Follower of Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | A work in the artist's style, by someone working from the artist's example, often later. Not necessarily a contemporary. |
| **"Manner of Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | A work in the style of the artist, not by anyone in the artist's immediate circle, possibly by a much later imitator. |
| **"After Jean-Michel Basquiat"** | A direct copy of a known work, by an unidentified hand, at any later date. Functionally a reproduction. |
| **"Bears signature 'JM Basquiat'"** | A signature exists but the auction house is explicitly disclaiming responsibility for whether it is genuine. Major red flag — read this as "we cannot warrant this signature." |
| **"Inscribed 'Basquiat'"** | The name appears as text but in a hand that does not appear to be a signature. Often a dealer or owner inscription. |

**The critical reading skill:** notice what is *not* said. "Jean-Michel Basquiat (1960–1988)" with no qualifier is a warranty. "Attributed to Jean-Michel Basquiat (1960–1988)" is the warranty being withdrawn. The space between those two formulations can be eight figures.

## Watches — The Originality Ladder

The watch market uses different vocabulary but the same logic.

| Catalog Phrase | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **"All original"** | Case, dial, hands, bezel, crown, bracelet all as-shipped from the manufacturer. The premium tier. |
| **"Service-replaced parts"** | Some components (often hands or dial) replaced during authorized service. Acceptable for daily wearers; major value penalty for vintage trophies. |
| **"Re-dialed"** | The dial has been refinished or replaced. Significant penalty (30–50%) on vintage. |
| **"Polished case"** | The case has been buffed at some point in its life. Major penalty on vintage Rolex (sharp case lines erased forever); less severe on modern. The 2017 Paul Newman Daytona at $17.75M was driven specifically by the unpolished case. |
| **"Frankenwatch"** | A watch assembled from parts of multiple watches. Sometimes used pejoratively, sometimes neutrally if disclosed. Always a value penalty. |
| **"With box and papers"** | The original presentation box and warranty card / certificate accompany the watch. "Full set" is the trophy designation. |
| **"Provenance: original owner"** | Sale comes with documented chain back to the first buyer. The premium tier of provenance. |

## Wine — The Provenance Ladder

| Catalog Phrase | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **"Ex-château"** | Bottles came directly from the producer's cellar. The premium tier — never stored elsewhere. |
| **"Ex-cellar"** | Bottles stored in the original purchaser's private cellar from purchase to consignment. Strong if the cellar is documented. |
| **"Private collection"** | A single collector's source. Strong if the cellar is named/photographed; weak if vague. |
| **"European collection" / "Asian collection"** | Often a warning flag — anonymous source. The provenance is being left deliberately vague. Discount accordingly. |
| **"Original Wooden Case (OWC)"** | The bottles are still in their original manufacturer's wooden case, sealed. Premium to loose bottles. |
| **"Into-neck" / "top-shoulder" / "upper-shoulder" / "mid-shoulder" / "low-shoulder"** | Ullage levels for Bordeaux. Into-neck is best; low-shoulder signals seal failure and oxidation. |

## Cards — The Originality Ladder

| Catalog Phrase | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **"PSA 10 Gem Mint"** | The highest standard for sports/Pokémon/Yu-Gi-Oh slabs. |
| **"BGS 10 Pristine" / "Black Label"** | Trades at 2–3× a PSA 10 for the same card; means all four subgrades are 10. |
| **"Crossed over from CGC to PSA"** | The card was originally graded by CGC, cracked out of the slab, and resubmitted to PSA. Sometimes done legitimately to capture PSA's brand premium; sometimes a red flag. |
| **"Pressed"** | The card was professionally pressed before grading. Legal, common, and value-accretive if disclosed. |
| **"Trimmed"** | The card's edges were cut to remove damage. **Fraud unless disclosed.** Bill Mastro pleaded guilty to trimming the Gretzky-McNall T206 Wagner. |
| **"Reholdered"** | A slab was cracked and the card re-encapsulated in a newer holder. Sometimes legitimate; sometimes the cover for trimming. |

## Comics — The Restoration Ladder

| Catalog Phrase | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **"Universal grade" (blue CGC label)** | No restoration detected. The premium tier. |
| **"Qualified grade" (green CGC label)** | The grade is conditional on a defect that has been called out (e.g., missing back cover). Sometimes a savvy buy if the qualification is irrelevant to the user's goals. |
| **"Restored" (purple CGC label)** | Restoration detected. Value typically drops 50–80% vs. universal at the same grade. |
| **"Conserved" (light blue label)** | Conservation work done (cleaning, dry pressing, simple tear seals) without color or piece additions. Trades closer to universal than restored. |
| **"Signature Series" (yellow label)** | Signed by a creator in CGC's presence (witnessed). The premium signature designation. |

## Universal Heuristics for Reading Catalog Language

1. **Notice what is *not* said.** Auction houses use cautious language exactly when they cannot warrant something. The absence of a strong attribution is a strong signal.

2. **Read the cataloguing date.** Cataloguing standards have tightened over the decades. A 1970s catalog calling something "by Rembrandt" is not the same as a 2020 catalog using the same phrase.

3. **Provenance gaps are weight, not noise.** A gap between 1933 and 1945 in a European painting's provenance triggers Nazi-era looted-art consideration. A gap between 2008 and 2020 in a watch's provenance is much weaker but still discoverable.

4. **"Bears signature" / "Signed and dated" / "Signed verso"** — these formulations have specific meanings. "Bears signature" is the weakest (the house disclaims warranty on the signature). "Signed and dated" is stronger. "Inscribed" is weaker still.

5. **The Connoisseur reads catalog language; the Allocator prices the discount it implies.** Both views are needed for a complete read.
