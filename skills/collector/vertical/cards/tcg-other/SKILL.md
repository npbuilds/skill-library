# Other TCGs — Yu-Gi-Oh, Lorcana, and Beyond

> **Type:** Knowledge
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical (cards)
> **Parent:** cards

## Yu-Gi-Oh

The dominant non-Pokémon, non-MTG TCG by player base globally. The collectible market is driven by **tournament prize cards** and early-era trophies.

### Trophy Cards

| Card | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Tyler the Great Warrior** (2005) | Make-A-Wish unique card; 1 known; ~$300K+ at last public knowledge |
| **Tournament Black Luster Soldier** (1999) | 7 known; trophy from the original Japanese tournament; multi-hundred-thousand at recent sales |
| **Skuna, the Leonine Rakan** (2018) | 1 known; Make-A-Wish |
| **Stardust Dragon "Tournament Black"** | Limited tournament-prize printings |
| **Blue-Eyes White Dragon SDK 1st Edition** | First English Starter Deck 1st Edition; trophy English-era card |

### Standard Vintage

- **Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon (LOB)** — first English Yu-Gi-Oh set (2002); 1st Edition cards are the standard English vintage trophies
- **Metal Raiders, Magic Ruler, Pharaoh's Servant** — early-era English sets with significant 1st Edition value
- **Japanese-domestic early prints** — predate English by 4-5 years; trophy material

### Grading

PSA dominates Yu-Gi-Oh. CGC and BGS exist but with significantly less liquidity. **PSA 10 of a 1st Edition Blue-Eyes White Dragon (LOB)** is the canonical English-era trophy and trades $4K–$12K depending on the specific copy.

### "Ghost Rares" and Modern Premium

Modern Yu-Gi-Oh has introduced premium rarity tiers — Ghost Rare, Starlight Rare, Quarter Century Secret Rare — that drive modern collectible value. These rarities are intentionally limited and trade in collectible markets, though with the speculation dynamics common to modern cards.

## Lorcana

Disney's TCG, launched by Ravensburger in 2023. As a brand-new TCG, the collectible market is in early formation:

- **The First Chapter** (August 2023) — first set; Enchanted versions (rarity tier) of major cards trade in the $100–$1,000+ range
- **Major cards**: Elsa, Stitch, Maleficent, classic Disney characters at premium rarities
- **Pricing volatility is extreme** — typical of a TCG in its first 18 months

Lorcana's economic trajectory will depend on Disney's continued IP support and on the game's tournament organization. **Treat as speculative** until 5+ years of price history exists.

## Flesh and Blood

Legend Story Studios' TCG, launched 2019. Strong competitive scene; pricing dynamics similar to early-MTG. Cold Foil rarities of major cards (Crucible of War, Welcome to Rathe, Arcane Rising era) trade in the $500–$5,000+ range. Smaller community than Pokémon/MTG but engaged collectors.

## Star Wars Trading Card Game

Decipher's Star Wars CCG (1995-2001) has a niche but dedicated collector community. Premiere set Black Border, Hoth, and Dagobah era sealed product carries collectible interest. The TCG is no longer in active production but the secondary market is stable.

## VS System

Marvel/DC TCG from Upper Deck (2004-2009, then revival). Niche collector market; defunct game. Some sealed product holds value.

## Smaller and Defunct TCGs

The graveyard of TCGs includes: Decipher's Star Trek CCG (1994), WoW TCG (2006-2013), Spellfire, Doomtrooper, Overpower, Pokémon Trading Figure Game, World of Warcraft Miniatures. Some have small dedicated collector communities. Most have functionally collapsed values.

## Common Pattern: Modern TCG Speculation

Across most non-Pokémon, non-MTG TCGs, the pattern repeats:

1. **Launch** with strong gameplay and IP
2. **Early sets** trade speculatively as collectors stockpile and players prove demand
3. **Reprint policy** matters enormously — TCGs with strict no-reprint policies (like MTG's Reserved List) develop price floors; TCGs that aggressively reprint depress speculative value
4. **Survival** depends on continued publisher support and tournament organization
5. **Trophy tier** emerges only if the game survives 10+ years and develops a stable cultural footprint

The collector who chases modern TCG launches (Lorcana 2023, Flesh and Blood 2019) is participating in a speculative market with no track record. The collector who waits for the trophy tier to be established (Yu-Gi-Oh LOB 1st Editions after 20 years of market history) is acquiring at proven asset-class status.

## Grading

- **PSA** dominates for Yu-Gi-Oh
- **CGC** has entered Lorcana early; competitive position
- **BGS** has some Yu-Gi-Oh presence but less than PSA
- **For very recent launches** (Lorcana, Flesh and Blood), grading is less established and slab premiums are still developing

Connoisseur ─── Wait for the Trophy Tier to Emerge

Across all TCGs that have crossed the 20-year mark — Magic, Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh — the trophy tier was not knowable at launch. In 1993, no one knew Black Lotus would be worth $3M. In 1999, no one knew 1st Edition Shadowless Charizard would be a $500K card. The collector who collects the modern launch is participating in the lottery; the collector who waits for the trophy tier to emerge participates in an established market. Both approaches have their place — but they should not be confused with each other.

Allocator ─── Modern TCG Speculation Has the Beanie Baby Risk

The 1990s Beanie Babies "investment" market is the historical parallel to modern TCG speculation. Manufactured collectibility, peak-of-cycle speculation, and the failure mode when the speculative demand evaporates. Allocate to modern TCG launches only with money the user can afford to lose; treat established trophy tiers (Yu-Gi-Oh LOB 1st Editions, Power Nine Alphas, Charizard 1st Ed Shadowless) as the asset-class core.
