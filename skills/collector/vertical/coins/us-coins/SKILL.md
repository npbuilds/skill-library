# US Coins — Sheldon Scale and the Canonical Series

> **Type:** Knowledge
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical (coins)
> **Parent:** coins

## The Major US Series

### Morgan Silver Dollars (1878-1904, 1921)

The most-collected US series. Large size (38.1mm), pure design (Liberty obverse, eagle reverse), wide date/mint mark variety. Trophy issues:

- **1893-S** — key date; PCGS MS-65 trades $100K+
- **1879-CC** — Carson City mint; major key
- **1889-CC** — Carson City; multi-thousand at any grade
- **1895** "King of Morgans" Proof — extremely rare
- **1878 8 Tail Feathers, 7/8 TF varieties** — first-year varieties

### Lincoln Cents (1909-present)

The most-collected coin in the US by volume. Trophy issues:

- **1909-S VDB** — key date; first year with designer's initials
- **1955 Doubled Die Obverse** — variety; not a date key
- **1969-S Doubled Die Obverse** — variety
- **1922 Plain** — key date variety (no D mintmark on Denver coin)
- **1914-D** — semi-key

For Lincoln cents, the **color designation** is critical:

- **RD (Red)** — full original copper color; premium tier
- **RB (Red-Brown)** — partial color preservation
- **BN (Brown)** — fully toned to brown; lowest color tier

A 1909-S VDB in MS-65 RD trades $20K+; in MS-65 BN trades a fraction.

### Walking Liberty Half Dollars (1916-1947)

The Adolph Weinman design. Trophy issues:

- **1916, 1916-D** — first-year keys
- **1917-S** Obverse mintmark variety
- **1921** — key date in any grade
- **1921-D, 1921-S** — three-year set with the 1921

**Full Bell Lines (FBL)** is the strike designation: the parallel lines on the Liberty Bell on the reverse must be complete and sharp. FBL premium varies but can be 50-200% over non-FBL examples.

### Other Major Series

- **Indian Head Pennies (1859-1909)** — key dates 1877, 1909-S
- **Buffalo Nickels (1913-1938)** — key dates 1918/7-D overdate, 1937-D 3-Legged variety
  - **Full Steps (FS)** designation on Jefferson nickels (different series)
- **Standing Liberty Quarters (1916-1930)** — key 1916; **Full Head (FH)** designation
- **Mercury Dimes (1916-1945)** — key dates 1916-D
- **Liberty / Indian Head $5 / $10 / $20 gold coins** — large category with substantial premiums

## Mint Marks

- **(no mark)** = Philadelphia (P sometimes used for modern)
- **D** = Denver
- **S** = San Francisco
- **CC** = Carson City (closed 1893; CC-marked coins are trophy)
- **O** = New Orleans (closed 1909; O-marked coins are trophy era)
- **W** = West Point (modern; bullion and special issues)

Mint marks are located on different parts of the coin for different series. For most US coins, the mark is on the reverse; for Morgan dollars specifically, it's below the eagle.

## Strike Designations (Specific to Series)

| Designation | Applies To | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **FB / FBL** (Full Bell Lines) | Franklin half dollars | Lower bell lines sharp and complete |
| **FH** (Full Head) | Standing Liberty quarters | Full hair detail on Liberty's head |
| **FS** (Full Steps) | Jefferson nickels | All steps on Monticello reverse visible |
| **FBL** (Full Bell Lines) | Walking Liberty halves | Bell-line detail clear |
| **FT** (Full Torch) | Roosevelt dimes | Torch design fully struck |

Strike designations indicate **complete striking** of design elements that often appear weak even on uncirculated coins. A non-FH Standing Liberty quarter and a FH version of the same date/grade can have multi-thousand-dollar spreads.

## Proof Designations

- **PF (or PR) 60-70** — same scale as MS, applied to proof coins
- **CAM (Cameo)** — proof coin with frosted devices contrasting with mirrored fields
- **DCAM (Deep Cameo)** — strongly contrasted cameo; premium tier
- **No designation** — modern proof with both fields and devices in similar reflectivity

A PF-69 DCAM 1955 Lincoln Cent Proof trades multiple times the same coin without DCAM designation.

Connoisseur ─── The Sheldon Scale Rewards the Sharpness of the Eye

A trained numismatist can tell MS-65 from MS-66 by examining the highest-relief points of a coin under good light — the difference may be three or four tiny surface marks that the eye registers as "softer" or "sharper." This precision separates working professionals from beginners. The discipline takes thousands of hours of looking at coins in person; books and online photographs are pale substitutes for hand-on-coin time.

Allocator ─── The Trophy Tier of US Coins Has Compounded 5-10% Nominal Over Multi-Decade Holds

Major-key US coins in trophy grades (PCGS MS-65 1909-S VDB, MS-66 1916-D Mercury, MS-66 1893-S Morgan) have compounded at roughly 5-10% nominal over 20-50 year holds. This is lower than equities pre-friction; comparable to bonds after-friction. The collector who builds a trophy-tier US coin collection over decades participates in an asset class with mature pricing data, well-codified condition, and stable cultural demand. The IRR will not beat the S&P; it will offer diversification and inflation hedging with relatively modest friction.
