# World Coins — Beyond the US Series

> **Type:** Knowledge
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical (coins)
> **Parent:** coins

## Major World Series

### British Coins

- **Gold Sovereigns** — issued since 1817; 7.32g of pure gold; circulated and bullion roles
- **Crowns** — large silver dollars; Victoria, Edward VII, George V designs
- **Pennies** — pre-decimal (pre-1971); large copper coins; date sets from Victoria era
- **Trophy issues**: 1839 "Una and the Lion" Five Pound, 1937 Edward VIII abdication coins (extremely rare)

### European

- **German thalers** — pre-Unification states; thousands of varieties from individual German states
- **French écus, louis d'or** — pre-Revolution and Restoration era
- **Italian states pre-Unification** — Papal States, Tuscany, Naples, Sardinia, Lombardy-Venetia
- **Swiss shooting thalers (Schützentaler)** — commemorative issues
- **Russian roubles and trophy gold (e.g., 1825 Konstantin rouble)** — rare imperial issues

### Asian

- **Chinese cash and trade coins** — pre-1949 imperial and Republic era
- **Japanese gold koban, oban** — Edo-period gold; extremely rare in high grade
- **Korean trade coins** — Joseon and Empire era
- **Modern Chinese commemoratives** — 1980s onward; specialized collector market

### Ancient

- **Greek silver and electrum** — Athenian tetradrachms, Aegina turtles, Syracuse decadrachms
- **Roman gold (aureus, solidus)** — major emperor portraits
- **Roman silver denarius and sestertius** — wider availability
- **Byzantine gold solidus and electrum trachy** — late antiquity through Byzantine era

## Authentication for World Coins

World coins face counterfeit risks that vary by category:

- **Ancient coins** — modern counterfeits are common; provenance through major dealers (Numismatica Ars Classica, Stack's Bowers Ancients, Heritage Ancients, CNG Coins) is essential
- **Imperial Russian gold** — high counterfeit rate; demand modern slabbing
- **Modern Chinese commemoratives** — sophisticated counterfeits from same era; grading services and dealer reputation matter
- **British vintage** — established market with low counterfeit rate but watch for restoration on gold

The major grading services (PCGS, NGC) grade world coins on the Sheldon scale. NGC Ancients uses a modified system that incorporates strike and surface quality.

## Grading World Coins

Most world series are graded on the Sheldon scale by PCGS and NGC. Specific adaptations:

- **NGC Ancients** — uses Sheldon scale with additional strike/surface notations specific to ancient coins
- **Strike quality** — for ancient and medieval coins, the manufacturing process produced variable striking; strike is part of the grade
- **Surface quality** — ancient coins often have surface deposits, light cleaning, or environmental damage that affects grade

## Major Venues

- **Heritage Auctions World & Ancient Coins** — major US venue
- **Stack's Bowers Galleries** — major US auctions; New York and California
- **Stephen Album Rare Coins** (now Album Auctions) — California; Islamic, Asian, ancient specialty
- **Classical Numismatic Group (CNG)** — Pennsylvania; major ancient coins specialist
- **Numismatica Ars Classica (NAC)** — Zurich; top-tier ancients
- **Roma Numismatics** — London; ancients; significant 2024 fraud concerns and ongoing litigation
- **Künker** — Germany; major European auctions
- **Spink** — London; British numismatic specialty
- **Baldwin's of St James** — London

## Common Mistakes in World Coins

1. **Buying ancients without strong provenance** — antiquities law and authenticity both depend on pre-1970 provenance for many objects
2. **Treating world coin Sheldon grades as identical to US** — service-specific tolerances vary by series and era
3. **Underestimating cultural property restrictions** — some countries restrict exports of pre-modern coins; international shipping requires compliance
4. **Overpaying on modern commemoratives** — the Chinese / Russian / Asian modern commemorative market has substantial speculation and counterfeit risk

Connoisseur ─── The Joy of World Coins Is the Scope of Human History

A 5th-century BC Athenian owl tetradrachm in your hand is the same object that paid for the Parthenon's construction. A 1709 Russian rouble was struck during Peter the Great's modernization. A 1933 South African Union pound represents the apex of British Empire numismatics. World coins are a kind of three-dimensional history — each piece carries the economic and political logic of its moment. The category rewards historical curiosity in a way that single-series collecting does not.

Allocator ─── Ancients Require Provenance Discipline; Modern Asian Has Speculation Risk

Ancient coin holdings benefit from pre-1970 documented provenance for both legal and authenticity reasons; without it, future sale options are limited and museum acquisition pathways closed. Modern Chinese / Russian / Asian commemoratives have experienced significant speculation cycles with subsequent corrections; treat these as discretionary collecting rather than core allocation. The European and British classical numismatic markets are mature with well-codified pricing and stable demand.
