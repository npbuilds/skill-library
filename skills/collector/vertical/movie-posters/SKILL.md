# Movie Posters — One-Sheets and Linen-Backing

> **Type:** Knowledge (Tier 2 standalone)
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical
> **Parent:** collector

## Format Vocabulary

US movie poster sizes (each format is a separate collectible):

| Format | Dimensions | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **One-sheet** | 27×41" (modern: 27×40") | The canonical US theatrical poster |
| **Half-sheet** | 22×28" | Smaller theatrical format |
| **Insert** | 14×36" | Vertical narrow format |
| **Three-sheet** | 41×81" (multi-panel) | Lobby display; large |
| **Six-sheet** | 81×81" (multi-panel) | Theater exterior |
| **24-sheet** (billboard) | 246×108" | Outdoor advertising |
| **Lobby card** | 11×14" | Small cards in sets of 8; theater lobby displays |

International formats add complexity:

- **British quad** — 30×40" horizontal
- **French grande** — 47×63"
- **Italian due foglio** — 39×55"
- **Japanese B2** — 20×29" or B1 — 28×40"

Each country's posters for the same film are separate collectible markets.

## The Condition Scale (C1-C10)

Jon Warren's *Warren's Movie Poster Price Guide* established the **C1-C10 scale**:

- **C10** — Mint; essentially new
- **C9** — Near Mint; minor flaws
- **C8** — Very Fine; light wear
- **C7** — Fine
- **C6** — Very Good
- **C5** — Good
- **C4** — Fair
- **C3** — Poor (significant damage)
- **C2-C1** — Very Poor

Most theatrically-distributed posters were folded for shipping (US tradition) or rolled (later years and many international). The fold lines themselves are not typically a major condition issue; **separation along folds** is. The discipline:

- Check for fold separations
- Check for tape stains (period-original tape repairs leave permanent marks)
- Check for pin holes (theater display nails)
- Check for water damage

## Linen-Backing

Linen-backing is the canonical conservation method for vintage movie posters:

- The paper poster is mounted on an acid-free Japanese-tissue + linen backing
- The mounting is **reversible** in principle (proper linen-backing uses water-soluble adhesives)
- Damage is **conservatively repaired** during backing (small fills, restored colors disclosed)
- Linen-backing can add 10-30% to a vintage poster's value by improving handling/display while protecting the original

A poorly done backing (with non-reversible adhesives, aggressive restoration) is a major value penalty. Use only experienced poster conservators (Heritage's recommended conservators, specialist vintage poster restorers).

## Style Variations

Many films have **multiple poster styles** for the same release:

- **Style A / Style B / Style C** designations
- **Advance** (pre-release) vs **release** posters
- **Recall** posters (occasionally pulled from circulation and rare)
- **Re-release** posters (later theatrical runs; lower tier than original)

The discipline: identify whether a poster is original release or re-release; consult major-house catalogs for specific film's poster history.

## Authentication

Movie posters face counterfeit risks:

- **Reproductions** sold as originals — modern reprints on aged paper
- **Restored areas presented as original** — color touch-ups, lining repairs that obscure damage
- **Mis-identified release year** — re-release sold as original

Authentication relies on:

- **Period-appropriate paper stock** — vintage posters have specific paper characteristics
- **Period-appropriate printing techniques** — offset vs lithograph differences
- **Period-appropriate fold patterns and folds** — original distribution folding leaves specific marks
- **Major-house cataloging** — Heritage's expertise in vintage movie posters provides institutional authentication

Connoisseur ─── The Movie Poster Is a Vanishing Artifact

Theatrical movie posters were designed to be displayed for two weeks, then thrown out. The poster department at every theater destroyed them after the film's run — they had no shelf space for old posters and no idea anyone would want them. The vintage posters that survive escaped this fate through accident or specific theaters' employees who saved them. A 1933 King Kong one-sheet in clean condition exists because some unknown theater worker in 1933 took it home rather than throwing it away. The collector who owns a clean vintage one-sheet owns the result of a random act of preservation 90 years ago.

Allocator ─── The Trophy Tier Has Compounded Significantly; Mid-Tier Is Volume Trade

Universal Classic Monsters one-sheets in good condition have compounded from $5-20K in 1990 to $100-500K+ in 2024 — strong multi-decade appreciation. The mid-tier (1950s-1970s posters by major directors in C7-C8 condition) trades in $1-10K range and compounds modestly. The lower tier (modern posters, re-releases, mass-produced styles) trades as decorative items rather than asset class. Allocate to the trophy tier with documented provenance and linen-backed conservation when appropriate.
