# Sealed Video Games — WATA, Seal Ratings, and the Modern Collectible

> **Type:** Knowledge (Tier 2 standalone)
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical
> **Parent:** collector

## The Grading Services

| Service | Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **WATA Games** | Sealed and loose; modern dominant | Founded 2018; rapidly became market leader |
| **VGA (Video Game Authority)** | Sealed; older service | Founded 2008; perceived as stricter on older games |
| **CGC Video Games** | Sealed and loose; newer entrant | CGC's expansion into video games (mid-2020s) |

Each uses a 1-10 scale for the game itself **plus** a separate seal rating for the shrinkwrap.

### Seal Rating System

For sealed games, the seal quality is graded separately:

- **A++** — perfect seal; trophy tier
- **A+** — near-perfect seal
- **A** — minor seal wear
- **B / B+** — moderate seal wear
- **C** — significant seal wear or partial detachment

A WATA 9.8 A++ is the canonical top-tier label that drove 2020-2021 record sales. The trophy designation combines high game-grade (9.8 of 10) with perfect seal (A++).

## The 2020-2021 Bubble

Sealed retro video games experienced one of the most dramatic speculation cycles in collectibles history:

- **April 2021**: Sealed Super Mario 64 (WATA 9.8 A++) — $1.56M (Heritage Auctions)
- **August 2020**: Sealed Super Mario Bros. — $114K, then $660K within months
- **July 2021**: Sealed Super Mario 64 — $1.56M
- **April 2021**: Sealed Legend of Zelda — $870K
- Multiple Nintendo titles broke six-figure marks in 2020-2021

The price drivers:

- COVID-era nostalgia and wealth deployment
- Crypto-wealth allocation to alternative assets
- Social media amplification (YouTube collecting channels)
- Newly-formed grading service infrastructure
- Heritage Auctions dominance as the venue
- Industry concerns about wash trading / non-arm's-length transactions (Karl Jobst's documentary investigation raised questions about the bubble's price discovery)

## The Correction

Post-2022, sealed retro video games experienced significant correction:

- The Super Mario 64 trophy tier softened materially
- Mid-tier sealed games (PS1, N64, SNES non-trophy titles) corrected 50-70% from peaks
- The category remains volatile

The lesson: sealed retro video games are a relatively young collectible category with limited multi-cycle data. Trophy items (sealed Nintendo Famicom-era classics, sealed original Atari, sealed first-generation pre-Crash titles) have established collector interest; speculation-tier modern sealed games (post-2000) are more volatile.

## Trophy Categories

- **Sealed original NES titles** (1985-1988 first-print titles)
- **Sealed first-print Super Mario Bros., Legend of Zelda, Metroid** — premier WATA-eligible items
- **Sealed Famicom-era Japanese games** — original 1980s Japan releases
- **Sealed pre-Crash Atari** (1977-1983) — Adventure, Asteroids, Combat
- **Sealed first-print Super Nintendo titles**
- **Sealed first-print Sega Master System, Genesis** — secondary platform
- **Sealed pre-2000 PC games** — original Sierra / LucasArts adventures

## Authentication

For sealed games, authentication is primarily:

- **Original shrinkwrap** — period-appropriate wrap material; correct shrink pattern
- **Sticker / stamp date codes** — first-print indicators
- **Internal manual content** (when not opened) — verified via X-ray analysis at WATA
- **Period-appropriate corner cuts, hangtabs, box variations**

Counterfeit sealed games exist — resealed boxes are sophisticated. Major grading services use X-ray and other forensic tools to detect resealed product.

## Loose vs Sealed

Loose retro games (unsealed cartridges, sometimes with original box and manual) trade in a separate market from sealed:

- **CIB (Complete in Box)** — cartridge, manual, box, all original — premium loose tier
- **Cart only** — cartridge alone; commodity tier
- **Manual / box only** — accessories; smaller market

Sealed trades 5-50× CIB depending on title and grading. CIB trades 2-5× cart only.

## Venues

- **Heritage Auctions** — dominant; weekly Heritage Video Games auctions plus signature events
- **Goldin** — recent entrant; sports cards crossover
- **eBay** — retail; lower tier and volume
- **WATAcollect** — WATA's own marketplace (though Heritage handles most major auctions)
- **Specialist dealers** — small specialty channels for sealed and CIB

Connoisseur ─── A Sealed First-Print Nintendo Game Is a Physical Time Capsule

A sealed 1986 Legend of Zelda is the box of a 1986 child's Christmas — the wrap is the wrap that came off the FedEx truck to Nintendo's distribution, the cartridge is the cartridge that the original buyer's parents brought home from Toys R Us. The shrinkwrap holds 38 years of stillness. The trained collector understands what survived — original wrap, original sticker placements, original box corner-cuts — and what didn't. Most copies were opened and played in 1986; a sealed survivor is an unlikely artifact of how mass-produced items achieve scarcity over decades.

Allocator ─── Treat Sealed Video Games as Speculative Until Multi-Cycle Data Exists

Sealed retro games are a young asset class (WATA founded 2018). The 2020-2022 cycle showed extreme volatility — Super Mario 64 went from $20K (2019) to $1.56M (2021) and corrected. Multi-decade price stability is not established. Allocate cautiously; treat as speculation rather than core collecting; prefer Nintendo first-prints (1985-1995) with WATA 9.8+ A++ provenance from major-house auctions; expect continued cycle volatility.
