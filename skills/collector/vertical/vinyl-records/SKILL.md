# Vinyl Records — Goldmine, Runout, and First Pressings

> **Type:** Knowledge (Tier 2 standalone)
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical
> **Parent:** collector

## Goldmine Grading Standard

The universal vinyl condition vocabulary (used by Discogs, dealers globally):

- **M (Mint)** — sealed only; never opened
- **NM / M- (Near Mint)** — appears unplayed; pristine
- **VG+ (Very Good Plus)** — minor signs of use; plays without distraction
- **VG (Very Good)** — visible wear; audible surface noise
- **G+ (Good Plus)** — significant wear; noise present
- **G (Good)** — playable but worn
- **F (Fair)** — significant damage
- **P (Poor)** — barely playable

Disc and sleeve graded separately ("NM/VG+" = disc NM, sleeve VG+).

## Matrix / Runout Numbers — The First-Pressing Tell

The numbers and letters etched into the **dead wax** (the run-out groove between the last song and the label) identify the specific stamper, mother, and lacquer used to produce the record.

For collectors hunting first pressings, the runout is the canonical authentication:

- **First-press numbers** are sequential and specific to the first production batch
- **Engineer initials** are often etched in the runout (Bob Ludwig's "RL" on Led Zeppelin II; "Porky" / "Pecko" for British engineers Porky Peckham and George Peckham)
- **Label design alone is insufficient** — labels were reused across pressings

### Examples

- **Led Zeppelin II "RL" pressing** — the original Bob Ludwig master had bass-heavy mixing; later pressings (with different engineer initials) sound different. The "RL" pressing trades at premium multiples.
- **The Beatles UK first pressings** — distinguished by specific Parlophone runout codes (e.g., "XEX 421" series matrix numbers for Sgt. Pepper)
- **Pink Floyd "Dark Side of the Moon"** UK first pressings vs US later — both genuine, vastly different sonics and values

## First Pressings by Country

For most rock and jazz collectors:

- **UK first pressings** of Beatles, Stones, Pink Floyd, Led Zeppelin — premium tier (artist's home country, original mastering)
- **US first pressings** of Beatles — separate Capitol-era market; different sonics from UK
- **Japanese pressings** — particularly for jazz (Blue Note classics), audiophile-grade with premium for original Toshiba/King Records pressings
- **German pressings (Polydor, Deutsche Grammophon)** — premium classical pressings

For classical music collectors, **Decca and Mercury Living Presence** original UK/US pressings are trophy tier.

## Audiophile Reissues

A separate market parallel to vintage first pressings:

- **Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab (MoFi)** — half-speed mastered reissues; premium audiophile-grade
- **Analogue Productions / Acoustic Sounds** — Chad Kassem's label; 200g vinyl, original master tape sources
- **Classic Records** — discontinued but holdings trade in collector market
- **Music Matters Jazz** — Blue Note reissues
- **MoFi One-Steps** — highest-tier MoFi production; limited edition; major premium

Audiophile reissues are not first pressings but a parallel collector market. A MoFi pressing of "Kind of Blue" is **not the same asset** as a Blue Note 1959 first pressing; both are collectible, both serve different collectors.

## Trophy Original Pressings

For rock:

- **The Beatles** — UK Parlophone first pressings of Please Please Me, A Hard Day's Night, Rubber Soul, Revolver, Sgt. Pepper, White Album, Abbey Road
- **The Rolling Stones** — original UK Decca pressings
- **Led Zeppelin** — "RL" pressing of Led Zeppelin II particularly
- **Pink Floyd** — UK first of Dark Side; UK first of The Wall
- **Bob Dylan** — original Columbia/CBS pressings; the rare "freewheelin' Bob Dylan" with the four extra-tracks cover

For jazz:

- **Blue Note 1500/4000 series** in original mono — Coltrane, Hancock, Davis, Monk
- **Prestige original pressings** — Coltrane, Davis, Rollins
- **Impulse! original pressings** — Coltrane (A Love Supreme), Pharoah Sanders

For classical:

- **Decca SXL series** — early stereo classical
- **Mercury Living Presence** — original mono and stereo
- **RCA Living Stereo** — original RCA shaded dog pressings

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Buying based on label design alone** — labels were reused across pressings; the matrix number is the foundation
2. **Confusing audiophile reissues with first pressings** — both are collectible, but they are not the same asset
3. **Ignoring "play-graded" vs "visually-graded"** — many NM-looking records have audible surface noise; play-grading discipline matters for serious listening
4. **Buying without examining the dead wax** — the matrix/runout is the authentication; photographs of the matrix are increasingly expected on high-value sales
5. **Overpaying on common pressings** — most major-artist records exist in vast quantities; only specific first pressings carry premium

## Venues

- **Discogs** — dominant marketplace and database
- **eBay** — high volume; signal-to-noise variable
- **Record fairs** — local and regional; primary discovery channel for many collectors
- **Specialist dealers** — Acoustic Sounds, Music Direct, dedicated vintage shops
- **Heritage Auctions** — high-end music collectibles including trophy vinyl

Connoisseur ─── The Dead Wax Is the Record's Birth Certificate

The matrix etching in the dead wax tells you which specific lacquer cut the record. A label saying "Capitol 1965" doesn't tell you whether you have a first pressing, a 1972 reissue, or a 1995 budget reissue. The dead wax does. The discipline of reading the runout — for every record above $50 in price — is foundational. Every serious vinyl collector has a magnifying glass, a strong light, and a reference book or website for cross-checking matrix numbers against known first pressings.

Allocator ─── First Pressings Compound Modestly; Audiophile Tier Trades Sideways

A Beatles UK first-press Sgt. Pepper in NM condition was $500 in 2000, $1,500 in 2015, $2,000-3,000 in 2024 — modest compounding compared to other collectibles. The trophy tier of vinyl (Blue Note jazz firsts, Beatles UK firsts, certain Pink Floyd / Led Zeppelin firsts) is established but not dramatically appreciating. Audiophile reissues trade more like consumer products — the latest MoFi One-Step is in demand at release, then settles. Allocate to vinyl primarily as consumption asset (the listening pleasure is the return); modest investment return for the trophy tier.
