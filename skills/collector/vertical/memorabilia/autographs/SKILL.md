# Autographs — The Authentication Discipline

> **Type:** Knowledge
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical (memorabilia)
> **Parent:** memorabilia

## The Major Authenticators

| Service | Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PSA/DNA** | Sports + entertainment + historical | Largest; deepest exemplar database; witnessed programs at major events |
| **JSA (James Spence Authentication)** | Sports + entertainment + historical | Strong reputation; founded by James Spence Jr. (formerly with PSA) |
| **BAS (Beckett Authentication Services)** | Sports + entertainment | Beckett brand; broad coverage |
| **Fanatics Authentic** | Current sports stars | Direct-from-athlete; hologram-tracked; dominant for active players |

For items above ~$500 in value, authentication from one of the Big Three is the market standard. Items without major-body authentication trade at significant discount and may be unsellable at scale.

## Witnessed vs Opinion-Based

### Witnessed Authentication

The authenticator was physically present when the signature was given. Examples:

- PSA/DNA on-site at a signing event (sports figure at a baseball card show)
- JSA witnessed signing at a corporate event
- Direct-from-athlete signings with the athlete's authentication partner present

A witnessed signature carries the strongest provenance — there is no question whether the subject signed. The authenticator can attest from direct observation.

### Opinion-Based Authentication

The authenticator examines the signature after the fact and compares to known exemplars (other signatures by the subject in the authenticator's database). The opinion is based on:

- Stroke patterns (pressure, speed, direction)
- Letter formation specific to the subject
- Period appropriateness (Ruth signed differently in 1925 vs 1945)
- Ink/paper analysis when applicable
- Overall consistency with database exemplars

Opinion-based authentication is less certain than witnessed. It is also the only option for deceased subjects or for vintage items signed before authentication programs existed.

For frequently-forged subjects (Mantle, Ruth, Cobb, Jackson, Wagner, Bonds), opinion-based authentication has been wrong in documented cases. The Operation Bullpen FBI investigation (late 1990s-2000s) revealed industrial-scale forgery operations producing fake Ruth, Mantle, and Cobb signatures that initially passed authentication. The investigation led to significant tightening of authentication standards.

## Babe Ruth Signature Evolution

The canonical example of period-appropriateness. Ruth's signature changed over his lifetime:

- **Early career (1914-1920)** — full "George Herman Ruth" or "Babe Ruth" with specific letter formations
- **Yankee era (1920-1934)** — established "Babe Ruth" signature; consistent letter formations
- **Post-career (1935-1948)** — signature deteriorated as Ruth's health declined
- **Pre-death (1947-1948)** — shaky, often illegible; rare and valued period

A "1925 Babe Ruth signature" should match Yankee-era characteristics, not late-career characteristics. Period mismatch is a major red flag.

## The Counterfeit-Prone Tier

The signatures most-counterfeited in the secondary market:

- **Babe Ruth** — the canonical target; industrial-scale forgery exists
- **Mickey Mantle** — extensive forgery; many items in the secondary market without proper authentication are fake
- **Lou Gehrig** — rarely-signed during lifetime; high counterfeit ratio
- **Joe Jackson** — Jackson was illiterate; "Shoeless Joe" signed items are extremely rare and almost all "Jackson autographs" in the market are fake
- **Honus Wagner** — heavily forged
- **Ty Cobb** — heavily forged
- **Walter Johnson, Christy Mathewson** — pre-modern era; high forgery rate

For these subjects, **only major-body authentication should be trusted, and even then with care**. Cross-referencing with the major-body's exemplar database (some are public; PSA/DNA Auto facts is a good starting point) is the discipline.

## Modern Sports Autographs

For currently-active or recently-active athletes (Tom Brady, Patrick Mahomes, LeBron James, Mike Trout, Aaron Judge), **Fanatics Authentic** is the dominant authentication brand:

- Athletes sign on Fanatics-organized private signings
- Each item is hologram-tagged at signing
- The hologram can be verified online via Fanatics' database
- Pricing is set by Fanatics and reflects supply control

A Fanatics-authenticated Brady jersey signed at a private signing event with Fanatics observation is the modern standard for current-player autographs.

## Inscriptions and Statistical Signing

A signed item may include additional **inscriptions** — statistics, dedications, dates, locations. Inscriptions add value if the inscription is meaningful:

- **"HOF '95"** or **"3,000 Hit Club"** — career achievements that add cultural significance
- **Personal inscription** ("To John, Best Wishes, Mickey") — adds emotional but rarely financial value over a clean signature
- **Multi-signature panels** (team signed balls, locker room photos) — value depends on roster significance

Major signers sometimes do "stat days" where they sign items with specific inscriptions. PSA/DNA tracks these events.

## Historical (Non-Sports) Autographs

The broader autograph market extends well beyond sports:

- **Presidents** — Lincoln, Washington (extremely rare and valuable); modern presidents (Reagan, Obama, etc.) more common
- **Founders and Revolutionary figures** — Hamilton, Jefferson, Franklin
- **Authors** — Hemingway, Steinbeck, Twain
- **Musicians** — Beatles members (extensively forged), Hendrix, Bowie
- **Film stars** — golden-age Hollywood (Bogart, Marilyn Monroe — heavily forged)
- **Scientists** — Einstein (heavily forged), Edison
- **Notable historical figures** — Napoleon, Churchill, Hitler-era ephemera (controversial; ethically sensitive)

PSA/DNA and JSA cover historical autographs but with different exemplar depth than sports.

## Major Venues

- **Heritage Auctions** — broad coverage across sports and entertainment
- **Goldin** — sports memorabilia and autographs; weekly auctions
- **Lelands** — sports memorabilia; high-end
- **Robert Edward Auctions (REA)** — sports; vintage focus
- **Hunt Auctions** — sports memorabilia
- **Sotheby's, Christie's** — major historical autographs; presidential, literary

---

Connoisseur ─── Trust the Authentication; Verify the Subject

The discipline of autograph collecting is to trust the major-body authentication for living markets and to verify period-appropriateness against published exemplars for vintage. A Babe Ruth signature should look like Babe Ruth in his Yankee era for a 1925 ball; like the late-career shaky version for a 1947 ball. The user who internalizes signature evolution and trusts only PSA/DNA, JSA, and BAS is buying authentic items at market prices; the user who trusts unknown COAs is buying decorations at market prices for fakes.

Allocator ─── Authenticated Vintage Sports Autographs Have Trophy Tiers

A PSA/DNA-authenticated single-signed Babe Ruth baseball in good condition trades $20K-$80K depending on inscription and provenance. A Mickey Mantle PSA/DNA-authenticated jersey trades $5K-$50K depending on era and condition. The trophy tier compounds with provenance — a Ruth ball with documented family-of-recipient provenance from a 1925 signing event trades multiples of a Ruth ball with no provenance even at the same authentication tier. The Big Three authentication is the floor; provenance adds the ceiling.
