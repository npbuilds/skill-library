# Stamps & Philately — A Mature, Codified Discipline

> **Type:** Knowledge (Tier 2 standalone)
> **Suite:** The Collector
> **Axis:** Vertical
> **Parent:** collector

## The Major Catalogs

Each major philatelic market uses a national catalog as its pricing and identification reference:

- **Scott Standard Postage Stamp Catalogue** — US-dominant; numbering used in American philately
- **Stanley Gibbons** — UK and Commonwealth dominant; "SG" numbers
- **Michel** — Germany / Central Europe; "Mi." numbers
- **Yvert et Tellier** — France and Francophone; "Y&T" numbers

For any cataloged stamp, the catalog number is the foundation of identification.

## Condition Vocabulary

The gum and hinging status dominates value for **mint** stamps:

- **MNH (Mint Never Hinged)** — original gum, never hinged; the premium tier
- **MNH-OG (Original Gum, Never Hinged)** — same as MNH; explicit
- **MH / H (Mint Hinged)** — original gum with hinge remnant; major value penalty
- **NH (Never Hinged)** — implies MNH but should be confirmed with **OG**
- **OG (Original Gum)** — gum is original, not regummed
- **NG (No Gum)** — gum missing
- **Regummed (RG)** — gum added later to deceive; major fraud concern

For **used** stamps, condition is graded by:

- **Cancellation quality** — light strike, off-center, "socked on the nose"
- **Centering** — design centered within perforations
- **Color preservation** — original color without fading
- **Perforation completeness**

### Grade Designations

Within each MNH or used category:

- **Superb** — perfect centering, full perforations, fresh color
- **Extremely Fine (XF/EF)** — near-perfect
- **Very Fine (VF)** — collector standard
- **Fine (F)** — modest centering issues
- **Average** — below collector standard

Some catalogs and graders use **PSE 70-100 numeric scale** as an additional precision layer.

## Expertization

For high-value stamps, formal expertization is the authentication standard:

- **Philatelic Foundation (PF)** — major US expertization body; founded 1945
- **Professional Stamp Experts (PSE)** — major US expertization
- **British Philatelic Association (BPA)** — UK expertization
- **Italian and German expertization committees** — national bodies for their stamps

An expertization certificate accompanies the stamp and includes:

- Catalog identification
- Condition assessment
- Grade designation
- Specific notes on faults, repairs, or alterations
- Signed and dated by the expert(izing body)

Cost of expertization: typically $25-150 depending on the stamp's value and complexity. For any stamp above $500 in expected value, expertization is standard.

## The Trophy World Rarities

### The Top Tier

- **British Guiana 1c Magenta** (1856) — unique known surviving example; sold for $9.48M (Sotheby's 2014); resold for ~$8.3M (Sotheby's 2021); the most-famous stamp
- **Inverted Jenny (US C3a, 1918)** — 100 sheets printed with inverted center airplane image; one example trades $1.5M+ in major condition
- **Treskilling Yellow (Sweden 1855)** — single surviving example; multi-million
- **Mauritius Post Office 1d & 2d (1847)** — first British colonial stamps; multi-million
- **Penny Black (UK 1840)** — first adhesive postage stamp; high-grade examples carry premium

### Major Country Rarities

- **US Confederate States issues** — limited civilian-era issues
- **Hawaiian Missionaries (1851-1853)** — very rare US territorial
- **Russian zemstvos** — district-issued local stamps; major specialty
- **Tibet, Bhutan, and other obscure issuing entities** — major specialty markets

## Errors and Varieties

Stamp errors trade at significant premiums:

- **Inverted designs** — Inverted Jenny is canonical; multiple country examples
- **Color shifts and color errors** — wrong color used for known design
- **Missing perforations** — imperforate varieties when perforation was standard
- **Missing inscriptions** — "missing color" on multi-color designs

Each major error has its own price history and collector community.

## Perfins

A specialist subfield: stamps with private corporate perforations (initials punched through). Companies used perfins for accounting (preventing employee theft). Major perfin specialists maintain catalogs of corporate uses.

## Venues

- **Robert A. Siegel Auction Galleries** — major US philatelic auctioneer
- **Cherrystone Auctions** — NY-based
- **Spink** — London; major British and international
- **Heinrich Köhler** — Germany; major European philately
- **David Feldman** — Switzerland-based; international
- **Stanley Gibbons** — UK; both auction and direct sale
- **APS StampStore** — American Philatelic Society; member dealer aggregator
- **Heritage Auctions** — secondary philately presence; not primary specialty

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing regummed for MNH** — visual inspection of gum integrity is critical; expertization for high-value
2. **Buying used as "investment"** — most used stamps trade as collection-builders, not investment-grade
3. **Ignoring centering** — perfectly centered examples are dramatically rarer than typical
4. **Buying without expertization for $1K+ stamps** — the certificate is the authentication anchor

Connoisseur ─── Philately Is the Oldest Codified Hobby Discipline

Stamp collecting has been organized as a hobby since the 1860s. The catalogs, the expertization committees, the trade-body infrastructure, the collector vocabulary — all are older than most other collectible markets. The discipline rewards the patient student who learns one country or one era deeply, who collects with intention rather than completeness, and who participates in a community whose conventions span generations.

Allocator ─── Trophy Philately Has Compounded Modestly; Most Stamps Are Hobby Tier

The trophy tier (British Guiana 1c Magenta, Inverted Jenny, Penny Black high-grade) has compounded at rates comparable to other top-tier collectibles. The vast bulk of philatelic material — common-date used stamps, mass-produced commemoratives, modern issues — has minimal investment appreciation and serves as collection-building inventory rather than asset class. Allocate to trophy material with expertization certificates; treat the rest as hobby spending rather than investment.
