# Character Interiority — Quick Reference


## Quick Reference

| Instead of | Try |
|-----------|-----|
| She was afraid | Her hands went cold. Something pressed against her sternum, a weight with no source. |
| He felt guilty | The bread tasted like paper. He ate it anyway, methodically, not tasting. |
| She was angry | Her jaw ached. She'd been clenching it since — since when? Since the phone call. Maybe before that. |

## Diagnosing Interiority Problems

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| "Told" emotions — "She felt sad" | Staying at Level 1-2 when the moment needs 3-4 | Drop into FID. Render somatic sensation or perception filter, not the label. |
| Over-tagged thoughts — "she thought, she realized" | Too much indirect thought, not enough FID | Remove the tags. Let the character's sensibility bleed through without attribution. |
| Long italicized thought passages | Over-reliance on direct thought | Convert most to FID. Reserve italics for sharp, decisive internal speech. |
| Character has the author's intelligence | Author intrusion | Audit diction. Every word in FID must be one the character would use. |
| Interiority feels monotonous | Same psychic distance throughout | Create rhythm — close, then pull back, then close again. Vary the modes. |
| Reader can't tell what the character wants | Interiority is reactive but never proactive | Add desire and anticipation. Consciousness is not just a mirror; it's an engine. |
| Emotional scenes feel flat | Interiority is cognitive rather than experiential | Shift from head to body. Somatic emotion, perception filter, temporal distortion. |
