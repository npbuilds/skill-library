# Narrative Geometry — Extended Shapes & Sources

## Additional Shapes from Alison

**Network / Cells**: Honeycomb or cracked-field patterns where frames are nearly identical in design, one echoing another. Readers mentally connect disparate vignettes. Unity comes through repetition and resonance rather than causality. Example: Sebald's *The Emigrants* (four interconnected narratives unified through tonal color and a recurring "Butterfly Man" figure). Best for: fragmented narratives, mosaic portraits, difficult subjects.

**Tsunami**: Nested stories folded into one overarching narrative, creating a bookend structure. Example: David Mitchell's *Cloud Atlas*. Best for: epic, time-spanning narratives where stories contain stories.

## Alison's Microstructure vs. Macrostructure

Alison divides narrative design into **microstructures** (sentence-level patterns of sound, rhythm, color, and pacing) and **macrostructures** (the overall geometry). Her core principle: narrative patterns should mirror emotional experience. Someone spirals into despair; thoughts meander; rage can be so great we feel we'll explode. Form approximates the shape of experience.

She calls the traditional dramatic arc "a bit masculo-sexual" and proposes looking to patterns found in nature instead.

## Rasa Theory (Extended)

The nine rasas with their traditional associations:

| Rasa | Emotion | Color | Deity |
|------|---------|-------|-------|
| Shringara | Romance/Love | Dark/brown-black | Vishnu |
| Hasya | Laughter/Mirth | White | Shiva |
| Raudra | Fury | Red | Rudra |
| Karunya | Compassion | Grey | Yama |
| Bibhatsa | Disgust | Blue | Mahakala |
| Bhayanaka | Horror/Terror | Black | Kala |
| Vira | Heroism | Bright white | Indra |
| Adbhuta | Wonder | Yellow | Brahma |
| Shanta | Peace/Tranquility | Perpetual white | Vishnu |

Rasa is produced through: **Vibhava** (determinants/causes) + **Anubhava** (consequent expressions) + **Vyabhicharibhava** (transitory states) = Rasa (aesthetic flavor).

The goal is not catharsis through resolution but **transportation** — lifting the spectator toward ultimate reality. Requires a **sahridaya** ("one who has heart") — a prepared, sensitive receiver.

## Kishotenketsu Extended

Craft advice: assign each act a verb — *introduce, establish, reveal, conclude*. The "ten" turn is NOT necessarily a conflict — it can be a contextual revelation, an overlooked detail surfacing, a tonal shift. Narrative energy comes from contrast and surprise rather than opposition.

## Circular/Indigenous Narrative

Circular patterns reflect a worldview where time is cyclical and every ending is a beginning. Repetition enhances memory and intergenerational transmission in oral tradition. The form IS the philosophy: cycles of existence over linear progress. Functions as cultural sovereignty — reclaiming storytelling techniques that challenge colonial linear perspectives.

## Braided vs. Mosaic (Distinction)

- **Braided**: Initially separate storylines converge and add to the main arc by the end
- **Mosaic**: Self-contained stories only loosely connect, providing a composite view

Peter Kispert's craft principles: propulsion within each strand + eventual coalescence. Equal distribution of timelines prevents deadness. Successful braiding prioritizes meaning over pattern.
