# Narrative Geometry — Shapes Beyond the Arc

The dramatic arc is culturally specific and not always the right shape. Jane Alison's *Meander, Spiral, Explode* draws from nature to offer alternative structures. This skill provides a geometry toolkit for writers whose stories resist the arc.

Companion to `narrative-arc` (linear frameworks). This skill covers **non-linear and alternative shapes**.

## The Geometry Toolkit

### Meander
Wanders, doubles back, revisits. Path IS the point. Tangents are essential texture, not deviations. Practitioners: Sebald, Montaigne, Dyer. Best for: memoir, essay, contemplative fiction, grief, identity. Challenge: must feel purposeful — each digression deepens the central preoccupation.

### Spiral
Returns to the same point at different elevations of understanding. Each pass reveals new meaning. Practitioners: Garcia Marquez (*Chronicle of a Death Foretold*), O'Brien (*The Things They Carried*). Best for: trauma, obsession, mystery. Challenge: each revolution must earn existence — new knowledge on each return.

### Radial / Explosion
Radiates outward from a central event like ripples from a stone. Practitioners: Egan (*Goon Squad*), Denis Johnson. Best for: ensemble stories, ripple effects, community narratives. Challenge: the center must be strong enough to hold threads together.

### Fractal
Self-similar at every scale — scene mirrors chapter mirrors book. Practitioners: Ali Smith, Lispector. Best for: thematic coherence over plot momentum, obsessive/recursive themes. Challenge: pattern should be felt, not seen — subliminal coherence, not gimmick.

### Wavelet
Oscillating peaks and troughs without a single climax. Practitioners: Woolf (*To the Lighthouse*), Robinson (*Housekeeping*). Best for: long timeframes, relationships, emotional ebb and flow. Challenge: each wave needs its own tension; risk is flatness.

### Braid / Mosaic
Multiple threads interweave, connected by theme rather than plot. Practitioners: Mitchell (*Cloud Atlas*), Calvino. Best for: epic scope, thematic complexity, connection across time. Challenge: threads must feel like they belong together.

### Cycle
Returns to its beginning, transformed. The end IS the beginning seen differently. Practitioners: Joyce (*Finnegans Wake*), Morrison, Indigenous storytelling traditions. Best for: homecoming, cyclical time, the past's grip on the present.

## Non-Western Forms

**Kishotenketsu**: The "ten" (twist) is juxtaposition, not climax — closer to radial geometry than to the arc.

**Rasa Theory** (Indian *Natyashastra*): Narrative organized around evoking emotional flavors (*rasa*) rather than conflict escalation. Nine rasas: love, humor, fury, compassion, terror, heroism, disgust, wonder, peace. Structure serves emotional architecture.

**Le Guin's Carrier Bag Theory**: Story as container — gathers elements into meaningful relationship. No hero, no antagonist, no climax. A community, a landscape, relationships, and a shape that emerges from gathering rather than hunting.

## Choosing a Geometry

| Material | Shape |
|----------|-------|
| Journey with tangents | Meander |
| Trauma, obsession, returning | Spiral |
| Single event, many ripples | Radial |
| Pattern repeating at every scale | Fractal |
| Emotional ebb and flow | Wavelet |
| Multiple lives, one theme | Braid |
| Cyclical return | Cycle |
| Emotional flavors over conflict | Rasa |
| Community, ecosystem | Carrier bag |
| Conflict, stakes, climax | Arc (`narrative-arc`) |

## Relationship to Other Skills

- **Narrative Arc**: Companion for linear structure. Many works combine both — arc at one level, spiral at another.
- **Micro-Tension**: Alternative geometries need micro-tension *more* than arcs, lacking built-in rising-action momentum.
- **Pacing**: Operates within any geometry — meanders still need tempo variation.
- **Worldbuilding/history-builder**: Fractal zoom (Microscope RPG) is fractal geometry applied to timelines.
