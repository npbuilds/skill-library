# Diction Checklists — Revision-Ready

Use these checklists during the line-editing pass, after structural and rhythmic issues are resolved.

## Checklist 1: Vagueness Audit

Read each sentence and ask: **can the reader see, hear, or feel this?**

| Vague | Concrete Alternative | Principle |
|-------|---------------------|-----------|
| "She felt sad" | The specific detail that produces sadness in the reader | Show the concrete; trust the reader to feel |
| "It was a nice day" | "Seventy degrees, no wind, the kind of sky that made you forget you had a job" | Specific > general |
| "He walked into the room" | "He shouldered through the door" / "He drifted into the kitchen" | The verb carries the characterization |
| "things," "stuff," "aspects" | Name the actual things | Every vague noun is a missed opportunity |
| "very," "really," "extremely" | Cut entirely, or find a stronger base word | "Very tired" → "exhausted." Or just "tired." |

## Checklist 2: Register Consistency

Read the passage as a whole. Flag any word that doesn't match the prevailing register:

**Formal register violations:**
- Contractions in otherwise formal prose (unless intentional)
- Slang, colloquialisms, or casual intensifiers ("totally," "kinda," "super")
- Sentence fragments (in formal register, these read as errors, not style)

**Casual register violations:**
- Latinate vocabulary where Germanic alternatives exist ("facilitate" → "help," "utilize" → "use")
- Overly complex sentence structures (subordinate clause nesting)
- Jargon or technical language the audience wouldn't use

**The test**: Read the passage imagining it spoken aloud by the author/narrator. Does every word sound like something that person would actually say? If a word belongs to a different speaker, it's a register break.

## Checklist 3: Dead Language

Search and evaluate:

**Dead metaphors** (flag, then decide if they serve or just take up space):
- "level playing field," "thinking outside the box," "at the end of the day"
- "shed light on," "tip of the iceberg," "the elephant in the room"
- Test: Does the reader still *see* the image? If not, it's dead.

**Filler and hedge words** (cut unless the hedge is intentional):
- just, really, very, quite, rather, actually, basically, certainly, definitely
- "in order to" → "to"
- "due to the fact that" → "because"
- "at this point in time" → "now"
- "began to" / "started to" → just use the verb ("began to run" → "ran")

**Nominalization** (verbs turned into nouns, weakening the action):
- "made a decision" → "decided"
- "conducted an investigation" → "investigated"
- "gave a presentation" → "presented"

## Checklist 4: The Connotation Pass

For every key word (especially nouns and adjectives that carry evaluative weight), ask:

1. **What does this word denote?** (Dictionary meaning)
2. **What does it connote?** (Emotional shading, cultural associations)
3. **Is the connotation what I intend?**

Common connotation traps:
- "Cheap" vs "affordable" vs "thrifty" vs "frugal" vs "stingy"
- "Childish" vs "childlike"
- "Stubborn" vs "determined" vs "persistent"
- "Scheme" vs "plan" vs "strategy"
- "Claim" vs "state" vs "assert" vs "insist"

If you used a thesaurus to find the word, double-check its connotation. Thesauruses group by denotation and ignore connotation entirely.

## Checklist 5: The Anglo-Saxon Pass

For Latinate/formal words, ask: **is there a shorter, more direct alternative?**

| Latinate | Germanic Alternative | When Latinate is Better |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| ameliorate | improve | Almost never |
| commence | begin / start | Legal or ceremonial contexts |
| demonstrate | show | When precision matters ("demonstrate" implies proof) |
| facilitate | help / ease | Almost never |
| implement | do / carry out | Technical project management contexts |
| indicate | show / point to | Scientific writing where "indicate" carries statistical meaning |
| utilize | use | Almost never |
| terminate | end / stop | Legal, technical, or deliberately cold register |

**Remember Orwell's Rule 6**: If the Latinate word is genuinely more precise, or if the voice register requires it, use it. These checklists are defaults, not commandments.
