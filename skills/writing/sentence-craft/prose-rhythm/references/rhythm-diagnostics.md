# Rhythm Diagnostics — Step-by-Step Protocol

Use this protocol when prose "doesn't flow" and you need to identify the specific rhythm problem.

## Step 1: Map Sentence Lengths

Count the words in each sentence of the paragraph. Write the sequence:

```
Example: 24, 8, 22, 19, 21, 23
Diagnosis: The 8-word sentence is the only short one. The rest cluster around 20-24. Flat rhythm with one spike.
```

**What to look for:**
- All similar lengths → flat, monotonous
- All short → staccato, exhausting, choppy
- All long → trance-like, potentially exhausting, hard to parse
- Good variation → the passage has rhythmic shape

**Target**: No two adjacent sentences should be within 5 words of each other unless the similarity is deliberate (parallel structure, building repetition).

## Step 2: Check Emphasis Placement

Identify the most important word or image in each sentence. Where does it fall?

- **End of sentence** → strong (the period spotlights it)
- **Beginning of sentence** → moderately strong
- **Buried in the middle** → weak

If key words consistently land in the middle of sentences, the prose will feel like it's working hard but never arriving. Restructure to move emphasis to sentence-final position.

## Step 3: Listen for the Short Sentence

Short sentences (under 10 words) are emphasis tools. Check:
- Are there any? (If not, the prose may feel relentless — nowhere for the reader to breathe.)
- Where do they fall? (After long sentences = maximum impact. After other short sentences = diminishing returns.)
- Do they carry weight? (A short sentence that says something trivial wastes its power.)

## Step 4: Check Paragraph Shape

Does the paragraph have a rhythmic arc?

- **Opening sentence**: Sets the tempo. Medium-length openers feel neutral; long openers feel immersive; short openers feel urgent.
- **Body sentences**: Should create movement — building, developing, varying.
- **Closing sentence**: The strongest position. A short, emphatic closer lands hard. A long, rolling closer creates a sense of completion.

The weakest paragraph shape is uniform: all sentences roughly the same length, no build, no emphatic close.

## Step 5: The Aloud Test

Read the passage aloud (or "aloud in your head"). Mark:
- Where you run out of breath → sentence is too long or poorly structured
- Where your voice drops into monotone → rhythm is flat
- Where you naturally add emphasis → these are the passage's power points
- Where your voice stumbles → syntax problem (route to syntax-patterns)

## Common Rhythm Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| **Flat rhythm** | All sentences 15-20 words | Add 1-2 short sentences (under 8 words) at emphasis points |
| **Choppy rhythm** | All sentences under 12 words | Combine sentences with subordination; add cumulative modifiers |
| **Breathless rhythm** | All sentences 30+ words | Break at natural clause boundaries; let short sentences punctuate |
| **False emphasis** | Short sentences on unimportant content | Move short sentences to moments of genuine emphasis |
| **Buried emphasis** | Key words in mid-sentence | Restructure so key words fall at sentence end |
| **Monotonous openers** | Every sentence starts the same way | Vary: gerund, subordinate clause, prepositional phrase, fragment |
