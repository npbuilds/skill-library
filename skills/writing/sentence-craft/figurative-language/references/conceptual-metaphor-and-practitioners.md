# Figurative Language — Conceptual Metaphor & Practitioner Deep Dives

## Lakoff's Three Types of Conceptual Metaphor

**Structural**: One concept structured by another. ARGUMENT IS WAR — we "attack" positions, "defend" claims. TIME IS MONEY — we "spend," "waste," "invest" time. These aren't decorative; they determine how we organize thought and society.

**Orientational**: Spatial relationships organizing whole systems. HAPPY IS UP / SAD IS DOWN. HEALTH IS UP / SICKNESS IS DOWN. Arise from embodied physical experience.

**Ontological**: Abstractions treated as bounded entities. INFLATION IS AN ENTITY. THE MIND IS A CONTAINER. Let us quantify and reason about abstractions.

**Craft implication**: When a character consistently uses economic metaphors for relationships ("investing" in people), that's not style — it's cognitive architecture. The metaphors betray the worldview.

## Image System Design (Extended)

**McKee's distinction**: External symbolism draws on cultural associations (water = cleansing). Internal symbolism creates associations through narrative context (a thimble = a kiss, in *Peter Pan*). The most powerful image systems combine both.

**Evolution principle**: Show the character's relationship to the image system changing over time. In Collins' work, birds and snakes represent competing philosophical arguments, appearing in evolving contexts. Early appearances establish; later appearances complicate or invert.

**Revision technique**: Don't preplan rigidly in first drafts. Write the story, look for patterns that emerged organically, then strengthen, prune, and fill gaps.

## Practitioner Deep Dives

### Toni Morrison — Metaphor as Incantation
Opening of *Beloved*: "124 was spiteful. Full of a baby's venom." Personifies a house and deploys metaphorical contradiction in seven words. Her sentences mimic trauma — nonlinear, recursive, rhythmically off-kilter. Image systems: milk (nurture/exploitation/trauma simultaneously), trees (refuge and lynching), eyes (surveillance/longing/erasure), flight (transcendence and return). Invented language when existing vocabulary failed: "rememory" and "disremembered" capture cyclical inherited trauma. Figurative language as cultural recovery tool.

### Cormac McCarthy — Landscape as Metaphor
"Loquacious precision" — dense, archaic prose that paradoxically clarifies. Landscapes function simultaneously as literal setting and metaphorical commentary on moral terrain. Key technique: metaphors are visually verifiable. Wasps passing through barnslat light "in a succession of strobic moments" — the imagery works because horizontal barnslats genuinely create that light pattern. Metaphor extracted from reality through precision of observation, not applied to it. Long unpunctuated sentences without qualifiers ("it seemed," "perhaps") create prophetic inevitability.

### Jeff VanderMeer — Biological Metaphor
In *Annihilation*, all perception filtered through biological vocabulary. A face compared to "the discarded shell of a horseshoe crab." This enacts the novel's theme: human consciousness embedded in biological processes, not separate from them. Weird fiction uses metaphor to approach what cannot be understood: "strives for understanding even when something cannot be understood, and acknowledges failure as sign of our limitations."

### Virginia Woolf — Metaphor as Consciousness
Sensory metaphor is not decoration applied to ideas but the mechanism through which consciousness emerges. Morning air as "flap of a wave; the kiss of a wave" — doesn't merely describe weather, activates Mrs. Dalloway's consciousness, triggering memory cascades. The lighthouse functions not as fixed symbol but as shifting metaphorical presence whose meaning depends on perceiver and context. "Time Passes" section treats the abandoned house as a character. Her method prioritizes rhythm over plot, suggestion over statement. The metaphors don't serve the story; they are the story.

### Vladimir Nabokov — Metaphor as Performance
Draws from personal synesthetic experience. The narrator's figurative dexterity IS their character. Dazzling, virtuosic comparisons that draw attention to their own brilliance — the beauty is sometimes a lie, and recognizing that is part of the reading experience.

## AI Dead Metaphor Patterns (Extended)
- "The weight of the crown" appears 8-45 times in AI-generated fantasy novels
- Formulaic intensity markers: "bone-chilling scream," "surge of triumph"
- Tell-don't-show: "a renewed sense of focus" instead of concrete demonstration
- Padding through metaphorical variation: rephrasing the same idea with different figures
- Human differentiator: grief as "a drawer that won't close all the way" — specific sensory experience no statistical model generates
