# Macro Rubric — the whole story

The dimensions for `whole-story-judge`. Each is **boolean**, names a **function**, cites a **source**, carries a **floor**. The `_shared/critic-core` loop and guards apply verbatim — including the **evidence-anchor cap** (here, a *pair* of locations: setup ↔ payoff, complication ↔ climax) and the **steelman** (a deliberate macro choice is not a failure).

> These are the properties that **only exist at the whole**. A scene can't have an "arc shape" or a "paid-off setup" by itself — which is exactly why the scene gate can't catch their failure, and why this judge exists.

## Required dimensions

| Dimension | Source | Pass-test (function) | Floor | Catches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Payoff realization** | CFPG / Chekhov's gun | Every prominent setup/foreshadow/promise is **fired** (paid off) or **deliberately, visibly subverted** — none is silently dropped | required | Dangling setups, unfired guns, abandoned plot threads, the "compelling-setup-no-resolution" gap |
| **Arc shape** | `narrative-arc` / `narrative-geometry` (Labov's story grammar) | The whole has a **discernible shape** — orientation → complication → climax → resolution, or an owned variant (escalation / reversal / convergence). There is a recognizable turn the whole builds to | required | Shapeless drift, no climax, flat "and then" sequence, a middle that doesn't escalate |
| **Causal connectivity** | plot logic (propositional) | Events connect **causally** (*this-because-that*), not merely sequentially (*and-then*). The story is a chain, not a list | required | Episodic strings, unmotivated turns, coincidence-driven plot |
| **Global coherence** | `character-belief-tracker` | **No contradiction across the whole** — character behavior, plot facts, world rules, and timeline hold from first scene to last | required | Continuity errors, character drift, a fact that flips, who-knew-what violations |
| **Promise kept** | `narrative-arc` | The **opening's implicit promise** — its tone, central question, and stakes — is honored (delivered or deliberately subverted) by the ending | required | Bait-and-switch, an ending that answers a question the book never asked, premise abandoned |

## Advisory dimension

| Dimension | Pass-test | Floor | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thematic throughline** | A discernible idea the whole expresses, carried across its parts | advisory | Theme is the author's to choose and need not be explicit; reported, never gating. (Bounded authority.) |

## The subversion note (the macro conformity guard)

Not every gun must fire, not every arc must resolve, not every promise must be kept *as posed*. A **deliberately** unfired setup (the gun that pointedly stays silent), an **anti-cathartic** ending (the climax withheld), an **ambiguous** resolution — these are earned macro choices, the macro analog of the prose `transgression-trap`.

Apply the steelman: a non-payoff PASSes if it is **patterned and effect-bearing** — the omission is felt as a *choice* (it lands an effect the payoff couldn't) rather than a *forgotten thread*. The tell of a real subversion vs a dropped setup: a subversion is **marked** (the text knows the gun didn't fire and makes you feel it); a dropped setup is **silent** (the text forgot). A judge that reflexively fails every unfired gun is a conformity engine — it would flunk every literary open ending.

## Note on input scale

A whole novel rarely fits in one read. Judge the **structure** from a complete outline / beat-sheet when the full draft is too long — the macro properties live in the skeleton (the setups ledger, the arc, the causal chain, the promise), which an outline preserves. Cite beats/chapters as the location pair.
