## The Old World Framework

**Terroir primacy.** Old World labeling is place-first: Chablis, not "Chardonnay from Chablis." The assumption is that *where* tells you *what*.

**Classification logic.** Every major Old World country has a hierarchical appellation system:
- France: AOC — tightest at top (Grand Cru), broader toward base
- Italy: DOCG → DOC → IGT — similar pyramid, more producer-driven variation within tiers
- Spain: DOCa → DO → Vino de Pago; age designations (Crianza, Reserva, Gran Reserva) overlay geography
- Germany: Prädikat sorts by must weight at harvest: Kabinett → Spätlese → Auslese → BA → TBA
- Portugal: DOC with sub-regions; Port governed by Douro DOC with IVP oversight

**Climate signature.** Old World = continental and maritime moderation. Lower alcohol, higher acid, earlier harvest compared to New World equivalents. Terroir expresses tension rather than ripeness.

## Cross-Country Comparisons Worth Knowing

**Pinot Noir benchmarks:** Burgundy Premier/Grand Cru vs. Alsace rouge vs. German Spätburgunder — same grape, radically different expressions.

**Sparkling methods:** Champagne (traditional, aged on lees) vs. Prosecco (Charmat, immediate fruit) vs. Cava (traditional, Macabeo/Xarel·lo/Parellada base) — price and style diverge sharply.

**Dessert wine routes:** Sauternes (botrytis), German TBA (botrytis + ice), Vin Santo (passito/dried grape), Tokaji Aszú (botrytis, Hungarian) — four mechanisms, similar richness.
