# Noble Grapes — The Royal Court

Fifteen grape varieties account for the vast majority of the world's fine wine. Knowing them is knowing the grammar of wine. This skill provides navigation and quick comparison; deep per-variety profiles live in the reference files.

## How to Read a Grape

Every noble variety has four dimensions:

1. **Structural signature** — body, tannin, acid, alcohol tendency. Genetic and climate-independent.
2. **Aromatic range** — what it smells like in cool vs. warm climates, young vs. aged.
3. **Winemaking sensitivity** — how much it changes with oak, lees, oxidation, or extraction.
4. **Terroir expression** — the regions that push it toward its best self.

## The Reds at a Glance

| Variety | Structure | Climate | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cabernet Sauvignon | High tannin, high acid, full body | Warm | Blackcurrant, pencil shavings |
| Merlot | Medium tannin, plush texture | Versatile | Plum, chocolate |
| Pinot Noir | Low tannin, high acid, light body | Cool only | Red cherry, earth, silk |
| Syrah/Shiraz | High tannin, full body | Cool = peppery, warm = jammy | Olive tapenade / blackberry |
| Nebbiolo | Very high tannin, high acid | Cool (Piedmont) | Tar, roses, dried cherry |
| Sangiovese | High acid, medium tannin | Tuscan warmth | Sour cherry, leather |
| Tempranillo | Medium tannin, low acid | Spanish plateau | Red berry, tobacco |
| Grenache | Low tannin, high alcohol | Hot and dry | Raspberry, garrigues |
| Zinfandel | High alcohol, jammy | California heat | Bramble, black pepper |

## The Whites at a Glance

| Variety | Acid | Body | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chardonnay | Medium | Medium–full | Neutral base, reflects terroir and oak |
| Sauvignon Blanc | High | Light–medium | Grassy, grapefruit, gooseberry |
| Riesling | Very high | Light | Petrol, green apple, lime; ages magnificently |
| Chenin Blanc | High | Variable | Quince, lanolin, honey; extreme versatility |
| Gewürztraminer | Low | Full | Lychee, rose petal, broad aromatic |
| Pinot Gris | Low–medium | Full | Peach, smoke, richness without drama |

## Three Key Distinctions

**Aromatic vs. neutral grapes.** Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Sauvignon Blanc announce themselves. Chardonnay whispers — its flavor comes from terroir and winemaking. When assessing an unknown white: aromatic = follow the scent; neutral = read texture and weight.

**Tannin sources in reds.** Nebbiolo and Cabernet are both high-tannin but different in origin — Nebbiolo from cool-extracted skin contact, Cabernet from thick skins in warm ripe fruit. Same scale number, different mouthfeel.

**Acid as the aging engine.** Riesling ages because of acid. Pinot ages because of acid plus light-tannin secondary complexity. Grenache doesn't age well solo — neither acid nor tannin in sufficient quantity.
