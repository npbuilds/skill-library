# Quality Assessment — Quick Reference


## Quick Reference

| Category | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short | < 30 seconds | Flavors cut off quickly; poor quality indicator |
| Medium | 30–45 seconds | Acceptable persistence; majority of quality table wines |
| Long | > 45 seconds | Quality wine; flavors linger and are present |
| Very Long | > 60 seconds | Grand Cru / outstanding quality; 1–2 minutes is exceptional |

## Quick Reference

| Level | Description | BLIC Profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Faulty** | The wine is defective; undrinkable due to a fault (TCA, extreme VA, mousiness, heat damage, severe oxidation) | BLIC is irrelevant — route to fault-diagnosis |
| **Poor** | No fault present, but the wine fails on multiple quality criteria: unbalanced, short finish, low intensity, no complexity | Unbalanced + short + thin + simple |
| **Acceptable** | Meets a basic standard: clean, some balance, adequate finish, adequate intensity | Roughly balanced + medium finish + moderate intensity + minimal complexity |
| **Good** | Clear quality characteristics: balance, some length, clear intensity, emerging complexity | Balanced + medium-long finish + pronounced intensity + some complexity |
| **Very Good** | Demonstrates quality throughout: well-balanced, long finish, concentrated, genuinely complex | Well-balanced + long finish + concentrated + clearly complex |
| **Outstanding** | Exceptional quality: flawless balance, very long and evolving finish, deep intensity, profound complexity; wine of distinction | Perfect balance + very long evolving finish + deep intensity + profound and evolving complexity |

## Quick Reference

| System | Scale | Origin / Context | Philosophy | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Robert Parker / Wine Advocate** | 50–100 (effectively 85–100 in practice) | US; Robert Parker developed this for commercial wine criticism in the 1970s | Flavor-forward, concentration-focused, fruit ripeness rewarded; iconic wines from top producers reliably score high | Commercial purchasing decisions; cellar investment; widely understood by consumers |
| **Jancis Robinson MW** | 20-point (12–20 in practice) | UK/Europe; the European precision model | 12 = faulty or unbalanced; 14 = fine, no particular fault; 15 = good; 16 = fine achievement; 17 = excellent; 18 = a great wine; 18.5+ = extraordinary; 20 = theoretical perfection rarely assigned | Professional and trade analysis; distinguishes nuanced quality differences at the top end better than the 100-point system |
| **Decanter** | 5-star / 100-point hybrid | UK; consumer-focused publication | 1 star / < 85 points = faulty or poor; 3 stars / 90–94 = highly recommended; 5 stars / 97+ = perfect | Consumer-facing recommendations; accessible to non-trade audiences |
| **WSET Quality Ladder** | 6-level qualitative scale (Faulty → Outstanding) | WSET global education standard | Analytically rigorous; framework-based; not numerical; designed for training consistent, replicable assessments | Education; exam preparation; building analytical tasting skills; context-neutral across styles |
| **Wine Spectator** | 50–100 | US; James Suckling, Matt Kramer | Similar philosophy to Parker; ripe fruit rewarded; broad commercial reach | Commercial purchasing; restaurant-by-the-glass programs |

## Readiness by Structural Marker

| Marker | Too Early | In Window | Too Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tannin (red) | Grippy / drying / aggressive | Silky to fine-grained / integrated | Collapsed / barely present |
| Acidity (white) | Sharp / dominant over fruit | Integrated / lifting without attacking | Flat / lost |
| Color (red) | Purple-ruby | Ruby-garnet | Garnet-tawny to brown |
| Rim variation (red) | None | Slight orange-brick rim | Wide orange-brown rim; browning core |
| Fruit character | Primary dominant (fresh berry/citrus) | Primary + secondary/tertiary | Fruit dried/faded; tertiary fading |
| Nose development | Youthful | Developing to complex | Tired / flat / oxidized |
| Finish | Long but primary/simple | Long and evolving | Short; flat or harsh |
