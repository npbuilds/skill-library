# Service Protocol — Quick Reference


## Quick Reference

| Style | Ideal Range | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Sparkling NV (Champagne, Cava, Prosecco) | 6-9°C (43-48°F) | Served too warm (18-20°C) — loses freshness, goes flat faster |
| Sparkling Vintage / Prestige Cuvée | 9-11°C (48-52°F) | Over-chilled — suppresses the complexity that justifies the price |
| Light whites (Muscadet, Pinot Grigio, Vinho Verde) | 7-10°C (45-50°F) | — |
| Aromatic whites (Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Viognier, Albariño) | 8-12°C (46-54°F) | Often over-chilled — aromatic expression requires moderate temperature |
| Full whites (white Burgundy, white Rhône, oaked Chardonnay) | 12-14°C (54-57°F) | Served refrigerator-cold (4°C) — texture collapses, oak seems harsh |
| Rosé | 8-12°C (46-54°F) | — |
| Light reds (Beaujolais, Pinot Noir, Gamay, Schiava) | 13-15°C (55-59°F) | Served at room temperature (20-22°C) — alcohol dominates, loses freshness |
| Medium reds (Rioja Reserva, Chianti Classico, Côtes du Rhône) | 15-17°C (59-63°F) | — |
| Full reds (Barolo, Brunello, Napa Cabernet, Barossa Shiraz) | 17-19°C (63-66°F) | Served at warm room temperature — alcohol becomes the dominant impression |
| Fortified dry (Fino Sherry, Manzanilla, dry Sercial Madeira) | 7-10°C (45-50°F) | Served at room temperature, which destroys their freshness and saline delicacy |
| Fortified sweet (Ruby/Tawny Port, sweet Sherry, sweet Madeira) | 15-18°C (59-64°F) | — |
