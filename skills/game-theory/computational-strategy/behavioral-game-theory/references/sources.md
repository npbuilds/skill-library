# Sources — Behavioral Game Theory

## Primary Texts

**Camerer, C.F. *Behavioral Game Theory: Experiments in Strategic Interaction* (Princeton, 2003)**
The definitive reference. Covers all major experimental findings, level-k thinking, QRE, social preferences, learning, and more. 500+ pages of experiments and models.
→ Used for: all topics in this skill. The single most important source.

**Crawford, V.P., Costa-Gomes, M.A. & Iriberri, N. (2013)** "Structural Models of Nonequilibrium Strategic Thinking: Theory, Evidence, and Applications." *Journal of Economic Literature* 51: 5-62.
→ Comprehensive survey of level-k and cognitive hierarchy models. Formal definitions, estimation methods, and applications.

## Key Papers — Level-k and Cognitive Hierarchy

**Nagel, R. (1995)** "Unraveling in Guessing Games: An Experimental Study." *American Economic Review* 85: 1313-1326.
→ The beauty contest experiment. Shows that human reasoning depth is limited — most subjects iterate 1-2 steps.

**Stahl, D.O. & Wilson, P.W. (1994)** "Experimental Evidence on Players' Models of Other Players." *Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization* 25: 309-327.
→ Early formalization of level-k thinking in experiments. Different types of reasoning observed.

**Stahl, D.O. & Wilson, P.W. (1995)** "On Players' Models of Other Players: Theory and Experimental Evidence." *Games and Economic Behavior* 10: 218-254.
→ Extended analysis. Estimation of level distributions in experimental data.

**Camerer, C.F., Ho, T.-H. & Chong, J.-K. (2004)** "A Cognitive Hierarchy Model of Games." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 119: 861-898.
→ The cognitive hierarchy model. Poisson distribution of levels. Better fitting than level-k for many datasets.

## Key Papers — Quantal Response Equilibrium

**McKelvey, R.D. & Palfrey, T.R. (1995)** "Quantal Response Equilibria for Normal Form Games." *Games and Economic Behavior* 10: 6-38.
→ Introduces QRE. Logit equilibrium as a parametric family. Shows QRE organizes many experimental anomalies.

**McKelvey, R.D. & Palfrey, T.R. (1998)** "Quantal Response Equilibria for Extensive Form Games." *Experimental Economics* 1: 9-41.
→ Agent QRE for sequential games. Noisy choices at each information set.

## Key Papers — Social Preferences

**Fehr, E. & Schmidt, K.M. (1999)** "A Theory of Fairness, Competition, and Cooperation." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 114: 817-868.
→ Inequality aversion model. Two parameters (α, β) capture disutility from disadvantageous and advantageous inequality. Explains ultimatum, public goods, and market experiments.

**Bolton, G.E. & Ockenfels, A. (2000)** "ERC: A Theory of Equity, Reciprocity, and Competition." *American Economic Review* 90: 166-193.
→ Alternative social preference model based on relative payoff concerns. Complementary to Fehr-Schmidt.

**Rabin, M. (1993)** "Incorporating Fairness into Game Theory and Economics." *American Economic Review* 83: 1281-1302.
→ Intentions-based reciprocity model. Kindness is reciprocated with kindness; unkindness with retaliation.

## Key Papers — Experimental Classics

**Guth, W., Schmittberger, R. & Schwarze, B. (1982)** "An Experimental Analysis of Ultimatum Bargaining." *Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization* 3: 367-388.
→ The original ultimatum game experiment. Established that responders reject "unfair" offers.

**Kahneman, D. & Tversky, A. (1979)** "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk." *Econometrica* 47: 263-292.
→ While not a game theory paper, prospect theory fundamentally shapes how behavioral GT models risk preferences.

## Nobel Prize Context

**2002 Nobel in Economics**: Daniel Kahneman (with Vernon Smith) "for having integrated insights from psychological research into economic science" — foundational for behavioral game theory.

**2017 Nobel in Economics**: Richard Thaler "for his contributions to behavioural economics" — nudge theory, mental accounting, and behavioral insights applied to strategic settings.
