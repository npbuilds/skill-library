# Sources — Matching Markets

## Primary Texts

**Roth, A.E. & Sotomayor, M.A.O. *Two-Sided Matching* (Cambridge, 1990)**
The definitive monograph. Covers stable matchings, the lattice structure, many-to-one matching, strategy-proofness, and the college admissions problem.
→ Used for: all foundational matching theory, lattice structure, algorithm properties.

**Roth, A.E. *Who Gets What — and Why* (Mariner Books, 2015)**
Popular-audience treatment by the 2012 Nobel laureate. Covers matching markets, kidney exchange, school choice, and the principles of market design.
→ Used for: design principles (thick, uncongested, safe), case study narratives.

**Abdulkadiroglu, A. & Sonmez, T. (2003)** "School Choice: A Mechanism Design Approach." *American Economic Review* 93: 729-747.
→ Framework for school choice mechanisms. Comparison of Boston mechanism, DA, and TTC.

## Key Papers — Foundational

**Gale, D. & Shapley, L.S. (1962)** "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage." *American Mathematical Monthly* 69: 9-15.
→ Founding paper. DA algorithm, existence of stable matchings, proposer-optimality.

**Shapley, L.S. & Scarf, H.E. (1974)** "On Cores and Indivisibility." *Journal of Mathematical Economics* 1: 23-37.
→ Housing market model. TTC algorithm produces the unique core allocation.

**Roth, A.E. (1982)** "The Economics of Matching: Stability and Incentives." *Mathematics of Operations Research* 7: 617-628.
→ No stable mechanism is strategy-proof for all participants. The impossibility that constrains matching market design.

**Roth, A.E. (1984)** "The Evolution of the Labor Market for Medical Interns and Residents." *Journal of Political Economy* 92: 991-1016.
→ Shows the NRMP is equivalent to hospital-proposing DA. Landmark applied game theory.

## Key Papers — Kidney Exchange

**Roth, A.E., Sonmez, T. & Unver, M.U. (2004)** "Kidney Exchange." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 119: 457-488.
→ Theoretical foundation for kidney exchange via top trading cycles with chains.

**Roth, A.E., Sonmez, T. & Unver, M.U. (2007)** "Efficient Kidney Exchange: Coincidence of Wants in Markets with Compatibility-Based Preferences." *American Economic Review* 97: 828-851.
→ Extends to non-simultaneous chains. Shows that long chains from altruistic donors dramatically increase transplant volume.

## Key Papers — School Choice

**Abdulkadiroglu, A., Pathak, P.A. & Roth, A.E. (2005)** "The New York City High School Match." *American Economic Review Papers & Proceedings* 95: 364-367.
→ Documents the NYC school choice redesign. Companion to the full AER paper.

**Pathak, P.A. & Sonmez, T. (2008)** "Leveling the Playing Field: Sincere and Sophisticated Players in the Boston Mechanism." *American Economic Review* 98: 1636-1652.
→ Shows the Boston mechanism disadvantages unsophisticated participants. Motivates the switch to DA.

## Key Papers — Probabilistic and Random Matching

**Bogomolnaia, A. & Moulin, H. (2001)** "A New Solution to the Random Assignment Problem." *Journal of Economic Theory* 100: 295-328.
→ Probabilistic Serial mechanism. Ordinally efficient and envy-free in expectation.

## Nobel Prize Context

**2012 Nobel in Economics**: Alvin Roth and Lloyd Shapley "for the theory of stable allocations and the practice of market design." Recognized Shapley's foundational theory and Roth's practical deployments.
