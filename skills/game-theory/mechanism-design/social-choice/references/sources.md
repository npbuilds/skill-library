# Sources — Social Choice

## Primary Texts

**Arrow, K.J. *Social Choice and Individual Values* (Yale, 2nd ed. 1963)**
The founding text. Proves the impossibility theorem. Essential reading for understanding the axiomatic approach to social choice.
→ Used for: Arrow's theorem, axiomatic framework.

**Moulin, H. *Fair Division and Collective Welfare* (MIT Press, 2003)**
Modern treatment of social choice, fair division, and collective decision-making. Covers both impossibility results and constructive mechanisms.
→ Used for: modern axiomatic analysis, connections to mechanism design.

**Mas-Colell, A., Whinston, M.D. & Green, J.R. *Microeconomic Theory* (Oxford, 1995)**
Chapter 21 provides a rigorous treatment of social choice theory within the broader microeconomic framework.
→ Used for: formal definitions, proofs of impossibility theorems.

## Key Papers — Impossibility Results

**Arrow, K.J. (1951)** "A Difficulty in the Concept of Social Welfare." *Journal of Political Economy* 58: 328-346.
→ The original Arrow impossibility result. Proved that no social welfare function satisfies universal domain, Pareto, IIA, and non-dictatorship simultaneously.

**Gibbard, A. (1973)** "Manipulation of Voting Schemes: A General Result." *Econometrica* 41: 587-601.
→ Proves that any non-dictatorial, onto social choice function with 3+ alternatives is manipulable.

**Satterthwaite, M.A. (1975)** "Strategy-Proofness and Arrow's Conditions." *Journal of Economic Theory* 10: 187-217.
→ Independent proof of the Gibbard result, connecting it to Arrow's framework.

**Sen, A.K. (1970)** "The Impossibility of a Paretian Liberal." *Journal of Political Economy* 78: 152-157.
→ Shows the conflict between Pareto efficiency and individual rights.

## Key Papers — Constructive Results

**Black, D. (1948)** "On the Rationale of Group Decision-Making." *Journal of Political Economy* 56: 23-34.
→ Single-peaked preferences and the median voter theorem. The key domain restriction enabling strategy-proofness.

**Moulin, H. (1980)** "On Strategy-Proofness and Single Peakedness." *Public Choice* 35: 437-455.
→ Characterizes all strategy-proof rules on single-peaked domains: generalized median voter rules.

**Gibbard, A. (1977)** "Manipulation of Schemes that Mix Voting with Chance." *Econometrica* 45: 665-681.
→ Extends impossibility to randomized rules.

## Key Papers — Modern/Applied

**Brandt, F., Conitzer, V., Endriss, U., Lang, J. & Procaccia, A.D. (eds.) *Handbook of Computational Social Choice* (Cambridge, 2016)**
→ Comprehensive reference for computational aspects: complexity of winner determination, manipulation detection, and algorithmic social choice.

**Weyl, E.G. (2017)** "The Robustness of Quadratic Voting." *Public Choice* 172: 75-107.
→ Theoretical foundations of quadratic voting. Shows approximate efficiency under reasonable assumptions.

**Lalley, S.P. & Weyl, E.G. (2018)** "Quadratic Voting: How Mechanism Design Can Radicalize Democracy." *AEA Papers and Proceedings* 108: 33-37.
→ Accessible introduction to quadratic voting and its democratic applications.

## Nobel Prize Context

**1972 Nobel in Economics**: Kenneth Arrow — for general equilibrium theory and welfare economics, including his impossibility theorem.
**1998 Nobel in Economics**: Amartya Sen — for contributions to welfare economics, including the liberal paradox and capability approach.
