# Sources — Signaling & Screening

## Primary Texts

**Bolton, P. & Dewatripont, M. *Contract Theory* (MIT Press, 2005)**
The comprehensive graduate textbook on contract theory. Covers adverse selection, moral hazard, incomplete contracts, and multi-agent incentive problems. The standard reference for the field.
→ Used for: all contract theory models, moral hazard, multi-agent problems.

**Fudenberg, D. & Tirole, J. *Game Theory* (MIT Press, 1991)**
Chapters 7-9 cover signaling games, perfect Bayesian equilibrium in signaling contexts, and the foundations of information economics within the game theory framework.
→ Used for: formal signaling models, equilibrium refinements in signaling games.

**Laffont, J.-J. & Martimort, D. *The Theory of Incentives* (Princeton, 2002)**
Principal-agent theory with emphasis on regulation and public economics applications. Comprehensive treatment of moral hazard and adverse selection in contractual settings.
→ Used for: principal-agent models, regulation, mechanism design connections.

## Key Papers — Adverse Selection

**Akerlof, G.A. (1970)** "The Market for 'Lemons': Quality Uncertainty and the Market Mechanism." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 84: 488-500.
→ The founding paper of information economics. Shows how asymmetric information about quality causes market failure. Nobel Prize 2001.

**Rothschild, M. & Stiglitz, J.E. (1976)** "Equilibrium in Competitive Insurance Markets: An Essay on the Economics of Imperfect Information." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 90: 629-649.
→ Screening through menu design. The competitive insurance model. Non-existence of equilibrium puzzle. Nobel Prize 2001 (Stiglitz).

## Key Papers — Signaling

**Spence, A.M. (1973)** "Job Market Signaling." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 87: 355-374.
→ Education as a costly signal of unobservable productivity. Separating and pooling equilibria. Nobel Prize 2001.

**Riley, J.G. (1979)** "Informational Equilibrium." *Econometrica* 47: 331-359.
→ General signaling framework. Reactive equilibrium concept. Important for equilibrium selection in signaling games.

**Cho, I.-K. & Kreps, D.M. (1987)** "Signaling Games and Stable Equilibria." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 102: 179-221.
→ The Intuitive Criterion — a refinement that selects "reasonable" equilibria in signaling games by eliminating incredible off-path beliefs. Became the standard equilibrium refinement for signaling.

## Key Papers — Moral Hazard

**Holmstrom, B. (1979)** "Moral Hazard and Observability." *Bell Journal of Economics* 10: 74-91.
→ Optimal contracts with moral hazard. The informativeness principle and sufficient statistic result. Nobel Prize 2016.

**Holmstrom, B. (1982)** "Moral Hazard in Teams." *Bell Journal of Economics* 13: 324-340.
→ Team production with unobservable individual effort. Shows budget-breaking is needed for incentive compatibility.

**Hart, O. & Holmstrom, B. (1987)** "The Theory of Contracts." In *Advances in Economic Theory*, ed. T.F. Bewley, Cambridge University Press.
→ Survey of contract theory at the time. Unified treatment of adverse selection and moral hazard.

## Nobel Prize Context

**2001 Nobel in Economics**: George Akerlof, Michael Spence, and Joseph Stiglitz "for their analyses of markets with asymmetric information."

**2016 Nobel in Economics**: Oliver Hart and Bengt Holmstrom "for their contributions to contract theory."
