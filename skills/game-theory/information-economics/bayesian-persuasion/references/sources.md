# Sources — Bayesian Persuasion

## Primary Texts

**Kamenica, E. & Gentzkow, M. (2011)** "Bayesian Persuasion." *American Economic Review* 101: 2590-2615.
→ The founding paper. Introduces the concavification approach, the prosecutor example, and the general framework for optimal information design with commitment.

**Bergemann, D. & Morris, S. (2016)** "Bayes Correlated Equilibrium and the Comparison of Information Structures." *Theoretical Economics* 11: 487-522.
→ Extends persuasion to games with multiple interacting receivers. Introduces Bayes correlated equilibrium as the solution concept for information design in games.

**Bergemann, D. & Morris, S. (2019)** "Information Design: A Unified Perspective." *Journal of Economic Literature* 57: 44-95.
→ Comprehensive survey unifying Bayesian persuasion, information design in games, and mechanism design with information. The standard reference for the modern literature.

## Key Papers — Cheap Talk

**Crawford, V.P. & Sobel, J. (1982)** "Strategic Information Transmission." *Econometrica* 50: 1431-1451.
→ Foundational model of cheap talk. Partition equilibria, the role of interest alignment, and the limits of costless communication.

**Battaglini, M. (2002)** "Multiple Referrals and Multidimensional Cheap Talk." *Econometrica* 70: 1379-1401.
→ With multiple senders in a multi-dimensional setting, full information revelation can be achieved in equilibrium. Competition among senders benefits the receiver.

## Key Papers — Disclosure

**Grossman, S.J. (1981)** "The Informational Role of Warranties and Private Disclosure about Product Quality." *Journal of Law and Economics* 24: 461-483.
→ The unraveling argument — if disclosure is verifiable and costless, full disclosure is the unique equilibrium.

**Milgrom, P.R. (1981)** "Good News and Bad News: Representation Theorems and Applications." *Bell Journal of Economics* 12: 380-391.
→ Independent development of the unraveling result. The "no news is bad news" principle.

**Dye, R.A. (1985)** "Disclosure of Nonproprietary Information." *Journal of Accounting Research* 23: 123-145.
→ Breaks unraveling with uncertainty about whether the sender is informed. Partial disclosure can be sustained in equilibrium.

## Key Papers — Multi-Receiver and Applications

**Goldstein, I. & Leitner, Y. (2018)** "Stress Tests and Information Disclosure." *Journal of Economic Theory* 177: 34-69.
→ Bank stress tests as Bayesian persuasion. Regulators design the information revealed to financial markets.

**Gentzkow, M. & Kamenica, E. (2016)** "A Rothschild-Stiglitz Approach to Bayesian Persuasion." *American Economic Review Papers & Proceedings* 106: 597-601.
→ Alternative derivation of optimal persuasion using the Rothschild-Stiglitz mean-preserving spread framework.

**Gentzkow, M. & Kamenica, E. (2017)** "Competition in Persuasion." *Review of Economic Studies* 84: 300-322.
→ Multiple senders competing to persuade a receiver. More competition leads to more information revelation. Applications to media competition.

## Surveys

**Kamenica, E. (2019)** "Bayesian Persuasion and Information Design." *Annual Review of Economics* 11: 249-272.
→ Accessible survey of the field as of 2019. Covers extensions, applications, and open questions.
