# Sources — Cooperative Games

Primary and supplementary references grounding this skill's content.

## Primary Texts

**Maschler, Solan & Zamir, *Game Theory* (Cambridge, 2nd ed. 2020)**
Chapters 13-18 provide the most comprehensive textbook treatment of cooperative game theory. Covers the core, Shapley value, nucleolus, bargaining set, kernel, and NTU games. Every theorem proved. Second edition expands NTU coverage.
→ Used for: formal definitions of all cooperative solution concepts, axiomatic characterizations, existence/uniqueness proofs.

**Osborne & Rubinstein, *A Course in Game Theory* (MIT Press, 1994)**
Part IV covers coalitional games, the core, Shapley value, and bargaining. Concise mathematical treatment.
→ Used for: core and Shapley value foundations, bargaining theory.

**Roth & Sotomayor, *Two-Sided Matching* (Cambridge, 1990)**
The definitive monograph on matching theory. Covers stable matching, the Gale-Shapley algorithm, the lattice structure, many-to-one matching, and the college admissions problem.
→ Used for: all matching theory content, lattice structure, strategy-proofness results.

**Moulin, *Fair Division and Collective Welfare* (MIT Press, 2003)**
Comprehensive treatment of fair division, cost sharing, and collective choice. Covers proportionality, envy-freeness, efficiency, and their interactions.
→ Used for: fair division concepts, impossibility results, practical mechanisms.

## Key Papers — Foundational

**Shapley, L.S. (1953)** "A Value for N-person Games." In *Contributions to the Theory of Games II*, ed. Kuhn & Tucker, pp. 307-317.
→ Introduces the Shapley value. The original axiomatic characterization.

**Gillies, D.B. (1953)** "Some Theorems on N-Person Games." PhD dissertation, Princeton.
→ Introduces the core as a solution concept.

**Schmeidler, D. (1969)** "The Nucleolus of a Characteristic Function Game." *SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics* 17: 1163-1170.
→ Introduces the nucleolus. Proves existence and uniqueness.

**Nash, J.F. (1950)** "The Bargaining Problem." *Econometrica* 18: 155-162.
→ The Nash bargaining solution. Four axioms → unique solution.

**Rubinstein, A. (1982)** "Perfect Equilibrium in a Bargaining Model." *Econometrica* 50: 97-109.
→ Non-cooperative foundation for Nash bargaining via alternating offers.

**Kalai, E. & Smorodinsky, M. (1975)** "Other Solutions to Nash's Bargaining Problem." *Econometrica* 43: 513-518.
→ Alternative to Nash bargaining using monotonicity instead of IIA.

## Key Papers — Matching

**Gale, D. & Shapley, L.S. (1962)** "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage." *American Mathematical Monthly* 69: 9-15.
→ The founding paper. Deferred acceptance algorithm, existence of stable matchings.

**Roth, A.E. (1984)** "The Evolution of the Labor Market for Medical Interns and Residents: A Case Study in Game Theory." *Journal of Political Economy* 92: 991-1016.
→ Shows the NRMP algorithm is equivalent to hospital-proposing DA. Landmark applied game theory.

**Roth, A.E. & Peranson, E. (1999)** "The Redesign of the Matching Market for American Physicians." *American Economic Review* 89: 748-780.
→ Design of the current NRMP algorithm. Handles couples and complex constraints.

**Abdulkadiroglu, A. & Sonmez, T. (2003)** "School Choice: A Mechanism Design Approach." *American Economic Review* 93: 729-747.
→ Framework for school choice. Comparison of Boston mechanism, DA, and TTC.

**Roth, A.E., Sonmez, T. & Unver, M.U. (2004)** "Kidney Exchange." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 119: 457-488.
→ Theoretical foundation for kidney exchange programs.

## Key Papers — Fair Division

**Brams, S.J. & Taylor, A.D. (1995)** "An Envy-Free Cake Division Protocol." *American Mathematical Monthly* 102: 9-18.
→ First bounded envy-free protocol for 3 players (Selfridge-Conway is earlier but was unpublished until later).

**Aziz, H. & Mackenzie, S. (2016)** "A Discrete and Bounded Envy-Free Cake Cutting Protocol for Any Number of Agents." *FOCS 2016*.
→ Resolves a decades-long open problem: bounded envy-free cake cutting for n agents.

**Caragiannis, I., Kurokawa, D., Moulin, H., Procaccia, A.D., Shah, N. & Wang, J. (2019)** "The Unreasonable Fairness of Maximum Nash Welfare." *ACM Transactions on Economics and Computation* 7(3).
→ Shows maximizing Nash welfare (product of utilities) yields EF1 allocations for indivisible goods. Connects Nash bargaining to discrete fair division.

## Supplementary References

**Roth, A.E. *Who Gets What — and Why* (Mariner Books, 2015)**
Popular-audience book on market design by one of its founders. Covers matching, kidney exchange, school choice, and the principles of "thick, uncongested, safe" markets. Excellent for intuition and applications.

**Shapley, L.S. & Shubik, M. (1971)** "The Assignment Game I: The Core." *International Journal of Game Theory* 1: 111-130.
→ TU matching markets. Core = competitive equilibria. Foundation for matching with transfers.

**Young, H.P. (1985)** "Monotonic Solutions of Cooperative Games." *International Journal of Game Theory* 14: 65-72.
→ Alternative axiomatization of the Shapley value using strong monotonicity.
