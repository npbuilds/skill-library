# Sources — Classical Games

Primary and supplementary references grounding this skill's content.

## Primary Texts

**Osborne & Rubinstein, *A Course in Game Theory* (MIT Press, 1994)**
Chapters 1-6 cover strategic games, Nash equilibrium, mixed strategies, extensive games with perfect and imperfect information, and rationalizability. Mathematically precise, concise treatment. Freely available from the authors.
→ Used for: formal definitions, rationalizability, extensive-form games.

**Fudenberg & Tirole, *Game Theory* (MIT Press, 1991)**
Parts I-II cover static and dynamic games of complete information. Part III covers static games of incomplete information. Part IV covers dynamic games of incomplete information. Chapter 11 covers equilibrium refinements comprehensively.
→ Used for: equilibrium refinements hierarchy, signaling games, repeated games.

**Maschler, Solan & Zamir, *Game Theory* (Cambridge, 2nd ed. 2020)**
Chapters 1-12 cover strategic-form games, mixed extensions, two-player zero-sum games, extensive-form games, and equilibrium refinements. Every theorem is proved. Second edition adds stochastic games and expanded refinements.
→ Used for: comprehensive formal foundations, proofs, zero-sum theory.

## Key Papers

**Nash, J.F. (1950)** "Equilibrium Points in N-person Games." *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 36: 48-49.
→ The founding result. Existence of equilibrium in finite games via Kakutani's fixed point theorem.

**Selten, R. (1965)** "Spieltheoretische Behandlung eines Oligopolmodells mit Nachfrageträgheit." *Zeitschrift für die gesamte Staatswissenschaft* 121: 301-324.
→ Introduces subgame perfect equilibrium and the chain-store paradox.

**Selten, R. (1975)** "Reexamination of the Perfectness Concept for Equilibrium Points in Extensive Games." *International Journal of Game Theory* 4: 25-55.
→ Introduces trembling hand perfect equilibrium.

**Harsanyi, J.C. (1967-68)** "Games with Incomplete Information Played by 'Bayesian' Players, Parts I-III." *Management Science* 14: 159-182, 320-334, 486-502.
→ Foundation for Bayesian games. The "Harsanyi transformation" converts incomplete-information games to imperfect-information games.

**Aumann, R.J. (1974)** "Subjectivity and Correlation in Randomized Strategies." *Journal of Mathematical Economics* 1: 67-96.
→ Introduces correlated equilibrium.

**Kreps, D. & Wilson, R. (1982)** "Sequential Equilibria." *Econometrica* 50: 863-894.
→ Introduces sequential equilibrium for extensive-form games with imperfect information.

**Bernheim, B.D. (1984)** "Rationalizable Strategic Behavior." *Econometrica* 52: 1007-1028.
**Pearce, D. (1984)** "Rationalizable Strategic Behavior and the Problem of Perfection." *Econometrica* 52: 1029-1050.
→ Independently introduce rationalizability.

**Cho, I.-K. & Kreps, D. (1987)** "Signaling Games and Stable Equilibria." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 102: 179-221.
→ The intuitive criterion refinement for signaling games.

## Supplementary References

**Osborne, M. *An Introduction to Game Theory* (Oxford, 2004)**
Best undergraduate-level treatment. Clear exposition of all core concepts without requiring measure theory. Good for building intuition.

**Myerson, R. *Game Theory: Analysis of Conflict* (Harvard, 1997)**
Strong on Bayesian games and mechanism design foundations. Myerson's own treatment of proper equilibrium and the revelation principle.

**Binmore, K. *Playing for Real* (Oxford, 2007)**
Emphasizes interpretive foundations — what equilibrium concepts actually mean and when they apply. Philosophical rigor.

## Experimental/Empirical Sources

**Camerer, C. *Behavioral Game Theory* (Princeton, 2003)**
Chapters 2-7 document systematic deviations from classical predictions: ultimatum games, public goods, coordination, signaling. Essential for understanding where classical theory's predictions succeed and fail.

**Chiappori, Levitt & Groseclose (2002)** "Testing Mixed-Strategy Equilibria When Players Are Heterogeneous." *American Economic Review* 92: 1138-1151.
→ Confirms mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium predictions in professional soccer penalty kicks.

## Computational References

**Daskalakis, Goldberg & Papadimitriou (2009)** "The Complexity of Computing a Nash Equilibrium." *SIAM Journal on Computing* 39: 195-259.
→ Computing Nash equilibrium is PPAD-complete, even in 2-player games. One of the most important complexity results in algorithmic game theory.
