# Sources — Evolutionary Games

## Primary Texts

**Maynard Smith, J. *Evolution and the Theory of Games* (Cambridge, 1982)**
The founding text. Introduces ESS formally, analyzes hawk-dove, war of attrition, sex ratios, and the evolution of cooperation. Written by the biologist who (with Price) created the field.
→ Used for: ESS definition, hawk-dove, asymmetric contests, biological applications.

**Weibull, J.W. *Evolutionary Game Theory* (MIT Press, 1995)**
Rigorous mathematical treatment. Covers ESS, replicator dynamics, stability concepts, asymmetric games, and connections to learning theory. The standard graduate reference.
→ Used for: formal definitions, replicator dynamics connection, asymmetric games.

**Sandholm, W.H. *Population Games and Evolutionary Dynamics* (MIT Press, 2010)**
Modern, comprehensive treatment. Covers population games, revision protocols (imitation, best response, logit), deterministic and stochastic dynamics, and stability.
→ Used for: population game framework, revision protocols, modern stability concepts.

**Nowak, M.A. *Evolutionary Dynamics: Exploring the Equations of Life* (Harvard, 2006)**
Accessible treatment linking evolutionary game theory to mathematical biology. Covers replicator dynamics, finite populations, spatial games, and the five mechanisms for cooperation.
→ Used for: cooperation evolution, finite population effects, spatial structure.

## Key Papers — Foundational

**Maynard Smith, J. & Price, G.R. (1973)** "The Logic of Animal Conflict." *Nature* 246: 15-18.
→ Introduces ESS and the hawk-dove game. The founding paper of evolutionary game theory.

**Taylor, P.D. & Jonker, L.B. (1978)** "Evolutionary Stable Strategies and Game Dynamics." *Mathematical Biosciences* 40: 145-156.
→ Introduces replicator dynamics. Proves connection between ESS and dynamic stability.

**Hofbauer, J. & Sigmund, K. (1998)** *Evolutionary Games and Population Dynamics* (Cambridge).
→ Comprehensive mathematical treatment combining evolutionary game theory with ecological population dynamics.

## Key Papers — Cooperation

**Hamilton, W.D. (1964)** "The Genetical Evolution of Social Behaviour, I & II." *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 7: 1-52.
→ Kin selection: altruism evolves when rb > c (relatedness × benefit > cost). Hamilton's rule.

**Trivers, R.L. (1971)** "The Evolution of Reciprocal Altruism." *Quarterly Review of Biology* 46: 35-57.
→ Direct reciprocity: cooperation via repeated interaction. Foundation for tit-for-tat analysis.

**Axelrod, R. & Hamilton, W.D. (1981)** "The Evolution of Cooperation." *Science* 211: 1390-1396.
→ Axelrod's tournaments. Tit-for-tat wins iterated PD tournaments. Demonstrated evolutionary viability of cooperation.

**Nowak, M.A. & Sigmund, K. (1998)** "Evolution of Indirect Reciprocity by Image Scoring." *Nature* 393: 573-577.
→ Indirect reciprocity: cooperation sustained by reputation. Cooperate with those who have a good reputation.

**Nowak, M.A. (2006)** "Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation." *Science* 314: 1560-1563.
→ Unifying framework: kin selection, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection.

## Key Papers — Biological Applications

**Sinervo, B. & Lively, C.M. (1996)** "The Rock-Paper-Scissors Game and the Evolution of Alternative Male Strategies." *Nature* 380: 240-243.
→ Empirical confirmation of RPS dynamics in side-blotched lizards. Three male morphs cycle in frequency.

**Clutton-Brock, T.H. & Parker, G.A. (1995)** "Punishment in Animal Societies." *Nature* 373: 209-216.
→ Punishment as an evolutionary mechanism for cooperation enforcement in animal groups.
