# Sources — Population Dynamics

## Primary Texts

**Sandholm, W.H. *Population Games and Evolutionary Dynamics* (MIT Press, 2010)**
The modern definitive reference. Covers population games, revision protocols (imitation, best response, logit, Smith, pairwise comparison), deterministic dynamics, stochastic dynamics, and convergence. Unified framework.
→ Used for: revision protocols, convergence results, modern stability concepts.

**Hofbauer, J. & Sigmund, K. *Evolutionary Games and Population Dynamics* (Cambridge, 1998)**
Mathematical treatment combining evolutionary game theory with ecological dynamics. Covers replicator dynamics, Lotka-Volterra connection, permanence, and heteroclinic cycles.
→ Used for: replicator equation analysis, phase portraits, Lotka-Volterra connection.

**Nowak, M.A. *Evolutionary Dynamics: Exploring the Equations of Life* (Harvard, 2006)**
Accessible treatment. Strong on finite population effects, Moran process, fixation probabilities, and spatial dynamics.
→ Used for: Moran process, fixation probability formulas, spatial games.

## Key Papers — Replicator Dynamics

**Taylor, P.D. & Jonker, L.B. (1978)** "Evolutionary Stable Strategies and Game Dynamics." *Mathematical Biosciences* 40: 145-156.
→ Introduces replicator dynamics. Proves ESS is asymptotically stable under replicator dynamics.

**Schuster, P. & Sigmund, K. (1983)** "Replicator Dynamics." *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 100: 533-538.
→ Establishes key properties: dominated strategy extinction, folk theorem, relationship between interior fixed points and Nash equilibria.

**Nachbar, J.H. (1990)** "Evolutionary Selection Dynamics in Games: Convergence and Limit Properties." *International Journal of Game Theory* 19: 59-89.
→ The folk theorem of evolutionary game theory: if replicator dynamics converge, the limit is a Nash equilibrium.

## Key Papers — Stochastic Evolution

**Kandori, M., Mailath, G.J. & Rob, R. (1993)** "Learning, Mutation, and Long Run Equilibria in Games." *Econometrica* 61: 29-56.
→ Stochastic stability in coordination games with bounded-rationality agents. Risk-dominant equilibrium is selected.

**Young, H.P. (1993)** "The Evolution of Conventions." *Econometrica* 61: 57-84.
→ Independent development of stochastic stability. Conventions emerge as stochastically stable states.

**Nowak, M.A., Sasaki, A., Taylor, C. & Fudenberg, D. (2004)** "Emergence of Cooperation and Evolutionary Stability in Finite Populations." *Nature* 428: 646-650.
→ The 1/3 rule for finite populations. Selection favors cooperation when cooperators do well against a population that is 1/3 their type.

## Key Papers — Spatial and Network Dynamics

**Nowak, M.A. & May, R.M. (1992)** "Evolutionary Games and Spatial Chaos." *Nature* 359: 826-829.
→ Cooperation survives on lattices even in the PD. Spatial structure fundamentally changes evolutionary dynamics.

**Santos, F.C. & Pacheco, J.M. (2005)** "Scale-Free Networks Provide a Unifying Framework for the Emergence of Cooperation." *Physical Review Letters* 95: 098104.
→ Cooperation flourishes on scale-free networks. Network structure promotes cooperation more than regular lattices.

## Key Papers — Multi-Population and Asymmetric

**Schuster, P., Sigmund, K. & Wolff, R. (1979)** "Dynamical Systems Under Constant Organization. III: Cooperative and Competitive Behavior of Hypercycles." *Journal of Differential Equations* 32: 357-368.
→ Multi-species replicator dynamics and the connection to hypercycle theory.

**Cressman, R. *Evolutionary Dynamics and Extensive Form Games* (MIT Press, 2003)**
→ Extends evolutionary dynamics to extensive-form games and asymmetric interactions.
