# Diplomacy And International Order — Quick Reference


## The Three Schools of International Relations

| School | Core Claim | Key Thinkers | Explains Well | Explains Poorly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Realism** | States pursue power in an anarchic system; cooperation is temporary | Thucydides, Machiavelli, Morgenthau, Mearsheimer | Arms races, security dilemmas, great power competition | International institutions, norms, cooperation without coercion |
| **Liberalism** | Institutions, trade, and democracy reduce conflict | Kant, Keohane, Nye | EU integration, trade interdependence, democratic peace | Why democracies still fight, institutional failure |
| **Constructivism** | Ideas, norms, and identity shape state behavior | Wendt, Finnemore | Norm diffusion (human rights, sovereignty), identity-driven conflict | Material power dynamics, rapid change |

## Key International Orders

| Order | Period | Mechanism | Why It Ended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Concert of Europe** | 1815 – 1854 | Great power consensus, legitimist ideology | Crimean War broke the consensus |
| **Bismarckian system** | 1871 – 1890 | Complex alliance management by one statesman | Bismarck's dismissal; alliance rigidity |
| **Versailles order** | 1919 – 1939 | Collective security via League of Nations | US non-participation, failure to enforce |
| **Bipolar Cold War** | 1947 – 1991 | Nuclear deterrence, ideological competition | Soviet collapse |
| **Unipolar moment** | 1991 – ~2008 | US hegemony, liberal institutionalism | Iraq War overreach, financial crisis, rise of China |
| **Current transition** | 2008 – present | Contested; multipolar? bipolar? fragmented? | Ongoing |
