# Americas and Oceania — The Other Hemispheres

The Western Hemisphere and the Pacific world developed independently from Afro-Eurasia for thousands of years, producing civilizations of extraordinary sophistication — the Maya mathematical system, the Inca road network, Polynesian navigation across thousands of miles of open ocean. The Columbian Exchange (1492) violently connected these worlds, producing the most consequential biological, demographic, and cultural exchange in human history.

## When This Applies

- User asks about pre-Columbian civilizations (Aztec, Inca, Maya, Mississippian, etc.)
- User asks about the Columbian Exchange or European colonization of the Americas
- User asks about independence movements in Latin America or North America
- User asks about Polynesian navigation or Pacific Island societies
- User asks about settler colonialism and indigenous displacement

## Regional Timeline

| Period | Americas | Pacific/Oceania |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-contact** (15,000 BCE-1492) | Migration from Asia; development of agriculture (maize, potatoes); Maya, Aztec, Inca, Mississippian, Pueblo, Amazonian civilizations | Austronesian expansion (~3000 BCE); settlement of Polynesia (500 BCE-1200 CE); Polynesian navigation to Hawaii, New Zealand, Easter Island |
| **Contact and conquest** (1492-1600) | Columbus; Cortes and the Aztecs (1519-21); Pizarro and the Inca (1532); demographic collapse (50-90% from disease) | Magellan (1521); Spanish Philippines; limited initial contact |
| **Colonial** (1600-1800) | Spanish viceroyalties; Portuguese Brazil; British/French North America; plantation slavery; mission system | Dutch East Indies; British Australia (1788); increasing European contact |
| **Independence** (1776-1830s) | American Revolution (1776); Haitian Revolution (1791-1804); Latin American independence (Bolivar, San Martin, 1810s-20s) | Continued colonization; Maori resistance |
| **Nation-building** (1830s-present) | US expansion; Mexican-American War; US Civil War; Latin American caudillismo; 20th c. revolutions (Mexico, Cuba); Cold War interventions | Australian/NZ independence; Pacific WWII theater; decolonization; nuclear testing; climate vulnerability |

## Pre-Columbian Civilizations

| Civilization | Region | Peak | Key Achievements |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maya** | Mesoamerica | 250-900 CE | Writing system; base-20 mathematics with zero; astronomical precision; monumental architecture |
| **Aztec (Mexica)** | Central Mexico | 1325-1521 | Tenochtitlan (~200,000 people, larger than most European cities); tributary empire; chinampas (floating gardens) |
| **Inca** | Andes | 1438-1533 | Largest pre-Columbian empire; 25,000+ miles of roads; quipu record-keeping; vertical archipelago agriculture |
| **Mississippian** | North America | 800-1600 | Cahokia (~20,000 people, largest city in North America before Philadelphia); mound building; trade networks |
| **Polynesian** | Pacific | 500 BCE-1200 CE | Navigated thousands of miles of open ocean using stars, waves, and winds; settled the most dispersed civilization on Earth |

## The Columbian Exchange

The biological reconnection of the hemispheres after 1492 was the most consequential event in environmental and demographic history:

**Americas → Eurasia**: Potatoes, maize, tomatoes, cacao, tobacco, rubber, chili peppers, squash — these crops transformed Old World agriculture and nutrition. Potatoes alone enabled European population growth.

**Eurasia → Americas**: Wheat, horses, cattle, pigs, sheep — but above all, *disease*. Smallpox, measles, influenza, and typhus killed an estimated 50-90% of indigenous American populations. This demographic catastrophe is the single most important fact for understanding everything that followed in the Americas.

**The asymmetry**: Why did Old World diseases devastate the Americas but not vice versa? The answer is livestock domestication: Eurasians lived in close proximity to cattle, pigs, and chickens for millennia, generating zoonotic diseases and developing partial immunity. The Americas had far fewer domesticable animals.

## Connected Histories

- **To Africa**: Atlantic slave trade created Afro-American cultures; racial hierarchies shaped both continents
- **To Europe**: Colonial extraction funded European development; independence movements inspired European liberalism
- **To Asia-Pacific**: Manila Galleon trade (1565-1815) connected Americas to Asia; Asian immigration shaped Pacific coast
- **Within the Pacific**: Polynesian navigation networks; colonial disruption; nuclear testing; climate change vulnerability

## Diagnostic Framework

When analyzing American or Pacific history:

1. **What existed before European contact?** (Always start with indigenous civilizations, not European "discovery")
2. **What was the demographic impact of contact?** (Disease is usually the most important factor)
3. **What type of colonialism?** (Settler vs. extractive — profoundly different consequences)
4. **What is the indigenous agency?** (Resistance, adaptation, survival, cultural persistence)
5. **How does this connect to the Atlantic/Pacific world?** (No hemisphere is an island)
