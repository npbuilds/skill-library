# Periodization Conventions

When wan-shi-tong divides history into periods, the choice of scheme is *itself* an interpretation. This doc names the major schemes, their use cases, their hidden assumptions, and the rules for choosing — and flagging the choice — explicitly.

## Why Periodization Matters

Periodization is not chronology. Chronology is dates. Periodization is the act of grouping years together and naming the group ("Renaissance," "Tang Dynasty," "Pre-Columbian"). Every grouping embeds a thesis about which forces matter and which transitions are real.

Western "ancient → medieval → modern" assumes that European chronology is the universal armature. It badly distorts:

- **China** — where dynastic cycles are the lived periodization
- **Islamic world** — where Hijri dating and dynastic shifts (Rashidun → Umayyad → Abbasid → ...) are the working frame
- **Indigenous Americas** — where pre/post-1492 is the only externally-imposed division that matters, and pre-1492 is its own complex periodization
- **Sub-Saharan Africa** — where most "medieval" maps awkwardly; major polities (Mali, Great Zimbabwe, Aksum) are misframed by European time

## Major Schemes

### 1. Western "Tripartite" (Ancient / Medieval / Modern)

| Period | Span (CE) | Defining transition |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient | – ~500 | Fall of Western Rome |
| Medieval | 500 – 1500 | Renaissance / Reformation / 1492 |
| Modern | 1500 – present | (Often subdivided: early modern, modern, contemporary) |

**Use when:** Discussing Mediterranean / European history specifically.
**Avoid when:** Discussing East Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, the Americas, the Islamic world.
**Hidden assumption:** That the Roman trajectory is the universal arc.

### 2. Six-Register Trans-Temporal (Wan Shi Tong default)

| Register | Span |
|---|---|
| Deep | 300,000 BCE – 10,000 BCE |
| Ancient | 10,000 BCE – 500 CE |
| Post-classical | 500 – 1500 CE |
| Early Modern | 1500 – 1800 |
| Modern | 1800 – 1945 |
| Contemporary | 1945 – present |

**Use when:** Default for cross-civilizational analysis.
**Why "post-classical" not "medieval":** The "middle" framing is Eurocentric; "post-classical" centers the persistence of classical traditions while staying neutral about what comes next.
**Hidden assumption:** Still privileges global synchrony — most regions don't actually transition cleanly at 500 or 1500.

### 3. Chinese Dynastic

| Era | Span | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-imperial | – 221 BCE | Shang, Zhou, Warring States |
| Early Imperial | 221 BCE – 220 CE | Qin, Han |
| Period of Disunion | 220 – 589 | Three Kingdoms, North/South divisions |
| Sui-Tang | 589 – 907 | Reunification, classical peak |
| Song | 960 – 1279 | Commercial revolution, Neo-Confucian synthesis |
| Yuan | 1271 – 1368 | Mongol rule |
| Ming | 1368 – 1644 | Han restoration |
| Qing | 1644 – 1911 | Manchu rule, max territorial extent |
| Modern | 1911 – present | Republic → PRC |

**Use when:** Discussing East Asian history (China, Korea, Vietnam, sometimes Japan).
**Hidden assumption:** That dynastic transitions are the major causal joints (often true, sometimes overstated — Tang/Song transition is more important than dynasty alone implies; Ming/Qing less radical than dynasty alone implies).

### 4. Islamic / Hijri

| Era | Span (CE) |
|---|---|
| Pre-Islamic | – 622 CE |
| Rashidun Caliphate | 632 – 661 |
| Umayyad | 661 – 750 |
| Abbasid | 750 – 1258 |
| Fragmentation | 1258 – 1517 |
| Ottoman / Safavid / Mughal "Gunpowder Empires" | 1517 – 1800 |
| Colonial / Modern | 1800 – present |

**Hijri dates** are AH (Anno Hegirae) from 622 CE. Use AH alongside CE when working in primarily Islamic contexts (e.g., "1258 CE / 656 AH").
**Use when:** Discussing the Islamic world from Iberia to Indonesia.
**Hidden assumption:** That religion-political dynastic shifts are the central organizing principle (true for governance, less true for popular religion or material culture).

### 5. Indigenous Americas

| Era | Span (BP / CE) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Paleoindian | 15,000+ BP – 9,000 BP | First peopling |
| Archaic | 9,000 – 3,000 BP | Pre-agricultural |
| Formative / Preclassic | 2000 BCE – 250 CE | Olmec, early urbanism |
| Classic | 250 – 900 CE | Maya peak, Teotihuacan, Moche |
| Postclassic | 900 – 1521 CE | Toltec, Aztec, Inca |
| Colonial | 1521 – 1810s | Spanish/Portuguese rule |
| Modern | 1810s – present | Independence, indigenous resurgence |

**Use when:** Discussing pre-Columbian or early colonial Mesoamerica / Andes.
**Hidden assumption:** "Classic" and "Postclassic" are archaeological conventions inherited from Mayanist scholarship — they don't map cleanly onto Andean or North American sequences. Use region-specific subdivisions when possible (e.g., Inca-specific: Late Horizon).

### 6. African Periodizations (region-specific)

There is no single African periodization. Use region-specific:

| Region | Working scheme |
|---|---|
| Sahel / West Africa | Ghana → Mali → Songhai (Empires of the Sudan), then colonial / postcolonial |
| Ethiopia | Aksumite → Zagwe → Solomonic → modern |
| Swahili coast | Pre-Islamic → city-state era (10th–16th c.) → Portuguese / Omani interventions → colonial |
| Sub-equatorial Africa | Pre-Bantu → Bantu expansion → state formation (Great Zimbabwe, Kongo, Buganda) → colonial / postcolonial |

**Use when:** Discussing any African history. Avoid imposing European "medieval / modern" terms.

### 7. Indian Subcontinent

| Era | Span | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Indus Valley | 3300 – 1300 BCE | |
| Vedic | 1500 – 500 BCE | |
| Mahajanapada / Mauryan | 600 BCE – 184 BCE | |
| Classical | 320 BCE – 550 CE | Maurya, Gupta |
| Early Medieval / Regional Kingdoms | 550 – 1200 | |
| Sultanate Period | 1206 – 1526 | Delhi Sultanate, regional sultanates |
| Mughal | 1526 – 1857 | |
| Colonial | 1757 – 1947 | British East India Company → Raj |
| Independent | 1947 – present | India / Pakistan / Bangladesh |

**Hidden assumption:** "Classical" and "Medieval" are British-colonial-era impositions; they shape how Indian history is taught but don't fully fit Sanskrit sources' own self-periodization.

### 8. World-Systems / Wallersteinian

| Era | Defining feature |
|---|---|
| Pre-capitalist world-empires | – ~1500 |
| Long 16th century | 1450 – 1640 (Wallerstein's birth of the modern world-system) |
| Mercantile / Dutch hegemony | 1640 – 1815 |
| Industrial / British hegemony | 1815 – 1914 |
| 20th century / US hegemony | 1914 – ~2008 |
| Multipolar / contested | 2008 – present |

**Use when:** Doing world-systems analysis (Wallerstein, Arrighi, Frank).
**Hidden assumption:** That hegemonic cycles are the central armature. Useful for finance / trade analysis, less useful for cultural or intellectual history.

### 9. Geological / Anthropocene

| Era | Span | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Holocene | 11,700 BP – ? | Stable interglacial; agricultural civilizations |
| Anthropocene (proposed) | ? – present | Onset contested: 8,000 BP / 1610 / 1784 / 1950 |

**Use when:** Doing environmental history or deep history.
**See:** `historiographical-stance.md` Example 5 for the contested Anthropocene start date.

## Decision Rules

### Rule 1 — Match the scheme to the region

Default to region-native periodization when discussing a single region:
- China → dynastic
- Islamic world → Hijri / dynastic
- Pre-Columbian Americas → Mesoamerican or Andean archaeological sequences
- Africa → region-specific
- Europe → tripartite or six-register

### Rule 2 — Match the scheme to the analytical lens

- Trade / finance → world-systems
- Environment / deep time → geological / Anthropocene
- Cross-civilizational comparison → six-register trans-temporal
- Political / dynastic continuity → region-native dynastic
- Religious / intellectual currents → use both region-native and trans-religious (e.g., "Axial Age")

### Rule 3 — Flag the choice when it could matter

If the choice of scheme materially shapes the answer, name it. Examples:

> "Using the Western tripartite, the 'medieval' Islamic world flourished — but if we use Hijri dynastic, this is the Abbasid Golden Age, and the framing 'medieval' obscures what's actually happening: a peak, not a middle."

> "If we periodize by political dynasty (Ming → Qing), the 1644 transition looks epochal; if we periodize by economic regime, the silver-driven commercialization of the Yangzi Delta runs unbroken from the 1500s into the 1700s."

### Rule 4 — Avoid loaded labels

| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dark Ages | post-classical / early medieval | Ignores Islamic Golden Age, Tang flourishing |
| Pre-Columbian "primitive" | Pre-Columbian Mesoamerican / Andean | Tenochtitlan was urban, Inca administration was complex |
| Discovery (of the Americas) | Encounter / contact | "Discovery" erases existing inhabitants |
| Oriental | East Asian / West Asian / South Asian | Said's *Orientalism* — the term is a political construction |
| Tribal (for sub-Saharan polities) | Region-specific (Mali, Kongo, etc.) | "Tribal" flattens state-level complexity |
| The Renaissance (as universal break) | Italian Renaissance / Northern Renaissance | Was not a globally significant transition |
| Age of Exploration | Age of European Maritime Expansion | Decenters the colonial frame |

### Rule 5 — Calendar dating

| Convention | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| BCE / CE | Default for global use | Religiously neutral relative to BC/AD |
| BP (before present) | Deep history, archaeology | "Present" conventionally = 1950 |
| AH | Islamic contexts | Cite alongside CE |
| Dynastic regnal years | Internal to a culture's own historiography | "Year 5 of Kangxi" — useful for source-citation precision |

## Output Pattern

When periodization is interpretively loaded, surface it explicitly:

```
[Default to the most appropriate scheme for the region/lens.]
[If using a non-default scheme, name it briefly: "Using the Hijri dynastic frame…"]
[If the choice changes the answer, contrast the two framings.]
[Always avoid loaded labels per Rule 4.]
```

## Worked Example

**Question:** "What was happening in the Islamic world during the European Middle Ages?"

> **First, reframe the question.** "European Middle Ages" (~500–1500 CE) corresponds to the Rashidun, Umayyad, and Abbasid Caliphates plus the post-Abbasid fragmentation. Calling this period "medieval" in the European sense badly misframes it — for the Islamic world this is foundational, then a peak (8th–13th c. Abbasid Golden Age), then a reorganization. Hijri dating: roughly Year 1 to Year 900 AH.
>
> [Then proceed with the substantive answer using Islamic-world-native periodization.]
