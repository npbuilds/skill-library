# Schools Of Thought — Quick Reference


## The Major Schools

| School | Period | Core Method | Key Figures | Strength | Blind Spot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rankean** | 1830s+ | Archival research; "wie es eigentlich gewesen" (as it actually was) | Ranke | Rigorous source criticism; established history as a discipline | Assumes objectivity is possible; ignores power in archive formation |
| **Marxist** | 1840s+ | Material conditions and class struggle drive history | Marx, Thompson, Hobsbawm | Reveals economic forces and inequality invisible to political history | Can reduce everything to class; underestimates culture and ideas |
| **Annales** | 1929+ | Long-term structures (longue duree); total history; quantitative methods | Bloch, Febvre, Braudel, Le Roy Ladurie | Reveals deep forces; crosses disciplinary boundaries | Can neglect agency and events; sometimes inaccessibly abstract |
| **Social history** | 1960s+ | History from below; ordinary people's experience | Thompson, Gutman, Zinn | Recovers voices excluded from political history | Can lose sight of power structures by focusing on experience |
| **Postcolonial** | 1970s+ | Critique of imperial knowledge; subaltern agency; decentering the West | Said, Spivak, Guha, Chakrabarty | Reveals how colonialism shaped knowledge production itself | Risk of relativism; "the subaltern cannot speak" problem |
| **Gender/feminist** | 1970s+ | Gender as analytical category; women's experience and agency | Scott, Davis, Ulrich | Transforms every field by asking "where are the women?" | Early work focused on white Western women; intersectionality corrects this |
| **Microhistory** | 1970s+ | Intensive study of a single case to reveal larger structures | Ginzburg, Davis, Darnton | Vivid, accessible; reveals complexity invisible at macro scale | Risk of unrepresentative cases; generalization is difficult |
| **Linguistic turn** | 1980s+ | Language and discourse constitute reality, not just reflect it | White, LaCapra | Reveals how narrative shapes meaning; challenges naive realism | Can lose connection to material reality; "everything is text" overreach |
| **Digital humanities** | 2000s+ | Computational methods: text mining, GIS, network analysis, visualization | Moretti, White (Stanford) | Reveals patterns invisible to human reading; handles massive datasets | Risk of algorithmic reductionism; requires technical skills |
