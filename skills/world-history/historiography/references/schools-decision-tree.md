# Schools Decision Tree

Which interpretive lens to reach for when a historiographical question arrives. Eight major schools, what each centers, what each ignores, and when each is the right tool.

## The Eight Schools (in rough chronological emergence)

| School | Centers | Tends to ignore | Native question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rankean / Empiricist** | Documentary evidence; "wie es eigentlich gewesen" | Subaltern voices, structural forces | "What actually happened, per the sources?" |
| **Marxist** | Class conflict, modes of production, material base | Culture-as-autonomous, individual agency | "Whose material interests did this serve?" |
| **Annales** | Long-term structures (*longue durée*), mentalités, geography | Events, individuals, narrative | "What were the slow forces shaping this?" |
| **Subaltern / Postcolonial** | Colonized voices, resistance, epistemic violence | Elite cohesion, sometimes elite agency entirely | "Whose voice has the archive silenced?" |
| **World-Systems** | Core/periphery dynamics, hegemonic cycles | Local agency, cultural specificity | "How does this fit the world-system's structure?" |
| **Cultural Turn** | Discourse, representation, meaning-making | Material constraints, structural power | "What does this *mean* in its symbolic system?" |
| **Microhistory** | A single case, deeply contextualized | Generalization, structure | "What does this one weird case reveal?" |
| **Gender / Feminist** | Gender as analytical category, women's experience | Sometimes class or race when treated alone | "How does gender structure this?" |

These are not mutually exclusive. Most contemporary historians blend several.

## The Decision Tree

```
What is the question really asking?
│
├── "What happened, per the sources?"
│       └─► Rankean / Empiricist (start here for Establishing facts)
│
├── "Whose interests did this serve?" / "Class dynamics?"
│       └─► Marxist
│
├── "What were the slow forces?" / "Mentalités?" / "Geographic deep structure?"
│       └─► Annales
│
├── "Whose voice is missing?" / "Colonial / racial structure?"
│       └─► Subaltern / Postcolonial
│
├── "How does this fit global / hegemonic patterns?"
│       └─► World-Systems
│
├── "What did this *mean* to participants?" / "Symbolic system?"
│       └─► Cultural Turn
│
├── "What does this one strange case reveal about the larger system?"
│       └─► Microhistory
│
├── "How does gender structure this?" / "Where are the women?"
│       └─► Gender / Feminist
│
└── "I don't know — surface the disagreement among schools"
        └─► Show 2–3 schools' takes, name the stakes
```

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — "Why did the French Revolution happen?"

| School | Take |
|---|---|
| Rankean | Begin from documentary record: cahiers de doléances, royal financial papers, Constituent Assembly debates. The crisis was first fiscal. |
| Marxist | Bourgeois revolution dismantling feudal property; the Estates General is class conflict made visible. (Soboul, Lefebvre.) |
| Annales | Long-term price-series shifts, demographic pressure, the *mentalités* of Old Regime peasants. (Le Roy Ladurie.) |
| Cultural Turn | The Revolution is a contest over symbols and discourse — Lynn Hunt on family romance; Furet on revolutionary language as autonomous force. |
| Gender | Joan Scott on gender and the rights of citizenship; the Revolution was structured by who could be a "citizen." |

**Which to lead with?** Depends on the user's question. "What caused it?" → Annales + Marxist. "What did it mean?" → Cultural Turn + Gender. "What happened?" → Rankean + a touch of all the others.

### Example 2 — "Why did the Industrial Revolution happen in Britain?"

| School | Take |
|---|---|
| Rankean | Trace the documentary record: patents, factory records, parliamentary commissions. |
| Marxist | Primitive accumulation, enclosure, proletarianization, capital concentration. (Engels, Hobsbawm.) |
| Annales | Long-run agricultural productivity, demographic recovery, energy regimes. |
| World-Systems | Britain's core position in the Atlantic system; New World resources; slavery's role. (Wallerstein, Beckert.) |
| Cultural Turn | Enlightenment knowledge culture, Mokyr's "Industrial Enlightenment." |
| Gender | Women's labor in proto-industry and early factories; the rewriting of public/private spheres. |

**Note:** No single school explains the Industrial Revolution well. The strongest contemporary syntheses (Allen, Mokyr) explicitly mix Annales (energy regimes), Marxist (institutional transformation), and Rankean (technological detail).

### Example 3 — "What was life like for an enslaved woman in 18th-century Saint-Domingue?"

| School | Take |
|---|---|
| Rankean | Limited — most enslaved women left no documentary record. |
| Marxist | Plantation as unit of capitalist production; gendered labor regime. |
| Subaltern / Postcolonial | Center the systematic silencing of enslaved voices. (Trouillot, *Silencing the Past*.) |
| Cultural Turn | Recover meaning through ritual, religion (vodou), naming practices. |
| Gender | Marisa Fuentes, *Dispossessed Lives* — methodological tools for reading the archive's silences against itself. |
| Microhistory | Single-case deep dives on individual women whose names appear in court or plantation records. |

**Lead with:** Subaltern + Gender + Microhistory. This is exactly the kind of question where Rankean alone fails badly — "the sources don't tell us" misreads as "we cannot know," when the right move is to read the archive *against itself* for the silences.

### Example 4 — "Why did the Roman Empire fall?"

| School | Take |
|---|---|
| Rankean | Source-driven narrative of late antique military and political crisis. (Heather.) |
| Marxist | Crisis of the slave mode of production; transition to feudalism. (Anderson, *Passages from Antiquity*.) |
| Annales | Long-run climate (Late Antique Little Ice Age), plague cycles (Antonine, Justinianic), demographic exhaustion. (Harper, *The Fate of Rome*.) |
| World-Systems | Rome as core of a Mediterranean system; collapse as system contraction. |
| Cultural Turn | Late antique transformation of religious sensibility; "fall" reframed as "transformation." (Peter Brown.) |
| Microhistory | Individual estates, villages, cities — Wickham's bottom-up reconstruction. |

**Lead with:** Annales + Rankean + Cultural Turn. Notice that the contemporary mainstream synthesis (Wickham, Heather, Harper) blends 3+ schools.

### Example 5 — "What does the menocchio case (Carlo Ginzburg's *The Cheese and the Worms*) tell us?"

| School | Take |
|---|---|
| Microhistory | This is the textbook example: one 16th-century Friulian miller's cosmology, recovered from Inquisition transcripts, opens onto popular culture and the limits of elite documentation. |
| Cultural Turn | Menocchio's worldview reveals the *mentalités* of pre-modern popular religion. |
| Rankean | The Inquisition transcripts are the primary source backbone. |

**Lead with:** Microhistory + Cultural Turn. This is microhistory's native register.

## Choosing Lenses for Multi-School Questions

When a question genuinely needs multiple schools:

1. **Identify the question's grain.** Big-structure question → Annales / World-Systems / Marxist. Meaning question → Cultural Turn / Gender. Voice question → Subaltern. Single-case question → Microhistory. Documentary-record question → Rankean.
2. **Pick the school that frames the question.** That's primary.
3. **Pick 1–2 schools that complicate or supplement.** Those are supporting.
4. **Name the stakes of the disagreement** if there is one.

## When NOT to Use a School

| School | Don't use when |
|---|---|
| Rankean | Question is interpretive, not documentary |
| Marxist | Question is genuinely about meaning rather than material structure |
| Annales | Question is about a specific event or individual decision |
| Subaltern | Question is about elite cohesion or institutional structure |
| World-Systems | Question is about local agency or cultural specificity |
| Cultural Turn | Question is about material constraints |
| Microhistory | Question requires generalization rather than depth |
| Gender | (Almost always relevant — but center other schools when gender is genuinely epiphenomenal to the specific question) |

## Conflict Resolution Among Schools

When two schools disagree:

1. **Empirical disagreement** (different evidence weighting) → Surface the evidence, note which school's frame fits the better-supported reading.
2. **Methodological disagreement** (different sources count) → Surface the methodological commitment of each school.
3. **Political disagreement** (different normative commitments) → Name the politics; do not pretend it's a neutral evidentiary dispute.

The Scriptorium does not adjudicate; it *names the disagreement* and *teaches the user to see it*.
