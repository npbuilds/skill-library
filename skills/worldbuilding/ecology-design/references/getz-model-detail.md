# Getz Biomass Transformation Web — Detailed Reference

The full 10-niche model for designing complete ecosystems.

## The Three Axes

**Axis 1: Mobility**
- Gatherer (G) — mobile, seeks food actively
- Miner (M) — stationary or slow, waits for food to come

**Axis 2: Trophic Source**
- Autotroph consumer — eats plants/producers
- Heterotroph consumer — eats animals
- Saprovore — eats dead/decaying matter

**Axis 3: Decomposition Role**
- Live consumer — eats living organisms
- Dead consumer — eats recently dead organisms
- Particle consumer — processes detritus and waste

## The 10 Niches in Detail

### 1. Victivore (Gatherer + Plant-eater)
Mobile herbivores. The base of most food chains above plants.
- **Real**: Deer, rabbits, grasshoppers, bison, elephants
- **Ecological role**: Controls plant growth; shapes landscape through grazing
- **Fantasy hook**: What if the dominant victivore is enormous? Landscapes shaped by megafauna grazing.

### 2. Lectivore (Miner + Plant-eater)
Stationary or slow herbivores. Often overlooked but critical.
- **Real**: Caterpillars, aphids, leaf miners, bark beetles
- **Ecological role**: Targeted plant damage; drives plant defense evolution
- **Fantasy hook**: Organisms that embed in magical plants, slowly draining their properties.

### 3. Bestivore (Gatherer + Animal-eater)
Mobile predators. The dramatic niche — but not the most important one.
- **Real**: Wolves, hawks, spiders, orcas
- **Ecological role**: Controls herbivore populations; maintains ecosystem balance
- **Fantasy hook**: Predators that have evolved to hunt magic-users specifically.

### 4. Zotanophage (Miner + Animal-eater)
Stationary predators. Ambush specialists.
- **Real**: Barnacles, sea anemones, venus flytraps, antlions
- **Ecological role**: Controls small mobile organisms; creates danger zones
- **Fantasy hook**: Organisms in ruins or magical sites that trap the unwary.

### 5. Carcasivore (Gatherer + Dead-eater)
Mobile scavengers. The cleanup crew.
- **Real**: Vultures, hyenas, crows, crabs
- **Ecological role**: Removes dead matter before it becomes a disease vector
- **Fantasy hook**: Creatures that follow wars and disasters; battlefield ecology.

### 6. Thanatophage (Miner + Dead-eater)
Stationary scavengers. Process death where it falls.
- **Real**: Dung beetles, carrion-feeding larvae, sexton beetles
- **Ecological role**: Localized decomposition; nutrient return to soil
- **Fantasy hook**: Organisms that grow on graves, in tombs, on abandoned ships.

### 7. Detritivore (Particle consumer — mobile)
Eats tiny particles of dead matter. The invisible foundation.
- **Real**: Earthworms, millipedes, woodlice
- **Ecological role**: Soil creation; nutrient cycling; aeration
- **Fantasy hook**: Without these, nothing rots into soil. Remove them and the world slowly dies.

### 8. Sarcophage (Parasite on living hosts)
Lives on or in living organisms, feeding without immediately killing.
- **Real**: Tapeworms, ticks, mistletoe, cordyceps
- **Ecological role**: Population control; drives immune system evolution
- **Fantasy hook**: Parasites that feed on magical energy — magic users as hosts.

### 9. Necrophage (Parasite on dead/dying hosts)
Colonizes organisms at or near death.
- **Real**: Certain fungi, blowfly larvae, screw-worms
- **Ecological role**: Accelerates decomposition of dying organisms
- **Fantasy hook**: Organisms that sense impending death; grow on the dying before they're fully dead.

### 10. Decomposer (Breaks down all dead matter)
The final processors. Without them, nothing recycles.
- **Real**: Fungi, bacteria, actinomycetes
- **Ecological role**: Returns ALL nutrients to the cycle; without decomposers, life stops
- **Fantasy hook**: If the fantasy system affects decomposition (e.g., magic preserves corpses), the entire nutrient cycle breaks. Massive worldbuilding implications.

## Designing a Complete Ecosystem

### Step 1: Start with the energy source
- What powers the base of the food web? (Sunlight? Magical energy? Geothermal? Chemical?)

### Step 2: Define the producers
- What organisms convert raw energy into biomass? (Plants? Crystal-organisms? Chemosynthetic bacteria?)

### Step 3: Fill each niche
- For each of the 10 niches, define at least one organism (even if unnamed)
- Ask: what does this organism eat? What eats it? How does it reproduce?

### Step 4: Add the fantasy element
- How does the magic system interact with each niche?
- Are there new niches created by the fantasy system?
- Are any niches suppressed or distorted?

### Step 5: Check the web
- Does energy flow from producers through consumers to decomposers?
- Can dead matter be recycled back into the system?
- Is there at least one predator for each prey? (Or explain why not)
- Is there at least one decomposer path for each type of dead matter?

## Quick Ecosystem Templates

### Forest Ecosystem
Producers: trees, understory plants, fungi
Key niches: Lectivores (caterpillars on leaves), Bestivores (owls, foxes), Decomposers (forest floor fungi), Detritivores (earthworms)

### Ocean Ecosystem
Producers: phytoplankton, kelp, coral symbiotes
Key niches: Zotanophages (filter feeders, anemones), Carcasivores (deep-sea scavengers), Decomposers (marine bacteria)

### Desert Ecosystem
Producers: cacti, drought-adapted grasses
Key niches: Victivores (small rodents), Bestivores (raptors, snakes), Thanatophages (beetles), minimal Decomposers (slow cycling)

### Magical Ecosystem
Producers: organisms that convert ambient magic into biomass
Key niches: Sarcophages (magic-parasites on users), new niche — "Thaumavores" (organisms that eat pure magical energy), Decomposers may be enhanced or suppressed by magic
