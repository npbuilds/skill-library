# Sensory Worldbuilding — What the World Feels Like

Your world has geography, cultures, and systems. Can you tell me what it *smells* like? What the air feels like at dawn? What sounds fill the market? Structural worldbuilding creates the skeleton. Sensory worldbuilding gives it skin.

## The Five Channels

### Sound
Ambient sound determines emotional atmosphere before anything else. Design: ambient baseline, time-marking sounds (bells, calls, birdsong), danger signals (silence, specific insects), music and instruments, volume of daily life.

### Smell
Most memory-linked sense. A single smell establishes place more powerfully than visual paragraphs. Design: city/village/wilderness scent, status markers (perfume vs. labor sweat), seasonal smells, culturally significant scents.

### Touch/Texture
Physical experience of moving through the world. Design: surface materials (worn stone, rough timber, flexing organic materials), climate feel (humid, dry, gritty wind), clothing against skin, food texture.

### Taste
Sparingly but powerfully — food and drink are the most culturally specific sensory experiences. Design: local cuisine flavor profile, beverages, air taste (salt, dust, metallic).

### Proprioception
The body's sense in space — overlooked but powerful. Design: narrow vs. vast spaces, elevation changes, how the fantasy system feels physically, architectural scale.

## The Sensory Signature

Every location needs a **sensory signature** — 2-3 details that immediately identify it.

| Location | Signature |
|----------|-----------|
| Desert trading city | Cardamom + camel dung, fabric snapping in wind, sand in everything |
| Underground hold | Echoing hammers, hot metal + mineral water, constant cool |
| Magical academy | Ozone taste, edge-of-hearing hum, skin prickling near spells |
| Coastal village | Salt air, seabird cries, rough rope on hands |

**Test**: Can a reader identify where they are from sensory details alone?

## Sensory Design by Culture

Different cultures produce different sensory environments — because values, technologies, and relationships to the physical world differ. Sensory worldbuilding is not applied after cultural design; it's *derived* from it.

## Relationship to Other Skills
**From worldbuilding**: geography-ecology (climate determines baseline), cultures-societies (values shape built environment), technology-progression (tech level determines ambient sound and materials). **From writing**: concrete-detail (technique for rendering details), character-interiority (perception filter selects which details a character notices), figurative-language (sensory details become metaphorical through character), world-to-story (sensory details are the primary vehicle for immersive revelation).
