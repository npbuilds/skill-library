# Case-Study Library — Landmark Magic Systems

Twenty systems annotated against the six design axes (Source, Cost, Limits, Access, Interaction with Physics, Societal Integration). Distilled from the corpus review in the Obsidian vault: `skill-lab/2026-07-10-magic-systems-in-fantasy-research.md` (evidence tiers and citations there). Use these as calibration points when designing: find the two or three nearest neighbors to your concept and study what they paid for their effects.

## Cross-Case Patterns

1. **Every acclaimed system prices its magic; none differ on presence, only on kind** (moral / thermodynamic / material / social / cognitive). Cost-first design predates its Sanderson branding (Wrede's questionnaire; Le Guin's Taoist equilibrium).
2. **Convergence, refined:** systems that *solve plot problems* cluster at hard-rules-plus-preserved-mystery (Rowling per Sanderson's own essay, Kingkiller, Nen). Systems exempt themselves by never letting magic be the solution (Tolkien, JS&MN, Malazan).
3. **The societal-integration axis is the best predictor of acclaim** in this corpus: Fulcrum, Roke, Final Empire, consensus-reality Paradox — the systems remembered as great are the ones their societies are built from.
4. **Adaptation pressure:** any system built for play or serialization selects for administrable, meterable magic (Vancian slots, mana bars) — design for the medium's bookkeeping demands or they will redesign your system for you.
