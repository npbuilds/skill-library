# Religion Design Patterns

Structural templates for designing fictional religions.

## Deity Portfolio Framework

### Designing a Pantheon

**Step 1: Determine scope**
- Major deities: 2-6 (the ones most people know and worship)
- Intermediate: 3-8 per major deity (aspects, children, or specialists)
- Minor/local: as many as needed (patron saints of specific cities, trades, families)

**Step 2: Assign domains**
Every deity needs a primary domain and 1-2 secondary domains. Domains should cover the culture's essential concerns:

| Essential Concerns | Example Domains |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Survival | Agriculture, hunting, weather, sea, health |
| Social order | Justice, war, kingship, law, commerce |
| Life transitions | Birth, marriage, death, coming-of-age |
| Knowledge | Wisdom, prophecy, secrets, crafts, magic |
| Nature | Sun, moon, earth, storms, animals, forests |
| Abstract | Love, beauty, fate, chaos, time, dreams |

**Step 3: Define relationships**
Deities interact — alliances, rivalries, marriages, betrayals. These relationships mirror and sanctify mortal power dynamics.

| Relationship | Narrative Function |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Parent/child | Succession, authority, rebellion |
| Siblings | Rivalry, complementarity, betrayal |
| Lovers | Alliance, jealousy, creation myths |
| Enemies | Cosmic conflict that maps to mortal conflicts |
| Trickster/order | The exception that proves the rule |

## Organizational Templates

### Template: Hierarchical Church

```
Head of Faith (Pope/Patriarch/High Priest)
├── Council of [X] (Cardinals/Elders/Archpriests)
│   ├── Regional Leaders (Bishops/Abbots)
│   │   ├── Local Leaders (Priests/Monks)
│   │   │   └── Lay Members
│   │   └── Specialized Orders (Healers/Warriors/Scholars)
│   └── Inquisitorial/Doctrinal Branch
└── Monastic/Contemplative Branch (separate hierarchy)
```

**Power dynamics**: Top-down doctrine. Heresy is a political threat. Schisms split along lines of authority. Reform movements challenge the hierarchy itself.

### Template: Mystery Cult

```
Outer Circle (public devotees — know the surface teachings)
├── First Initiation (learn the first secret — Layer 1)
├── Second Initiation (learn deeper truths — Layer 2)
├── Inner Circle (the full teaching — Layer 3?)
└── The Keeper(s) (may know that even the inner teaching is incomplete)
```

**Power dynamics**: Knowledge IS power. Each initiation is a test. The uninitiated are controlled through ignorance. Maps perfectly to the revelation architecture.

### Template: Shamanic Tradition

```
No hierarchy. Individual practitioners.
├── The Gift (how one becomes a shaman — vision, illness, inheritance)
├── The Training (apprenticeship with an existing shaman)
├── The Practice (healing, divination, mediation with spirits)
└── The Community Role (advisor, healer, feared outsider)
```

**Power dynamics**: Personal authority, not institutional. Each shaman is unique. No doctrine to enforce. Hard for political power to co-opt (but they try).

### Template: Philosophical School

```
The Founder's Teachings (texts, sayings, principles)
├── Orthodox Interpretation (closest to original teaching)
├── Reform Interpretation (adapted to current conditions)
├── Syncretic Branch (merged with local traditions)
└── Radical Branch (took one idea to its logical extreme)
```

**Power dynamics**: Debate over interpretation. No single authority. Splits are intellectual, not institutional. Attracts intellectuals; dismissed by the powerful until it becomes politically useful.

## Sacred Calendar Design

A religion's calendar reveals its priorities:

| Element | Design Question |
|---------|----------------|
| **New Year** | When does it start? (Harvest? Solstice? A historical event?) |
| **Major festivals** | What events does the religion celebrate? (Creation? Victory? Renewal?) |
| **Fasting/abstinence** | When must devotees sacrifice? What do they give up? |
| **Holy days** | Weekly, monthly, or seasonal? How do they affect work and commerce? |
| **Mourning periods** | How is death commemorated? How long? What restrictions apply? |
| **Astronomical ties** | Linked to moon phases, solstices, equinoxes, star appearances? |

**Design rule**: The calendar should conflict with at least one other system (agricultural needs, military campaigns, trade schedules). Conflict between sacred and practical time creates narrative tension.

## Religion and Naming

How a religion names things reveals its theology:

- **Theophoric names**: Names containing a god's name (e.g., "Theodore" = "gift of God"). Common in cultures where divine favor matters.
- **Virtue names**: Names expressing desired qualities (Faith, Justice, Prudence). Common in moralistic religions.
- **Historical names**: Names from religious heroes/saints. Common in prophetic religions.
- **Nature names**: Names from sacred natural elements. Common in animistic traditions.
- **Taboo names**: Names that must NOT be used (the unspoken name of God). The taboo itself reveals what's sacred.
