# Religion Design — Quick Reference


## Quick Reference

| Pattern | Structure | Real Analog | World Implication |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Hierarchical** | Centralized authority, ranks, doctrine | Catholic Church | Can rival or merge with state power; orthodoxy vs heresy |
| **Congregational** | Autonomous local groups, shared tradition | Early Protestant churches | Resists centralization; adapts to local culture |
| **Mystery cult** | Initiation tiers, secret knowledge | Eleusinian Mysteries | Knowledge is power; revelation architecture maps directly |
| **Shamanic** | Individual practitioners, no institution | Indigenous traditions | Power is personal; no organization to rival the state |
| **Philosophical** | Ethical framework, minimal ritual | Buddhism, Stoicism | Shapes law and ethics more than politics |
| **State religion** | Government and religious authority merged | Imperial cult of Rome | No separation of spiritual and political power |
| **Prophetic** | Founded by a central figure, follows teachings | Islam, Christianity | Succession crises after the founder; schisms |
| **Ancestral** | Reverence for lineage, family-based practice | Chinese ancestor worship | Reinforces family structures; conservative tendency |
| **Animistic** | Everything has spirit; no separation of sacred/mundane | Shinto, many indigenous systems | Nature has agency; environmental ethics are built-in |
| **Syncretic** | Absorbs elements from other religions | Vodou, Bahá'í | Emerges at cultural contact points; adaptive |

## Quick Reference

| Model | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Universal** | One religion dominates all cultures | Worlds with a single dominant civilization or literal divine presence |
| **Overlapping** | Same gods, different cultural expressions | Worlds where cultures share mythic roots but diverged |
| **Contact** | Multiple pantheons, aware of each other | Worlds with active trade and cultural exchange |
| **No Contact** | Isolated religions, unaware of each other | Worlds with geographic barriers; discovery becomes a plot driver |
| **Competitive** | Religions actively converting and conflicting | Worlds where religious conflict drives politics and war |

## Quick Reference

| Category | Questions |
|----------|-----------|
| **Calendar** | What days are sacred? How does the religious calendar interact with agriculture, seasons, astronomical events? |
| **Rites of passage** | Birth, adulthood, marriage, death — what rituals mark these transitions? |
| **Daily practice** | Prayer, meditation, offerings, dietary rules — what does a devout person's day look like? |
| **Sacrifice** | What is given to the divine? Food, wealth, labor, blood, something symbolic? The sacrifice reveals what the culture values most. |
| **Sacred spaces** | Where is worship performed? Temples, natural sites, homes, anywhere? Architecture reflects theology. |
| **Forbidden acts** | Taboos — what absolutely cannot be done? Taboos reveal the culture's deepest fears. |
| **Sacred texts** | Written, oral, or embodied in practice? Who is authorized to interpret them? Control of interpretation is control of doctrine. |

## Quick Reference

| Relationship | Example | Implication |
|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **The system IS divine** | The Force, bending (Avatar) | Magic users are clergy or chosen; theological debates about proper use |
| **The system is SEPARATE** | Allomancy exists alongside religion | Religion may fear, regulate, or ignore the system |
| **The system DISPROVES the religion** | Magic exists but gods don't | Crisis of faith as a narrative theme |
| **The system CONFIRMS the religion** | The gods are real and demonstrable | Faith becomes knowledge; different theological problems |
| **The system IS UNKNOWN** | The religion explains the system incorrectly | Layer 0/Layer 3 tension; revelation architecture applies |
