# Conlang Craft — Extended Techniques & Practitioners

## Troemner's Reverse-Engineering Method

Generate vocabulary (even randomly), then look for phonetic similarities between unrelated words and infer cultural connections:

- **"Aeza" = miniature + dead** → Culture commemorates deceased by making tiny figurines for home shrines
- **"Beto" = sudden + receive** → Arid environment where abundance follows unpredictable rainstorms
- **"Bewu" = bracelet + peasant** → Visible class markers worn on the body; rigid social hierarchy
- **"Denu" = rose + harmony + accountant** → Accountant's guild adopted the rose; financial accuracy synonymous with beauty

Generated an intricate culture from scratch in under an hour.

## David Peterson's Methodology

**A priori method** (Dothraki): Start with canonical words from the source fiction, reverse-engineer a phonology that produces all of them, then expand outward.

**Historical method** (Valyrian): Create High Valyrian first, apply systematic sound changes to produce Low Valyrian as a daughter language. Low Valyrian plurals appear irregular because regular sound changes simplified different forms differently.

**Key principles:**
- Naturalism over cleverness — avoid cramming in every exotic feature
- Cultural embedding — grammar encodes worldview (Irathient uses vertical time: "after" from "above," "before" from "below")
- Start from constraints — work with what exists
- Irregularity through history — create regular proto-forms, let sound changes produce natural irregularity

## Practical Tiers for Fiction Writers

| Tier | Effort | What You Build | Who Needs This |
|------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| 1: Naming Language | Hours | Phonetic palette, syllable rules, naming conventions | Most novelists |
| 2: Phrase Language | Days-weeks | Basic grammar, 200-500 words, short dialogue | Fantasy with linguistic color |
| 3: Working Language | Months | Full grammar, 1000+ words, sample texts | TV/film, epic fantasy |
| 4: Tolkien-level | Years | Multiple related languages, etymologies, dialects | Language IS the project |

**Rules**: Keep words pronounceable. Shorter words for common use, longer for ceremony. Choose 1-3 real languages as phonetic inspiration. Use conlang sparingly in prose — a few well-placed words beat paragraphs of untranslated text.

## Writing System → Material → Culture

Writing medium shapes script form:
- **Stone** → angular lines (runes, ogham)
- **Brush on paper** → curves (Chinese, Arabic)
- **Clay tablets** → wedge shapes (cuneiform)
- **Palm leaves** → rounded shapes (to avoid tearing)

The material determines the script, which implies technological level and cultural priorities.

## Featural Systems

Korean Hangul: symbols encode phonetic features (voicing, place of articulation) in visual structure. Implies deliberate language planning — a reformist or revolutionary cultural moment. Contrast with organic scripts that evolved over centuries.

## Vocabulary Gaps as Anti-English

Unless you deliberately seed your lexicon with meaning-networks different from English, you risk making your conlang's semantics an unconscious copy. The antidote: decide what concepts your culture literally has no word for. A maritime culture might lack "desert" but have twelve wave-type words. A culture without private property might lack possessive pronouns.
