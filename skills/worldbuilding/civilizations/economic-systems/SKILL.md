# Economic Systems — What Gets Traded and Who Gets Rich

Economics is the substrate of power. Before politics, before war, before religion — someone had something someone else wanted. How that exchange is structured determines who has power, who doesn't, and what conflicts are inevitable. A world whose economy is an afterthought will feel hollow. A world whose economy drives faction dynamics, character motivation, and cultural identity will feel real.

This skill expands Pillar 2 (Economy) from the `cultures-societies` framework into a full design methodology.

## The Five Questions

Every fictional economy answers these, explicitly or implicitly:

### 1. What Is Scarce?

Scarcity is the engine of all economics. What's scarce determines what's valuable, which determines who has power.

| Scarcity Type | Examples | Narrative Effect |
|--------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Resource scarcity** | Water (Dune), fertile land, metal, fuel | Creates territorial conflict, trade dependencies, resource wars |
| **Fantasy system scarcity** | Magic costs life force, spellcasting materials are rare | Magic users become an economic class; magic becomes commodity |
| **Knowledge scarcity** | Literacy restricted, technical knowledge hoarded | Creates information asymmetry, priest/scholar caste power |
| **Labor scarcity** | Not enough people to work the fields/mines | Drives slavery, immigration, automation, social mobility |
| **Post-scarcity** | Replicators, unlimited energy (Banks' Culture) | Eliminates economic conflict; shifts drama to status, meaning, identity |

**The key insight**: Whatever is scarce becomes the axis of power. Change what's scarce and you change who rules.

### 2. How Is Value Exchanged?

| System | How It Works | Cultural Implication |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Barter** | Direct goods-for-goods exchange | Pre-monetary, small-scale, relationship-based |
| **Commodity money** | A physical good serves as currency (gold, salt, shells) | Value is tied to material reality; hoarding is possible |
| **Fiat currency** | Money by decree — coins, paper, tokens with no intrinsic value | Requires trust in the issuing authority; counterfeiting matters |
| **Debt/credit** | Value exists as obligation, not possession | Creates power asymmetry between creditors and debtors |
| **Gift economy** | Exchange through reciprocal gifting; refusal to give is social death | Creates dense social obligation networks; wealth is redistribution |
| **Reputation economy** | Standing, honor, social capital as currency | Status becomes the scarcity; competition is for attention and respect |
| **Soul/essence currency** | The Craft Sequence's soulstuff; magic-as-money | Makes the economic system literally metaphysical |
| **Labor tokens** | Work directly exchangeable for goods; no accumulation | Prevents hoarding; eliminates capital as a power base |

### 3. Who Controls Production?

| Control Pattern | Structure | Creates |
|----------------|-----------|---------|
| **Feudal** | Land owners control production; peasants work land | Rigid hierarchy, land as the ultimate asset |
| **Mercantile** | Merchant class controls trade routes and capital | Cities as power centers, guild systems, middle class |
| **Plantation/extraction** | Colonial power extracts resources from periphery | Core-periphery dynamics, forced labor, resistance |
| **Communal** | Community owns means of production collectively | Le Guin's Anarres — freedom vs. social pressure |
| **Fantasy-system-mediated** | Magic users control production directly | Mage-aristocracy, techno-magical inequality |
| **Corporate** | Organizations larger than states control resources | Cyberpunk dynamics, corporate citizenship |

### 4. How Does Geography Shape Trade?

Trade routes are the arteries of civilization. They determine which cities thrive, which cultures mix, and which factions gain leverage.

**Design principles**:
- **Resources cluster geographically** — mountains have ore, coasts have fish, valleys have grain. Trade exists because no region has everything.
- **Chokepoints create power** — whoever controls the mountain pass, the river crossing, the harbor controls trade. Control of trade IS political power.
- **Trade creates cultural mixing** — trading cities are cosmopolitan. Inland villages are insular. This is inevitable.
- **Trade routes shape alliances** — you don't go to war with your trading partner (usually). You go to war with whoever threatens your trade route.

### 5. What's the Relationship Between Wealth and Power?

This is where economics becomes politics:
- Can you buy political office? (Merchant republics)
- Does political power create wealth? (Feudal extraction)
- Are they separated? (Rarely, and always unstable)
- Can fantasy-system ability generate wealth? (Creates a magic-economy feedback loop)

## Innovative Currency Systems in Fiction

| Work | Currency | Why It Works |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Max Gladstone's *Craft Sequence* | Soulstuff — portions of your own life force | Makes economic transactions literally existential |
| Karina Sumner-Smith's *Towers* | Magic as the only currency that matters | The magically gifted are wealthy; the ungifted are homeless |
| Iain Banks' *Culture* | Post-scarcity — no currency at all | Eliminates economic plot drivers; forces character drama |
| Elizabeth Bear's *Eternal Sky* | Trade route control | Trade is money, money is armies, armies are power |
| Star Trek | Gold-pressed Latinum (unreplicatable) | Solves "why does money exist when you can replicate anything?" |
| Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* | No property, labor-based economy | Economic system IS the philosophical thought experiment |

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The "medieval gold" default**: Everyone uses gold coins. No one asks where the gold comes from, who mines it, or why it's valuable. Real medieval economies were far more complex — labor-in-kind, manorial obligation, church tithes.
2. **Infinite adventurer wealth**: Characters accumulate gold with no inflation effects, no taxation, no political consequences of sudden wealth.
3. **Everyone is a subsistence farmer except the protagonists**: Functional economies need artisans, merchants, administrators, entertainers, clergy, soldiers.
4. **Magic exists but doesn't affect the economy**: If wizards can create food, the agricultural economy collapses. If healers exist, the medical economy transforms. Every fantasy element has economic implications — trace them.
5. **Trade without trade routes**: Goods magically appear in markets with no explanation of how they got there from where they were produced.

## Relationship to Other Skills

- **Cultures-Societies**: This skill expands Pillar 2 (Economy). Load cultures-societies for the full seven-pillar framework; load this skill when economy needs depth.
- **Faction Design**: Factions often form around economic interests — trade guilds, labor movements, colonial enterprises. Economic structure determines what factions are possible.
- **Geography-Ecology**: Geography determines resource distribution, which determines trade routes, which determines economic power. Load geography before designing economy.
- **Magic-System-Design**: The fantasy system's relationship to economics must be explicitly designed. If magic can do economic work (heal, create, transport), it reshapes the entire economy.
- **Extrapolation Engine**: Run the "And Then What?" drill on economic axioms. If one resource is scarce, what cascading consequences follow?
- **Technology Progression**: Technology level constrains what economic systems are possible. No banking without literacy; no industrial economy without energy sources.

## When This Applies

- The user is designing a world's economy from scratch
- A culture's economic system needs to be more than three bullet points
- Scarcity is intended to drive the story's conflict
- Trade routes, currencies, or economic power dynamics are plot-relevant
- The user asks "how would this world's economy actually work?"
- A fantasy system exists and its economic implications haven't been traced
