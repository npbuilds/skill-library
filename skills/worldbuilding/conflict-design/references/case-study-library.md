# Conflict Case-Study Library

Conflicts annotated on the five axes (**Stakes / Cost / Lifecycle / Legitimacy / Termination**), each split into its stated (rational) face and felt (emotional) driver. Use these as calibration points: find the nearest neighbor to the conflict you're designing and study how its felt drivers align across the axes. Full evidence: `skill-lab/2026-07-19-conflict-design-research.md`; the fiction cases build on `skill-lab/2026-07-12-mecha-military-anime-narrative-research.md`.

## Historical / theoretical anchors

| Conflict pattern | Axis it illuminates | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **The security dilemma** (WWI-style spiral) | Lifecycle | Two status-quo powers spiral into a war neither wanted; you can't tell spiral (firmness inflames) from deterrence (softness emboldens) — the diagnostic uncertainty is the drama |
| **The blood feud** (Boehm, Montenegro) | Lifecycle (felt ratchet) | Reciprocal kin-vengeance self-sustains for generations — the real-world anchor for the cycle of hatred; feuds branch toward escalation or negotiated termination |
| **Cold War proxy wars** (stability-instability paradox) | Lifecycle | Lock the top rung (nuclear stalemate) and you *license* violence on the lower rungs — a direct design lever |
| **Civil-war demobilization** (Walter) | Termination | The killer is the moment of disarming: no one wants to lay down arms first, so settlements need a third-party guarantor |
| **The ripe moment** (Zartman) | Termination | Talks become possible only at a Mutually Hurting Stalemate + a perceived Way Out — and the moment must be *seized* or it passes |
| **Elite overproduction** (Turchin) | Metastability | Too many elite aspirants for the positions → intra-elite competition → the tip; the PSI decomposes the pressure (contested for modern states) |
| **1989 Eastern Europe** (Kuran) | Metastability | Preference falsification hid the opposition; a small reveal cascaded — why the collapse surprised everyone yet looks inevitable in hindsight |

## Reading the alignment

The fiction table's verdict column tracks one thing: **how completely the felt drivers align across the five axes.** LOGH and Attack on Titan align them fully (humiliation → willingness to pay → self-amplifying escalation → dynastic/identity legitimacy → sealed exit) — total, unbargainable wars. A conflict fought for divisible stakes with cheap exits and mercenary legitimacy is a limited one. When designing, decide the alignment first: it determines whether you're writing a war that can end or one that can't.
