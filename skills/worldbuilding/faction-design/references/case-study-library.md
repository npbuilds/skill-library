# Faction Case-Study Library — "No Clean Side" Warfare

Worked faction case studies from acclaimed military-political anime (Code Geass, Mobile Suit Gundam, Legend of the Galactic Heroes, 86), annotated on this skill's own tools: the **Four Essentials** (Want / Vulnerability / Internal tension / Public face vs. private reality), the **typed-edge relationships**, and **Power Vacuums**. Distilled from the corpus review in the Obsidian vault: `skill-lab/2026-07-12-mecha-military-anime-narrative-research.md` (evidence tiers and citations there). Use these as calibration points — especially against Common Mistakes #1 (monolithic factions) and #4 (factions that only oppose the protagonist), which these works are textbook counter-examples to.

## The Four Essentials, worked

| Faction (work) | Want | Vulnerability | Internal tension | Public face → Private reality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Galactic Empire** (LOGH) | Reinhard: destroy the corrupt Goldenbaum dynasty and rule | Depends on one exceptional man; aristocracy is rotten below him | Reformist Reinhard vs. entrenched high nobles (whom he "culls") | Projects imperial legitimacy → is a meritocratic coup against its own aristocracy |
| **Free Planets Alliance** (LOGH) | Preserve democracy; win the war | Corrupt leadership; a military that can coup it | Yang (principled) vs. careerist High Council + Junta plotters | Projects democratic virtue → manufactures a war for a domestic election |
| **Britannia** (Code Geass) | Total imperial dominance, social-Darwinist order | The Geass; its own royal succession intrigue | Charles's hidden metaphysical project vs. the war machine | Projects invincible empire → run by a family's private cosmology |
| **The Black Knights** (Code Geass) | Stated: liberate Japan. Actual (Lelouch): revenge + protect Nunnally | The gap between the two wants | Zero's private agenda vs. the members' national cause | Projects a people's liberation army → is one man's vendetta with a mask |
| **Principality of Zeon** (Gundam) | Spacenoid independence — as founded | Founder's ideology captured by a ruling family | Deikun's democratic Contolism vs. the Zabis' fascism | Projects a war of liberation → is a dynastic dictatorship using the cause |
| **Republic of San Magnolia** (86) | Win a "casualty-free" war; preserve its self-image | The lie (the "drones" are people); moral rot at the core | Reformers like Lena vs. a society comfortable with atrocity | Projects the "inventor of democracy" → practices racialized genocide-by-conscription |
| **Celestial Being** (Gundam 00) | Eradicate all war — by force | The paradox: waging war to end war | Ideologues vs. the human cost of "intervention" | Projects impartial peacemaker → is an unaccountable armed vanguard |

**Reading:** every faction has a *virtue* and a *crime*, and the most powerful entries put the crime inside the public/private gap. The autocrat (Reinhard) is competent and fair; the democracy (the Alliance) is corrupt — the deliberate refusal of a clean pole (author Tanaka: "two imperfect gray political entities... Romance of the Three Kingdoms, not Hollywood").

## Typed-edge relationships, worked

| Edge | Example | Trigger that transforms it |
|---|---|---|
| **Rivalry** (escalating) | Reinhard ↔ Yang (LOGH) — twin strategists, opposite causes | Neither can win militarily; *politics* decides (Vermilion halted by a civilian surrender order) |
| **Dependency (breeds resentment)** | Every faction ↔ **Anaheim Electronics** (Gundam) — the arms-supplier they all need | Anaheim arms *both* sides (gave the Titans a suit built for the AEUG) — dependency weaponized |
| **Secret cooperation** (fragile) | **Fezzan** ↔ both Empire and Alliance (LOGH) | Fezzan secretly balances both to prolong the war for profit; exposure/conquest ends it |
| **Client/Patron → betrayal** | Deikun's Republic → the Zabi family (Zeon) | The patron-family captures the client-movement and inverts its cause |
| **Dormant hostility** | Marley ↔ Eldia (Attack on Titan) | A historical oppressor-and-oppressed cycle; each "victory" just swaps who holds the machine |

**The war-economy neutral** (Fezzan, Anaheim) is a reusable pattern: a faction that **produces the war's means and profits from its continuation**, actively balancing the belligerents so neither wins. It is a third pole with agency, not a bystander.

## Power Vacuums, worked

- **Zeon founder's death (Gundam):** Deikun dies → the Zabi family fills the vacuum → a democratic independence republic becomes a fascist principality. The vacuum was filled by *whoever was fastest, not best* (this skill's Power Vacuum rule, exactly).
- **The Alliance's defeat (LOGH):** military humiliation → radicalization → the National Salvation Junta coup. A democracy's power vacuum is filled from *inside* — the state turning on itself, echoing how Rudolf von Goldenbaum was democratically elected before making himself dictator.

## Cross-Case Lessons (mapped to this skill's Common Mistakes)

1. **Against "monolithic factions" (Mistake #1):** the whole corpus is the counter-example — the Empire has a fair reformer *and* rotten nobles; the democracy has a principled admiral *and* careerist coup-plotters. Give every faction a virtue and a crime; put the crime in the public/private gap.
2. **Against "factions that only oppose the protagonist" (Mistake #4):** these factions have agendas that predate and outlast the hero — Fezzan profiteers whoever fights, the Republic of San Magnolia's atrocity runs on its own logic, Zeon's ideology was captured before the protagonist mattered.
3. **The insurgency's fracture is a principal-agent gap:** rebellions here corrupt not through force but through the divergence between their *stated cause* and their *leaders' actual drivers* (Black Knights, Zeon). Design the gap deliberately — it is the built-in betrayal.
4. **No-clean-side done badly is still possible:** SEED Destiny (characterization collapses) and Valvrave (escalation without payoff) show that distributed culpability fails if the human cost stops being legible. Keep the price on the page.
