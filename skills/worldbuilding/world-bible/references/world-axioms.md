## Trade & Economics

Resources don't matter until they move. A mountain of rare ore is worthless if no one can reach it. Trade routes — not resources themselves — determine where power concentrates.

### What to Define

**Production geography** — what each region produces and why. Production follows from geography and the fantasy system:
<!-- Example format:
| Region | Produces | Because | Needs |
|--------|----------|---------|-------|
| [Region A] | [resource] | [geographic/systemic reason] | [what they can't produce themselves] |
-->

**Trade routes** — how goods move between regions. Each route is a political fact:
<!-- Example format:
### Route: [Name]
**Connects**: [Region A] → [Region B]
**Carries**: [primary goods in each direction]
**Controlled by**: [who taxes, patrols, or monopolizes this route]
**Bottleneck**: [the single point where this route is most vulnerable — a pass, a port, a gate]
**If disrupted**: [what happens to both ends — economic collapse? War? Starvation?]
-->

**Currency and value** — what functions as money and why. Currency must be:
- Scarce enough to hold value but common enough to circulate
- Durable enough to survive transit
- Recognizable enough to prevent counterfeiting
- If the fantasy system creates or transforms matter, how does that affect currency?

**Bottleneck analysis** — where trade routes converge or narrow. These points are where cities grow, where wars are fought, and where the most powerful factions position themselves. A faction that controls a bottleneck doesn't need the largest army — they need the most strategic position.

### Economic Consequences of the Fantasy System

The fantasy system will distort economics. Ask:
- Can it create resources? If so, scarcity is artificial and political, not natural.
- Can it transport goods? If so, traditional trade routes may be obsolete — but only for those with access to the system.
- Does it require a resource to function? If so, that resource becomes the most valuable commodity in the world, regardless of its natural abundance.
- Does it create a class of people who can do things others can't? If so, those people are either commodified, conscripted, or feared — and the economic system will reflect that.
