# Ethical Framework Comparison

Quick-reference for the dilemma-analyzer. Each framework answers the question "What makes an action right?" differently.

## Framework Summary

| Framework | Core Question | Right Action Is | Key Thinkers | Strongest For |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Consequentialism** | What outcomes result? | The one producing the best overall consequences | Bentham, Mill, Singer | Policy analysis, resource allocation, triage |
| **Deontology** | Does it respect duties and rights? | The one consistent with moral duties regardless of outcomes | Kant, Scanlon, Korsgaard | Rights protection, institutional design, personal integrity |
| **Virtue Ethics** | What would a virtuous person do? | The one expressing good character in context | Aristotle, MacIntyre, Foot | Character development, professional ethics, ongoing relationships |
| **Care Ethics** | How does it affect relationships? | The one preserving and strengthening caring relationships | Gilligan, Noddings, Held | Healthcare, education, family, interpersonal decisions |
| **Contractualism** | Could it be justified to all? | The one no one could reasonably reject | Rawls, Scanlon | Justice, institutional design, policy with diverse stakeholders |

## When Frameworks Typically Diverge

| Scenario | Consequentialist Says | Deontologist Says | Virtue Ethicist Says |
|----------|----------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Lie to prevent harm | Lie (better outcome) | Don't lie (duty of honesty) | Depends on what wisdom dictates in context |
| Sacrifice one to save five | Yes (net lives saved) | No (can't use person as mere means) | Character of the agent matters |
| Break a promise for greater good | Break it (outcomes matter) | Keep it (duty to keep promises) | What would a trustworthy person do? |
| Redistribute from rich to poor | Yes if utility increases | Depends on rights (property rights vs. welfare rights) | Justice as a virtue demands concern for others |

## Common Misapplications

| Mistake | Reality |
|---------|---------|
| "Utilitarianism = whatever makes the most people happy" | Utility includes all well-being, not just happiness. Rule utilitarianism considers systemic effects. |
| "Kant says never lie" | Kant's universalizability test is more nuanced than simple rules. Korsgaard and other neo-Kantians allow for priority among duties. |
| "Virtue ethics has no principles" | Virtue ethics has robust principles (the virtues themselves) — it just applies them contextually rather than algorithmically. |
| "Care ethics is just about feelings" | Care ethics is a rigorous framework about relational responsibilities, attentiveness, and vulnerability — not sentimentality. |
