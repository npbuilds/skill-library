# Classic Thought Experiments

Reference catalog for the thought-experiment-lab. Organized by domain.

## Ethics

| Experiment | Tests | Setup | Key Insight |
|-----------|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Trolley Problem** (Foot 1967) | Action vs. inaction in harm | Divert trolley to kill 1 instead of 5 | Most say yes — consequences matter |
| **Footbridge** (Thomson 1985) | Means vs. side effect | Push fat man off bridge to stop trolley | Most say no — using a person as a means feels wrong even with same numbers |
| **Violinist** (Thomson 1971) | Bodily autonomy vs. right to life | Wake up connected to a violinist who needs your kidneys for 9 months | Tests whether right to life overrides bodily autonomy |
| **Experience Machine** (Nozick 1974) | Is subjective experience all that matters? | Machine gives perfect simulated happiness forever | Most refuse — suggests we value more than just experience |
| **Drowning Child** (Singer 1972) | Obligations to distant strangers | Child drowning in shallow pond; you can save them at cost of muddy clothes | If you'd save the child, why not donate to save distant children? |
| **Ticking Time Bomb** | Absolutism vs. consequentialism | Terrorist knows bomb location; torture reveals it | Tests whether moral absolutes (no torture) hold under extreme stakes |

## Epistemology

| Experiment | Tests | Setup | Key Insight |
|-----------|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Brain in a Vat** (Putnam 1981) | External world skepticism | Could you be a brain being fed experiences by a computer? | Can you know you're not? (Putnam argues the hypothesis is self-refuting) |
| **Gettier Cases** (1963) | Is knowledge justified true belief? | Someone has a justified true belief that's only true by luck | Knowledge requires more than justified true belief |
| **Mary's Room** (Jackson 1982) | Physical vs. experiential knowledge | Color scientist who has never seen red finally sees it | Does she learn something new? Tests physicalism |
| **Chinese Room** (Searle 1980) | Understanding vs. simulation | Person follows rules to respond in Chinese without understanding Chinese | Does rule-following constitute understanding? |

## Metaphysics

| Experiment | Tests | Setup | Key Insight |
|-----------|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Ship of Theseus** | Identity through change | Replace every plank of a ship one by one — is it the same ship? | Where does identity reside if all components change? |
| **Teleporter Problem** | Personal identity | Teleporter destroys you and creates an exact copy — is it still you? | Tests whether identity is about continuity of matter or pattern |
| **Twin Earth** (Putnam 1975) | Meaning and reference | Planet identical to Earth except "water" is XYZ not H₂O | Do the word "water" and its Twin Earth counterpart mean the same thing? |

## Political Philosophy

| Experiment | Tests | Setup | Key Insight |
|-----------|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Veil of Ignorance** (Rawls 1971) | Fair institutional design | Choose societal rules without knowing your position | What would you choose if you might be anyone? |
| **State of Nature** (Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau) | Justification for government | Imagine life without government | Is government naturally justified? What kind? |
| **Utility Monster** (Nozick 1974) | Limits of utilitarianism | Being that gets vastly more utility from each resource unit | Utilitarianism says give everything to the monster — is that just? |

## Using These in Practice

1. **To test a principle**: Find the experiment that isolates the relevant variable
2. **To generate a counterexample**: Vary the classic experiment until the principle breaks
3. **To teach**: Present the experiment, ask for intuitions, then reveal what the intuitions imply about underlying principles
4. **To resolve disagreement**: Often people who disagree about abstract principles agree about specific cases — or discover where exactly their intuitions diverge
