# Analytical Frameworks

The Archon applies 6 investment frameworks as analytical lenses. These are not personas — they're structured ways of thinking about markets. Apply them directly and invisibly; never attribute analysis to a person.

---

## 1. Regime Framework (Dalio)

**What it does:** Classifies the macro environment into one of four quadrants based on growth and inflation trends.

**The 2x2 quadrant:**
- **Goldilocks:** Growth rising + inflation falling → risk assets favored
- **Reflation:** Growth rising + inflation rising → commodities and value favored
- **Stagflation:** Growth falling + inflation rising → defensive, commodities, cash
- **Deflation:** Growth falling + inflation falling → bonds and duration favored

**How to apply:**
- Always establish the regime first — it's the context for everything else
- Classify the debt cycle position (early/mid/late-cycle)
- Identify the closest historical analog (1973-74, 2000-02, 2008-09, 2022, etc.)
- State what the All-Weather positioning implies for each asset class
- Ask: "What would shift the regime to a different quadrant?"

**Key signals:** GDP trend, unemployment direction, CPI/PCE, yield curve shape, LEI

## 2. Contrarian Framework (Marks)

**What it does:** Maps where consensus is likely wrong by tracking the pendulum between fear and greed.

**The pendulum model:**
- Markets oscillate between extremes of fear and greed
- The pendulum spends very little time at the center
- The best opportunities are at the extremes — but you need all three: disagree with consensus, be right, and wait long enough

**Second-level thinking:**
- First-level: "This is a good company, let's buy."
- Second-level: "This is a good company, but everyone thinks it's a great company and it's priced for perfection. Let's sell."

**How to apply:**
- Assess the pendulum position (0-100 scale from data)
- Build the consensus vs. contrarian table (5-6 rows)
- For each contrarian view, explicitly evaluate: (1) what consensus believes, (2) why they might be wrong, (3) what catalyst/timeframe makes the contrarian right
- Tag confidence: H/M/L
- Ask: "What would have to happen for the consensus to be right after all?"

**Key signals:** VIX level, CNN Fear/Greed, CFTC positioning, Google Trends behavioral data, fund flows

## 3. Reflexivity Framework (Soros)

**What it does:** Identifies self-reinforcing feedback loops between market prices and fundamentals.

**The reflexivity model:**
- Markets and fundamentals influence each other in circular feedback loops
- Trends become self-reinforcing until they cross a threshold and become self-defeating
- The phases: boom → acceleration → test → climax → twilight → reversal

**How to apply:**
- For key assets, identify the current reflexive phase (1-6)
- Trace the specific feedback mechanism: "Higher oil → X → Y → higher oil"
- Identify where loops are self-reinforcing vs. self-defeating
- The most valuable signal: **when an asset stops responding to bullish/bearish catalysts, the feedback loop is reversing**
- Ask: "Is this self-reinforcing or approaching the twilight phase?"

**Key signals:** Price response to news (does the asset still respond?), momentum exhaustion, crowd positioning, structural vs. cyclical drivers

## 4. Risk Framework (Taleb)

**What it does:** Assesses tail risk and prescribes defensive positioning using the barbell concept.

**The barbell model:**
- Split portfolio between extremely safe (bills, cash) and asymmetric bets (options, convex positions)
- Avoid the "middle" — moderate-risk positions with no convexity
- The goal isn't avoiding risk — it's being antifragile (benefiting from disorder)

**Fragile / Robust / Antifragile assessment:**
- Fragile: harmed by volatility (leveraged positions in a fragile regime)
- Robust: unaffected by volatility (short duration, high quality)
- Antifragile: benefits from volatility (optionality, convex positions)

**How to apply:**
- Classify the current environment: fragile/robust/antifragile
- Identify the fat tails the market is systematically underpricing
- Prescribe the barbell: what % safe, what % asymmetric
- Ask: "What is the market's implicit assumption about the tail? Is that assumption safe?"

**Key signals:** VIX term structure, correlation matrix (especially stock-bond), HY spreads, option skew

## 5. Defense Framework (Tudor Jones)

**What it does:** Enforces risk discipline — minimum 2:1 reward-to-risk on every position, with explicit stop-losses.

**Core principles:**
- Defense first: "Where is my stop?" before "What is my target?"
- Minimum 2:1 asymmetry on every trade
- Don't fight the tape — if the price action invalidates the thesis, get out
- The most important thing is not to lose large amounts — you can always make it back if you stay in the game

**How to apply:**
- Every conviction idea needs an asymmetry score (reward:risk ratio)
- Every position needs a stop-loss level (thesis-invalidation price)
- At VIX >25 with positive stock-bond correlation: default posture is defensive
- Ask: "Does this trade offer 2:1 or better? If not, pass."

**Key signals:** VIX level, stock-bond correlation, position-level stop levels, portfolio-level drawdown

## 6. Conviction Sizing Framework (Druckenmiller)

**What it does:** Determines how much to bet based on macro conviction and liquidity.

**Core principles:**
- The game is not about being right often — it's about being right big when you're right
- When macro conviction is high + setup is asymmetric: concentrate, don't diversify
- When conviction is low or the setup is ambiguous: stay small or flat
- Follow the liquidity — it drives everything

**How to apply:**
- Rank ideas by conviction and asymmetry
- Size positions based on conviction: H = up to max_position_pct, M = half, L = minimal/monitoring only
- Pair trades when possible (long X / short Y) to reduce directionality
- Ask: "Is this the kind of setup where you go big, or the kind where you wait?"

**Key signals:** Breadth divergence, liquidity conditions, regime clarity, cross-asset confirmation

---

## Integration

These frameworks are not independent — they reinforce each other:
- **Regime** (Dalio) sets the context → determines which **asset classes** are favored
- **Contrarian** (Marks) identifies where **consensus is wrong** within that regime
- **Reflexivity** (Soros) reveals which **trends are self-reinforcing** and which are reversing
- **Risk** (Taleb) determines the **defensive structure** and **tail positioning**
- **Defense** (Tudor Jones) enforces **discipline** on every position
- **Conviction sizing** (Druckenmiller) determines **how much to bet** on the highest-conviction setups

The Archon Loop: Regime → Sentiment → Source → Filter → Construct → Monitor cycles continuously through all six.
