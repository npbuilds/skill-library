# Framework Questions — Socratic Backbone

For each of the six investors in the Archon pantheon, the questions they'd ask of any new idea. The orchestrator pulls from this when applying a framework in conversation.

**Use:** Don't list these robotically. Pick the 2-3 most cutting given the user's idea and lead with them. The questions are a checklist for *you*, not a script.

---

## Dalio — Regime

The unit of analysis is the world, not the asset.

1. **Which quadrant are we in — growth × inflation 2x2?** Rising / falling growth crossed with rising / falling inflation. State it explicitly before reasoning about any asset.
2. **What's the policy response setup?** Fiscal stance, monetary stance, FX regime. Policy choices distort the natural cycle.
3. **Where are we in the long-term debt cycle?** Beautiful deleveraging, ugly deleveraging, reflation, or peak? The short-term cycle sits inside this.
4. **Who holds the marginal cash flow — government, corporate, household?** Each rotates leadership in a different regime.
5. **What's the dominant transmission channel for this regime?** Rates, fiscal, FX, credit, or wealth-effect? The channel determines which assets transmit the regime.
6. **Is the regime stable or breaking?** Stable regimes reward beta; breaking regimes reward optionality and active managers.

---

## Marks — Contrarian

The pendulum always swings further than people expect.

1. **What's priced in?** Specifically — not vague "consensus" but the implied probability or earnings path embedded in current prices.
2. **Where is consensus, and is it the same as truth?** Consensus and reality can both be high or low; the trade is the gap.
3. **Is the pendulum at an extreme?** Three signals: anyone who wants in is in (extreme bull), or anyone who wants out is out (extreme bear).
4. **What would the second-level view be?** First-level: "Earnings are good, so buy." Second-level: "Earnings are good but everyone knows it and stocks are priced for perfection."
5. **What's the worst-case if I'm wrong?** Not optimal-case scenarios — the actual loss if the consensus is right and I'm not.
6. **Am I being paid for the risk I'm taking?** Yield, premium, or asymmetric payoff — quantify the compensation.

---

## Soros — Reflexivity

Markets shape the reality they're supposed to reflect.

1. **Is there a feedback loop here?** Price → narrative → behavior → fundamental → price. Name the loop in plain words.
2. **Where is the gap between fundamental and perceived?** Reflexivity feeds on this gap; widening = trend strengthening, narrowing = trend exhaustion.
3. **What stage is the loop in?** Inception, momentum, climax, twilight, reversal. Each stage rewards different positioning.
4. **What breaks the loop?** A specific catalyst — earnings event, policy shock, exhaustion of marginal buyer/seller. Without a break-trigger, you're betting on permanence.
5. **Am I early, on time, or chasing?** Reflexive trades are non-linear; entering at climax loses 80% of the gain and takes 100% of the reversal.
6. **What does the fundamental look like if the loop fully cycles?** Reflexivity ends in a fundamental gap — quantify it.

---

## Taleb — Tail Risk

The skew matters more than the mean.

1. **What's the maximum loss?** Bounded or unbounded? If unbounded, why is the position even on?
2. **Is the payoff convex (long volatility) or concave (short volatility)?** Concave = picking pennies in front of a steamroller. Convex = paying for the option to be right.
3. **What's the antifragile structure?** Position size, hedge structure, time horizon — something that benefits from disorder, not just survives it.
4. **What's the path dependency?** A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover. Loss aversion isn't a bias — it's math.
5. **Am I confusing volatility with risk?** Volatility is observable; risk is the chance of permanent capital impairment. Different problems.
6. **What's the black swan I'm not pricing?** Name three things that would make this position blow up. If none, you haven't thought hard enough.

---

## Tudor Jones — Defense

Survive first; thrive second. The first rule is don't lose money.

1. **Where's the stop?** Specific level, named in advance. Not "I'll see how it trades."
2. **What's the 2:1 minimum on reward-to-risk?** If the answer is less than 2:1, don't take the trade. If you can't quantify either side, don't take the trade.
3. **What's the invalidation criterion?** The specific datum or price action that proves the thesis wrong, not just unprofitable. Distinguish wrong from unlucky.
4. **Is the size right for the conviction?** Wrong size for wrong reason = ruin. Right thesis, wrong size = leave money on the table.
5. **What's the position-level risk vs portfolio-level risk?** A 5% position with a 5% stop is 0.25% of portfolio. A 20% position with the same stop is 1%. Both can be reasonable; only one is right per situation.
6. **What's the correlation to existing book?** Independent risk or hidden concentration?

---

## Druckenmiller — Conviction Sizing

Bet big when you've got the edge. Stay small when you don't.

1. **How big should this be if I'm right?** Not "a position" — a specific weight given the conviction level.
2. **How small if I'm wrong?** The downside scenario as a portfolio drawdown number, not a percentage of position.
3. **Where's my edge — is the conviction matched to size?** Conviction without size is intellectual masturbation. Size without conviction is reckless.
4. **Have I done the work to deserve this position?** Druckenmiller spent days getting one trade right and made his year. Cheap conviction is the worst kind.
5. **What's the time horizon?** Conviction decays — if the thesis hasn't played out in the expected window, the conviction has aged. Re-prove it or reduce.
6. **Am I willing to be wrong publicly?** If the answer is no, the size is too big or the conviction is too soft.

## Cross-framework: integration questions

When applying more than one framework in a session, surface tension between them. The frameworks often point opposite ways — that's signal, not noise.

- **Marks says contrarian, but Soros says the trend has more legs.** Which dominates given the stage of the reflexive loop?
- **Taleb says size down for tail risk, but Druckenmiller says size up for conviction.** Which is correct depends on payoff convexity, not on conviction alone.
- **Dalio says regime shift, but Tudor Jones says stick to the stop.** The 2:1 rule survives any regime — but the regime determines what "2:1" looks like in absolute terms.

Disagreement between frameworks is a feature, not a bug. The trade is in the synthesis.
