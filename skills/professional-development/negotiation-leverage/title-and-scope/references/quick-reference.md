# Title And Scope — Quick Reference


## Quick Reference

| Element | What It Means | Affects |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Your level / signal to the outside world | Future market position, recruiter rankings, external credibility |
| **Scope** | What you actually own — programs, headcount, budget, charter | Daily work, internal influence, learning rate |
| **Reporting** | Who you report to | Day-to-day relationship, board exposure, decision authority |

## When Title Matters More

| Scenario | Why |
|---|---|
| **Next-role anticipation** | The next title-step matters; "Director" → "VP" is a step-up move |
| **External signaling** | Industry-facing role where title appears in press, board bios, etc. |
| **Recruiter ranking** | LinkedIn searches and recruiter pipelines weight by title |
| **Comp band ceiling** | Some companies cap comp by title; level matters for future raises |

## When Scope Matters More

| Scenario | Why |
|---|---|
| **Building credibility** | Owning a P&L or large team is what makes the next role possible |
| **Skill compounding** | Broader scope = more learning across functions |
| **Organizational influence** | Scope determines how much you matter inside the company |
| **Boardroom presence** | Heads-of-function with broad scope tend to present to boards |

## Negotiable Elements of Scope

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Programs / portfolio** | Which programs you own |
| **Headcount** | How many direct and indirect reports |
| **Budget** | What you control / approve |
| **Charter** | What kinds of decisions are yours alone |
| **External rep** | Whether you represent the company externally (conferences, investor calls) |
| **Board access** | Whether you regularly present to or attend board meetings |
| **Cross-functional authority** | Decisions you can make that affect other functions |

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Looks Like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Accept title without scrutiny | Take whatever title is offered | Audit against market for similar scope |
| Negotiate title, ignore scope | Win the title, lose the actual role | Negotiate both |
| Accept inflated title | Take the "VP" without the underlying scope | Be honest about what hurts you next |
| Skip reporting-line negotiation | Accept whoever you're assigned to report to | Reporting is often the most-leverage lever |
| No written charter | Verbal scope agreement | Get the scope in writing in the offer letter |
