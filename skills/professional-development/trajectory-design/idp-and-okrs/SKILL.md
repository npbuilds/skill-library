# IDP and OKRs — Making the Trajectory Measurable

A trajectory without measurement drifts. The work feels intentional in the moment but a year later the progress is unclear; the goals were vague enough that any outcome can be rationalized as success. Personal OKRs and IDPs solve this: the goals are specific, the time horizon is bounded, the metrics are honest, and the quarterly review tells you whether you're tracking. The frame transfers directly from organizational OKR practice — only the unit of work is the user.

## Key Concepts

### OKR Structure for Personal Use

| Element | Definition | Example (for MD → biotech VC transition) |
|---|---|---|
| **Objective** | Qualitative, ambitious, time-bound | "By Q4, be a credible candidate for biotech VC associate roles" |
| **Key Results** | 3–5 specific, measurable outcomes | "Publish 2 thesis essays; complete 15 firm-research deep-dives; hold 30 informational conversations; receive 3 first-conversations with target firms" |

The objective is the destination; the key results are how you'll know you got there. Not "did stuff" but "achieved measurable outcomes."

### Personal vs Organizational OKRs

Personal OKRs differ from organizational OKRs in important ways:

| Dimension | Organizational | Personal |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | Quarterly typical | Quarterly or semi-annual |
| Number | 3–5 objectives per quarter | 1–3; more becomes noise |
| Public/private | Often public for alignment | Private; share selectively with mentor / advisor |
| Scoring | 0.0–1.0; 0.7 is "good" | Same scoring works |
| Stretch | Usually built in | Be honest about stretch vs base |
| Connection to incentives | Tied to comp / promotion | Tied to trajectory progress, not incentives |

### IDP Structure

An IDP (Individual Development Plan) is the longer-horizon companion to OKRs. Where OKRs are quarterly tactical, the IDP is annual or 2-year strategic:

| IDP Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Current state** | Honest snapshot — role, scope, skills, network |
| **Target state** | Where you want to be in 12–24 months — role archetype, capabilities, position |
| **Skill gaps** | What you'll need that you don't have (cross-load with `skill-gap-analysis`) |
| **Growth plan** | How you'll close the gaps — projects, courses, mentors, exposure |
| **Milestones** | Quarterly checkpoints |
| **Risk and dependencies** | What could derail; what's outside your control |
| **Review cadence** | When you'll re-evaluate (typically quarterly) |

### Exploration vs Output OKRs

Senior professionals in transition often need *exploration* OKRs rather than *output* OKRs. The distinction:

| Mode | What It Measures | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Output OKR** | Things produced (artifacts, conversations, results) | When the trajectory is committed and the work is execution |
| **Exploration OKR** | Learning velocity (archetypes assessed, hypotheses tested, conversations had) | When the trajectory is genuinely uncertain |

Forcing output OKRs onto exploration produces theater (artifacts that don't compound) and burnout. Exploration OKRs are an underused frame that fits transition periods well.

### Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Looks Like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Vague objectives | "Get better at investing" | Specific, time-bound, falsifiable |
| Unmeasurable key results | "Build network" | "Hold 30 conversations with target-archetype people" |
| Too many objectives | 5+ objectives per quarter | 1–3 maximum |
| No mid-quarter check | Set in January, check in December | Monthly check-in; quarterly recalibration |
| Output OKRs in exploration phase | Forcing artifacts when learning is the work | Exploration OKR mode |
| Quiet abandonment | OKRs disappear after 2 weeks | Calendar the review; make it small enough to sustain |

### The Quarterly Review

Each quarter, the review:

1. **Score each KR** (0.0–1.0). Be honest. 0.7 is good for stretch goals; 1.0 means you sandbagged.
2. **Score each Objective** (composite of its KRs).
3. **Diagnose patterns** — Where did you over-estimate? Where did you under-estimate? What surprised you?
4. **Update IDP** — Has your understanding of target state shifted? Skill gaps changed?
5. **Set next-quarter OKRs** — Carry over what's still relevant; add new based on diagnosis.

The review is the discipline that makes the OKR practice work. Without review, OKRs become aspirational decoration.

## Self-Coaching Track

**For your situation (MD → biotech VC/operator):**

1. **Build your annual IDP.** Current state, 12-month target state, skill gaps, growth plan. Spend 2–3 hours; this is the strategic anchor for the year.

2. **Set this quarter's OKRs.** 1–3 objectives, each with 3–5 key results. Given the transition, lean toward exploration OKRs for the first 1–2 quarters, then shift to output OKRs as the path becomes committed.

3. **Calendar the monthly check-in and quarterly review.** 15 minutes monthly; 60 minutes quarterly. Without calendar discipline, the practice dies.

4. **Pick one accountability partner.** A mentor or advisor (cross-load with `feedback-loops/advisor-board`) who you share the OKRs with. Light external accountability dramatically improves follow-through.

5. **Year-end review.** Honest scoring across the year. Patterns diagnosis. Update IDP. Re-set for next year.

6. **Coach yourself against perfectionism.** OKRs that look polished but never get reviewed are useless. OKRs that are messy but honestly tracked compound. Choose messy and honest.

## Teach / Mentor-Others Track

**When coaching a junior or peer through OKR/IDP design:**

1. **Distinguish exploration from output OKRs.** This reframe alone changes how mentees approach transition periods. Show them the table.

2. **Cap the objective count.** Mentees often want 5–7 objectives. Coach the cap of 3. The constraint forces priority clarity.

3. **The KR test: would a stranger know if you achieved it?** Many KRs are vague enough that any outcome counts as success. Coach the falsifiability test.

4. **Calendar the review or it doesn't happen.** Mentees often skip this. Walk them through scheduling the review windows during the OKR-setting session itself.

5. **Honest scoring is hard.** Mentees often inflate scores to feel good. Coach the 0.7-is-good calibration; reframe scoring as diagnostic, not as performance.

6. **Sharing with an accountability partner is uncomfortable but high-leverage.** Many mentees keep OKRs entirely private. Coach selective sharing with one mentor; the external eye improves quality.

## When This Applies

- Annual planning
- Quarterly review and reset
- After a 360 or advisor conversation that suggests trajectory shift
- Onboarding to a new role (first-90-days OKRs)
- When trajectory feels active but unmeasured
