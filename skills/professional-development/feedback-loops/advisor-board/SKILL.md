# Advisor Board — The Small Trusted Circle That Compounds Judgment

Most senior professionals have a vague "people I bounce things off." The strong version is structured: a small explicit set of advisors, each chosen for a specific perspective, with deliberate cadence and a relationship designed for hard-truth-telling. The personal advisory board is the difference between drifting through a career with periodic generic advice and steering through a career with high-trust feedback from people who have seen the patterns you're navigating.

## Key Concepts

### What a Personal Advisory Board Is (and Isn't)

It IS:
- 4–7 specific people, named, with defined relationships to you
- A structured cadence (quarterly is typical)
- A set of perspectives covering the dimensions you need (sector, stage, function, role-archetype, peer)
- Reciprocal — you serve them too

It is NOT:
- A formal board with legal structure (that's an advisory board for a company)
- Compensation-driven (personal advisors are usually unpaid)
- A general "mentor network" (the structure and intentionality are tighter)
- Always made up of senior people only (peer advisors are valuable too)

### Composition

A useful personal advisory board has perspective diversity:

| Perspective | What They Provide |
|---|---|
| **Domain-deep senior** | Pattern recognition in your specific field (clinical development, biotech) |
| **Adjacent senior** | Different lens (operator, investor, scientist, lawyer) |
| **One-step-ahead** | Someone who's just made the move you're considering |
| **Cross-industry / outsider** | Different vocabulary; surfaces what insiders miss |
| **Peer / accountability** | Someone at your level who can be brutally honest |
| **Cultural / values** | Someone who knows you well enough to flag values drift |
| **Network connector** | Hub who can introduce you across the ecosystem |

You don't need all of these; you need the diversity to cover blind spots.

### Designing the Cadence

| Cadence | Format | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Quarterly 45–60 min** | Phone, video, in-person | Most advisors |
| **Monthly during high-intensity periods** | Phone | Pre-transition, during transition |
| **Annual deep-dive** | Half-day, in-person | Strategic-level advisors |
| **Ad-hoc on triggers** | Email or short call | Mature relationships |

The advisor sets the cadence they're comfortable with; you propose, they confirm.

### What to Bring to Each Meeting

A useful advisor meeting has:

1. **Agenda 24+ hours in advance** — 3–5 specific things you want to discuss
2. **Context document** — A short brief on the current situation (1 page max)
3. **Specific questions** — Not "what should I do?" but "should I take this offer; here's the analysis I've done"
4. **Open ear** — Be ready to hear things you don't want to hear

Without an agenda, the meeting drifts into generic catch-up; with an agenda, it produces specific decisions.

### The Hard-Truth Dynamic

The advisor relationship is most valuable when the advisor will tell you things others won't. Cultivating that:

1. **Explicitly ask for it.** "I want your honest read; I'd rather hear it from you now than discover it later."
2. **Respond well when it happens.** Don't get defensive; thank them; act on it.
3. **Demonstrate that you act.** When they advise something, do something specific with it and report back.
4. **Don't ask for permission.** Advisors are advisors, not authorizers. The decision is yours.

### Reciprocity

Personal advisors are unpaid; the relationship is sustained by reciprocity:

- **Be a good "reverse advisor"** for them when appropriate
- **Introduce them to people they'd value meeting**
- **Send them useful content** they wouldn't otherwise see
- **Update them on outcomes** — not just when you need them
- **Honor their time** — short, prepared, specific

### Refreshing the Composition

Once a year, audit the composition:

- Has your trajectory shifted? Different advisors may be needed.
- Has an advisor's relevance faded? Honor the past; move them to "active network" cadence.
- Are there blind spots not covered? Add an advisor.
- Is the diversity what it should be?

The board is not permanent. People rotate. Honor the changes.

### Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Looks Like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Diffuse "mentor network" | Many vague relationships | Pick 4–7 explicit advisors with named perspectives |
| Too senior | Only "people 3 levels above me" | Include peers and cross-perspective advisors |
| No cadence | Reach out only when in crisis | Quarterly cadence at minimum |
| No agenda | Drift conversations | 24h-advance agenda |
| Asking for permission | "Should I take this?" | Bring the analysis; ask for sharpening |
| No reciprocity | Take-take dynamic | Active reciprocity |
| Permanence | Same advisors for 10 years through life changes | Annual composition refresh |

## Self-Coaching Track

**For your situation (MD → biotech VC/operator):**

1. **Map perspective gaps.** For your current trajectory, which perspectives matter? Biotech VC partner perspective; operator-who-pivoted perspective; scientific-peer perspective; spouse/values perspective. Be specific.

2. **List candidates per perspective.** For each, list 2–3 candidates. People who'd give you honest feedback and whose perspective you respect.

3. **Make the explicit ask** (cross-load with `mentorship-design`). Don't ask them to "be on your advisory board"; ask for a quarterly conversation about your trajectory.

4. **Set the cadence.** Quarterly 45–60 min by default. Calendar it.

5. **Build the agenda discipline.** Before every advisor meeting, draft the 3–5 questions you want to discuss. Send a context doc if helpful.

6. **Practice the hard-truth ask.** "I want your honest read on this." Most advisors will appreciate the explicit permission.

7. **Annual composition refresh.** Every year, audit who's on the board, who's added, who's transitioning to lower-cadence acquaintance.

## Teach / Mentor-Others Track

**When coaching a junior or peer through advisor board construction:**

1. **Diffuse-mentor-network reframe.** Many mentees have a vague mentor set without structure. Walk through the diversified-perspective composition.

2. **The peer advisor is undervalued.** Many mentees only think senior. Reframe: peers and one-step-ahead advisors are often the most useful.

3. **Coach the explicit ask.** Mentees often want advisor relationships but don't ask explicitly. The ask is what creates the structure.

4. **Agenda discipline transfers fast.** Coach the 3–5 questions, sent in advance, every time. The meeting quality improves immediately.

5. **The hard-truth ask is the unlock.** Many mentees never explicitly ask for hard truths. Coach the language; rehearse the responding-well part.

6. **Reciprocity isn't optional.** Coach the explicit reciprocity practices. Without them, the relationships dry up.

## When This Applies

- Building the personal advisory board for the first time
- Annual composition refresh
- Approaching a major transition (advisor set may need adjustment)
- After feedback that suggests current advisors aren't serving the trajectory
- Coaching others through their advisor-board construction
